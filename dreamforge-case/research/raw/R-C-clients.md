## Conflicts
- C-RC1: Sources disagree on the EV100 India member count. The Climate Group's own India page (S163) states 14 India-headquartered businesses are committed; a 2024 Climate Group press release (S164) says more than 20 global and Indian businesses have committed to electrifying fleets in India by 2030. Ruling: use 14 as the India-headquartered member count (the owner-published, narrower figure) and note the 20+ figure separately as inclusive of multinational companies operating in India (for example Wipro and SBI's global commitments versus India-only membership counts differ by definition). Basis: S163 is the Climate Group's own country page and is the more specific, narrower-scoped statement; S164 is a press release that does not define its denominator as precisely. (also appended to raw/conflicts.md)
- C-RC2: Sources disagree on whether BRSR Core value-chain disclosure and assurance are mandatory or voluntary for the top 1,000 listed companies from FY2026-27. A vendor blog, Benchmark Gensuite (S189), frames reasonable assurance on BRSR Core as mandatory for all top 1,000 companies. Three independent advisory-firm sources (KPMG S184, Uniqus S187, Corporate Professionals S188) describe a 28 Mar 2025 SEBI circular that made value-chain ESG disclosure and its assessment or assurance voluntary (not mandatory) for the top 250 entities, with the assurance timeline running to FY2026-27 on a voluntary basis. Ruling: treat value-chain disclosure and assurance as voluntary under the revised (2025) framework, since three independent advisory sources agree and name a specific circular date, against one undated vendor blog that does not distinguish BRSR Core itself from its value-chain extension. BRSR Core's own reasonable-assurance requirement (not the value-chain layer) remains the one that phases in mandatorily to the top 1,000 by FY2026-27. Basis: majority and specificity of independent advisory-firm sources over a single vendor blog. (also appended to raw/conflicts.md)
- C-RC3: Delhivery's receivable-days figure differs by source and method. Screener.in's dedicated "debtor days" metric (S190) gives 64 days (FY24) and 58 days (FY25). An independent calculation from StockAnalysis.com's receivables and revenue figures (S191, see RC63) gives approximately 67 and 61 days on trade receivables alone. Ruling: use Screener's debtor-days metric (64, 58) as the primary figure for assumption A01, since it is a purpose-built financial-ratio product using a consistent definition; the StockAnalysis-derived figures are reported as a directional cross-check only, with the gap attributed to differing receivable and revenue denominators (trade receivables only versus total receivables, and standalone versus consolidated revenue bases) between the two data products. Basis: Screener's metric is explicitly labelled to match the needed definition (debtor days); the StockAnalysis figure requires an extra derivation step with unstated denominator assumptions. (also appended to raw/conflicts.md)

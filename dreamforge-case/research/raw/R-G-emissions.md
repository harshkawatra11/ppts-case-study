A secondary source (S477) reports CEA Version 22.0 (dated 1 August 2026) gives 0.675 tCO2/MWh for FY 2025,26, two versions and two fiscal years newer, and a separate secondary source (S478, now returning HTTP 410 on reopen) earlier reported Version 21.0 (FY 2024,25) at either about 0.710 or about 0.7117 tCO2/MWh depending on which of two sub-sources was read. Ruling: the primary-verified 0.727 (Version 20.0, FY 2023,24) is reported as the VERIFIED figure in this file, since it is the newest version this environment could access and read directly; the newer, lower figures are reported as UNVERIFIABLE or SNIPPET, flagged prominently, and noted as both internally plausible (CEA's own grid factor should fall as renewable capacity grows) and directionally favourable to the model's recommendation if true (also appended to research/raw/conflicts.md).
- C-G2: Whether the IPCC 2006 Guidelines alone can produce a single defensible kg CO2 per litre figure for motor gasoline. The case materials' planning-time lead (L11) and the model's assumption A21 treat 2.31 kg CO2 per litre as "the IPCC 2006 default". This lane's primary reading of IPCC Volume 2 Chapters 1 and 3 (S470, S471) found that IPCC publishes only mass- and energy-based factors (44.3 TJ/Gg NCV, 69,300 kg CO2/TJ effective factor) and no density figure at all; a litre-based figure of 2.31 is only reachable by also assuming a density of about 0.752 kg per litre, which is a defensible but undisclosed external assumption, not an IPCC default. Ruling: 2.31 is retained as within the honestly-derived uncertainty band (2.21 to 2.43, see RG68), but it should be described in the research document and the model's own comments as "IPCC energy factor combined with an assumed India-typical petrol density of about 0.75 kg per litre (IS 2796 range 0.72 to 0.775)", not as a bare "IPCC default" (also appended to research/raw/conflicts.md).

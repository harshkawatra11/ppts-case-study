### D.2 The cohort-weighted rounding gap

Exhibit 2a's own cohort table reports a total active (trailing 12 months) count of 1,20,372 when its six segment figures are summed directly, while Exhibit 1 reports the case's own true active count as 1,19,339 [C-E1, C-E2a]. The gap, 1,033 accounts, is not a data error in either exhibit; it is a mechanical consequence of the case's own rounding instruction, which states every percentage to the nearest whole point [C-N1]. A segment as large as the Salary Account cohort, 6,27,853 accounts, turns a rounding step of half a percentage point into a swing of over 3,000 accounts once that rounded percentage is multiplied back out against the segment's full size, and the six segments' rounding errors do not cancel evenly. This document resolves the gap by using Exhibit 1's unrounded 1,19,339 as the true active count everywhere a single figure is needed, and by using Exhibit 2a's percentages only for within-cohort, year-on-year comparisons, where the same rounding step applies to both years and so affects the comparison far less than it affects a single-year total. Section 3.9 of this document's own research text, in parts A to E, works through this arithmetic in full, summing Exhibit 2a's six segment figures step by step to show exactly where the one-account gap against the exhibit's own stated total and the 1,033-account gap against Exhibit 1 each arise.

### D.3 The inactive-and-dormant translation, restated for a reader who has not read section 0

The case defines two account states that read as near-synonyms but mean different things. Inactive, in the case's own words, is an account deactivated due to fraudulent activity or a regulator's notice [C-D3]. Dormant is an account that has not traded even once in the last 24 months [C-D4]. The exchange's own governing circular on the 24-month condition, NSE/INSP/64718 of 25 October 2024, uses the word "inactive" in its own text for exactly the condition the case calls dormant, which means a verified research row quoting that circular verbatim would, if copied without translation, use the case's "inactive" word for the case's "dormant" meaning. This document translates every such instance: wherever a source uses the exchange's "inactive" for the 24-month no-trade condition, the text instead reads "dormant [C-D4]," and the word "inactive" is reserved throughout for the case's own fraud-and-regulator-notice meaning. This translation rule applies most visibly in the reactivation flow (the holdings-check lever's dormant-account path) and in the warm-cohort defence lever's own ethical design, both of which depend on the 24-month condition specifically.

### D.4 Model limits

Table D.2 restates the model's own limits directly, since a reader weighing confidence in any single result in this document should hold these four limits in mind regardless of which lever or scenario the result concerns.

| Limit | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| A reach-times-response structure, not a measured funnel | Every tap-through rate, order-after-tap rate and coverage multiplier in the model is a team estimate checked against the nearest available benchmark, a vendor figure, a published study or a comparator's own disclosure, never a measurement taken from ONL's own accounts. Every sentence in this document reporting a model result says "in our model" for this reason |
| Constant response rates within a scenario | The model treats each lever's response rate as unchanging across all thirteen weeks of the programme, when a real rollout would likely see some decay as the easiest-to-convert accounts respond first; the weekly path in T12 is a planning curve built on this simplification, not a guaranteed trajectory |
| Independence assumed between overlapping levers | The overlap calculation that combines levers touching the same cohort treats each lever's conversion probability as independent of the others, conditional on reach; this is reasonable where the probabilities are small, as they are for most cohort-lever pairs, but is a known simplification for the Elite, Elevate and Prestige cohorts, where the RM review probability runs close to 9% to 12% and a real behavioural correlation between responding to one lever and another is not separately modelled |
| A uniform dormancy-friction factor | The model applies the same dormancy-adjustment factor, 0.75 in the base case, to every cohort's idle accounts equally, when re-KYC friction plausibly differs by cohort; a Prestige client with an active bank relationship likely completes re-verification faster than a dormant Credit Card account holder, and the model does not separately estimate that difference |

A small number of individual assumptions also remain team estimates with no comparable benchmark found in this research, rather than figures checked against a sourced range. The clearest example is A53, the email per-message cost used in the cash-cost build, where no vendor or official source gave a directly comparable Indian per-email transactional cost figure; this document states that plainly in section 24.3 rather than attach a borrowed source to a number this research could not independently verify. None of these team-estimate-only inputs is among the thirteen assumptions the tornado ranking in section 23 identifies as moving the base case's result by a wide margin, which means the limits in this appendix affect how much confidence a reader should place in specific cost or timing details, not the headline activation-rate result itself.

### C.2 Case terms that read as similar words with different meanings

A small number of terms in this document are not short forms but full words whose meaning shifts depending on whether the case or an outside regulator is using them. Table C.2 sets these out directly, since a reader who skipped section 0.10 of this document should still be able to find the mapping here.

| Term, as the case defines it | What the case means | What an outside source sometimes means by a similar word | How this document resolves the collision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Active | At least one trade in the last 12 months [C-D1] | An exchange's own "active client" count, which may use a different trailing window or a different trade-count threshold | This document always states "active (trailing 12 months)" or cites the exchange's own window explicitly when the two could be confused |
| Highly Active | At least one trade in the last 3 months [C-D2] | Not a term any outside source in this research uses in the same sense | No collision found; the term is case-specific throughout |
| Inactive | An account deactivated due to fraudulent activity or a regulator's notice [C-D3] | The exchange's own circular, NSE/INSP/64718 of 25 October 2024, uses "inactive" for an account with no trade in 24 months, a different condition entirely | This document reserves "inactive" for the case's own fraud-and-notice meaning and translates every exchange use of "inactive" for the 24-month condition to the case's own word, "dormant," with the tag [C-D4] attached each time |
| Dormant | Has not traded even once in the last 24 months [C-D4] | Rarely used by outside sources at all, which otherwise default to "inactive" for this same condition | This document uses "dormant" consistently for the 24-month no-trade condition, following the case's own word rather than the exchange's |

The inactive-and-dormant collision is the one translation this document applies most often, since the exchange's own rule, the reactivation flow and the warm-cohort lever's own ethical design all turn on the 24-month condition, and every one of those passages uses the case's word, dormant, rather than carry the exchange's "inactive" into a sentence where a reader could mistake it for the case's fraud-and-notice meaning instead.

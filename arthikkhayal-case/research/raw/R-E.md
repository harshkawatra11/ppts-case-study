## Conflicts

1. E03 against E04: the widely cited 3.5% to 13.6% SMarT savings-rate jump applies only to employees who actively joined the plan, not to the average employee at the firm (whose average rose a smaller 4.4% to 10.6%). Both figures come from the same DOL CLEAR review; neither supersedes the other, they describe different populations (joiners vs. everyone), and research-section text must state which population a cited figure describes.
2. E09 and E10 (Madrian and Shea 2001 percentage-point lift and firm description) come from secondary commentary (a blog-style causal-claims summary and legislative testimony), not the primary NBER/QJE abstract text, which gives no numeric participation rates on its own abstract page (E08). Flag [blog] on E09's source and treat the 48pp/11pp and 29,267-employee/86% figures as secondary-sourced, not primary-verified, until the QJE full text is checked directly.
3. E33's loss-aversion coefficient (about 2 to 2.25) is flagged by a 2018 peer-reviewed review (Yechiam) as resting on over-interpreted early evidence; research-section text citing "losses feel about twice as bad as gains" should note this is contested, not settled.
4. E38 and E41 point in different directions on net effect: Karlsson et al. 2009 show attention (logins) rises with market returns and falls after bad news, while the later Columbia summary of Sicherman et al. 2016 (E41) notes ostrich-type investors are also less likely to trade after a downturn, i.e. lower attention here coincides with lower, not higher, trading. This matters for research section 8, which should not claim inattention after a downturn causes extra trading; the evidence says the opposite (reduced attention, reduced trading).
5. E59 against E60/E61: the case lane question names "FCA 2022 on digital engagement," but the quantified experimental effect sizes (push notifications, prize draws, leaderboards raising trading frequency and risk) belong to a later FCA online experiment reported from around 20 June 2024 and formally written up as Occasional Paper 66 in April 2025. The 2022 item itself is a supervisory warning with no published effect sizes. Research-section text citing "FCA 2022" for a specific percentage or demographic effect should be corrected to cite the 2024/2025 study instead, or state both dates and what each one contains.
6. E56 is an early working-paper approximation (about 5% next-month decline, about 9% in extreme herding episodes) for Barber et al.'s published Journal of Finance return-reversal finding; the primary paper's own published figure was not confirmed in this search (E58) because the primary PDF could not be reached. Treat the 5%/9% figures as indicative, not a confirmed published number, until the Journal of Finance text itself is read.

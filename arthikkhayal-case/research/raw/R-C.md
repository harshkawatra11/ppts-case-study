## Conflicts

1. Industry-wide demat account totals for mid-to-late 2026 disagree by source and unit: 234.4 million (23.44 crore) for July 2026 (C09) against a Sahi-reported 23.15 crore for June 2026 and a SEBI-bulletin-derived 21.6 crore for December 2025 (C11). The gap looks larger than one month of growth can explain and may reflect "total" versus "active client" account definitions; not resolved this pass.
2. Retail F&O trader counts for FY26 disagree: the government's Rajya Sabha reply (11 Aug 2026) gives 78.6 lakh individual traders, down 20% from FY25's 98.1 lakh (C64), while a Sahi article describing a SEBI FY26 study gives 87.5 lakh active traders, down 18% year on year (C68). The two counts and their percentage declines do not reconcile; they may use different trader definitions (unique registered versus active in the year). This matters because the research document's section 4 and section 22 (Lane C tests A63 to A65 and the activation diagnosis) cite F&O participation as context; use the Rajya Sabha figure (C64 to C67) as the primary number since it is a government-sourced, dated reply, and footnote the Sahi figure (C68) as a conflicting count.
3. Nifty 50 one-year and 2026-YTD return figures vary by cutoff date and method across the same tracker site: minus 13.12% total return to 2 Oct 2026 (C31) against minus 14.2% YTD to 16 Oct 2026 (C32) and a separately cited minus 16.7% YTD loss with dividends reinvested "as of early October" found in the same search pass but not independently sourced to a stable URL. All three come from the same class of ETF-proxy tracker, not an NSE-published index return; treat all Nifty/Midcap/Smallcap return figures in section 5 as indicative, not official, and flag them [blog] throughout the research document.
4. SIP stoppage ratio figures for prior months disagree on rounding and precise value: C51's source gives August 2026 at 81.05%, while a Bajaj Broking headline (C57) gives an earlier month's figure as 81.9%. These may refer to different months (July versus August 2026); the September 2026 figure itself (78.53%) is consistent across the two Angel One and Hans India sources (C51, C52) and should be treated as the reliable value.
5. HDFC Securities' sell-side depository charge is quoted as "INR 25 Per Scrip" in one comparison table (C73) and as "about ₹20 plus 18% GST, or roughly ₹23.60 in total" in a planning-time search result for the same broker; neither was checked against HDFC Securities' own current tariff page this pass, so the exact current figure is unresolved.

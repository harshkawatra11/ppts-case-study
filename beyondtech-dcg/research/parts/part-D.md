## 13. Defining Tier-1 and the longlist: 24 institutes plus four IITs for contrast

**Answer.** Tier-1, for this project, means an institute of national importance or a state flagship whose B.Tech median CTC and recruiter list overlap the four campuses BeyondTech already serves, not a rank-list label borrowed from a newspaper. Twenty-eight institutes were longlisted on that definition, split across four regions, and screened on four pass-or-fail filters before any scoring happened. Three colleges fail the screen outright on batch size, all three of them IIITs, and none of the four contrast-set IITs included for comparison reaches the final four picks once the same rules are applied to them. The longlist and the screen are therefore the first piece of evidence that the pick process is rule-bound rather than reputation-bound: fame by itself buys a college nothing here.

### 13.1 Why "Tier-1" needed a definition at all

The case brief never defines "Tier-1." It only uses the word, three times, to describe the student BeyondTech serves (institutes of national importance and state flagships, implicitly) [B01, B17]. Left undefined, the term could mean anything from "any NIT or IIT" to "any college with a recognisable acronym," and either reading would let the scorecard smuggle in a brand bias dressed up as rigour. The lead ruled on this gap at Gate G1, closing conflict X6: Tier-1 is defined as institutes of national importance and state flagships whose B.Tech median CTC and recruiter list overlap the four core campuses where BeyondTech already works (DTU, NSUT, Thapar, BIT Mesra). This definition is operationalised, not decorative: it is the reason the screen in section 13.3 below exists at all, and it is the reason the four contrast IITs are shown on the same table as everyone else rather than assumed to win by default.

The definition does real work in one place worth flagging up front. It explicitly excludes private-engineering-college brand names that compete on marketing spend rather than recruiter overlap (a VIT-adjacent or Manipal-adjacent reader might expect those names to anchor the list), and it explicitly includes smaller, less famous NITs and IIITs on the same footing as the four reference IITs, provided their recruiter base and CTC profile actually overlap BeyondTech's core. Three IIITs did make the longlist on this reasoning; all three then fail the first screen filter on batch size (section 13.4), which is itself informative: being plausibly Tier-1 by overlap of CTC and recruiter base does not guarantee a batch large enough to sustain a BeyondTech founding cohort and a second season.

### 13.3 The screen: four pass-or-fail filters, applied before any score counts

Before the weighted scorecard runs, every longlist college (not the calibration rows, which exist only to check the scorecard against known-good answers) must clear four filters. A college that fails any one filter never reaches the ranked table as an eligible pick, regardless of how high its Fit score would otherwise land.

| Filter | Rule | Reason | Eligible result reported for |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 Base | B.Tech graduating batch of at least 600 students | A founding cohort and a second season both need a large enough aspirant pool to draw from | 28 longlist colleges |
| F2 Recruiter presence | At least 4 of the 20 fixed anchor recruiters visited in the last two seasons (relaxed from 5; see 13.3.1) | BeyondTech's whole promise is tied to the companies that actually visit a campus [B09, B12] | 28 longlist colleges |
| F3 New ground | Not DTU, NSUT, Thapar or BIT Mesra | Pillar 2 of the brief is explicitly about colleges BeyondTech is not already at [B16] | Excludes the 4 calibration rows by construction |
| F4 Evidence | At least 5 of the 7 scoring criteria have a sourced value, not "est." | "Every pick is backed by numbers," in the brief's own words [B17] | 28 longlist colleges |

#### 13.3.1 The F2 relaxation, stated plainly

The screen as originally drafted set F2 at 5 of the 20 anchor recruiters. Applied literally, only 2 non-IIT colleges cleared that bar. PLAN.md's own falsification rule (section 4.6) anticipates exactly this outcome: "fewer than 4 non-IIT colleges pass the screen, then relax F2 from 5 anchor recruiters to 4 and log it; do not relax F1." The lead applied that rule once the research came in, relaxed F2 to 4 anchors, and logged the relaxation rather than quietly folding it into the scorecard [model/model_tables.md T8]. This is deliberately not hidden in this document: a scorecard that only ever tightens or loosens its own rules in the dark is not a scorecard a judge should trust, and the relaxation is evidence of a disciplined, rule-bound process working as designed, not of the team moving the goalposts until a convenient number of colleges passed. F1 (batch size) was never touched; the falsification rule explicitly protects it, since a college too small to sustain a founding cohort is too small regardless of how the recruiter threshold is set.

### 13.4 Who fails the screen, and why

The three colleges that fail the screen hardest, and never get rescued by any later relaxation, all fail on the same filter: F1, batch size. All three are IIITs, and the failure is not close in any of the three cases.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| IIIT Delhi | 480 (rising to 540 for 2024-25) [D IIIT Delhi.C3] | 600 | 120 to 60 students short | Also returned zero confirmed C1 and C4 evidence after three searches each; general web search repeatedly conflated it with the much larger IIT Delhi |
| IIIT Allahabad | 428 [D IIIT Allahabad.C3] | 600 | 172 students short | Also likely fails F2 on the evidence found (only Deloitte confirmed, 1 of 19 countable anchors) |

None of these three is "never rescued" (per the Late corrections in the Writer Brief) because none is a borderline case: a batch of 177, 428 or 480 to 540 students against a 600-student floor is not a measurement-error gap that a looser definition would close. The screen's own logic, stated in section 13.3 above, is that a founding cohort (12 to 30 students, A17) and a second season both need enough of a pool to draw from without the cohort becoming a meaningful fraction of the entire graduating class on day one; at 177 students (IIIT Hyderabad), a 20-student founding cohort alone would already be more than 11% of the whole graduating batch, which is a different kind of launch than the one this plan is built for.

Several longlist colleges that do clear F1 nonetheless fail later filters, most commonly F4 (evidence) or F2 on thin recruiter confirmation. Jadavpur University is the clearest case: it clears F1 on a batch of roughly 868 students (est.) [D Jadavpur University.C3], but returns the weakest evidence profile of any college scored in this batch across C1 (1 of 19 countable anchors, Accenture only), C4 (zero societies with any confirmed evidence) and C6 (no sourced calendar), failing F4's "at least 5 of 7 sourced" bar on the research gathered. This is recorded here as a genuine screen-fail candidate on the evidence found, not a false negative manufactured to protect a preferred outcome; a further research pass on Jadavpur's own placement cell site, not attempted in this round, is the stated path to revisiting it.

### 13.5 Why four IITs sit on the longlist, and how they actually fare

The contrast set (IIT Delhi, IIT Roorkee, IIT (BHU) Varanasi, IIT Guwahati) is on the longlist for one reason only: PLAN.md section 4.3's working hypothesis was that old IITs would win on recruiter overlap (C1) but lose on prep vacuum (C5), since alumni-run consulting clubs with free casebooks are assumed to crowd BeyondTech's opening out. Showing the IITs on the same scorecard, scored by the same mechanical formula as every other longlist college, is the only honest way to test that hypothesis rather than assume it [PLAN.md 4.3, 4.6].

The result is reported plainly, as instructed, rather than massaged: none of the four contrast IITs reaches the final four picks. Two of them, IIT Delhi and IIT Roorkee, do clear the screen. The other two, IIT (BHU) Varanasi and IIT Guwahati, also clear the screen. All four score below the Fit cutoff of 2.40 that the actual fourth and fifth-ranked picks share.

Two findings here matter more than the headline. First, the "IITs win recruiter overlap, lose prep vacuum" hypothesis is only half confirmed, and the honest half-confirmation is more useful to a judge than a clean story would be. C1 (recruiter overlap) held clearly for all four IITs: each independently confirms at least 2 of the fixed anchor recruiters, and IIT Delhi's evidence is the single best-sourced C1 profile in the entire 32-college dataset, built on Careers360's reporting of the institute's own Office of Career Services data rather than an aggregator's guess. But C5 (prep vacuum) came back murky for every IIT checked, including IIT Delhi, where zero named undergraduate consulting or finance club with a casebook could be found after three distinct searches; the dossier pass later found DMS-level consulting and finance clubs (Samadhan and Vittarth at Roorkee, for example), but those sit inside the management-programme department, and whether they reach the broader undergraduate B.Tech population BeyondTech would actually sell to is an open evidence gap, not a confirmed answer either way [D IIT Delhi.C5, D IIT Roorkee.C5]. The scorecard does not oversimplify this to a clean "IITs lose on prep vacuum" claim, because the research does not support that clean a claim.

Second, and more decisive for the actual picks: C6, calendar fit, is where the IITs lose outright, and it is a structural loss, not an evidence gap. All four IITs score 1 of 5 on C6 because the common IIT placement calendar (Phase 1) is confirmed to start on 1 December across IIT Delhi, IIT Roorkee, IIT Bombay and IIT Kanpur [S645, S646]. Measured against BeyondTech's own 15 January 2027 next-batch start [W05], the next IIT placement season after that date is December 2027, eleven months away, the single worst C6 band any college in the dataset returns. Every one of the four non-IIT picks sits at 7 months or fewer to its own next season. No weighting adjustment inside the stability test moves an 11-month calendar lag enough to overcome a near-floor score on the one criterion BeyondTech's own 11-week, January-anchored bootcamp calendar cares about most directly [M T9]. This is also why none of the four IITs appears anywhere in the reserve bench with a meaningfully high stability share: IIT Roorkee's 5.3% and the other three's 0.0% (section 15.3 below) are not close calls.

## 14. The scorecard: seven criteria, one formula, 29 colleges

**Answer.** Every longlist college is scored on the same seven weighted criteria, from recruiter overlap down to calendar fit and access, producing a single Fit score out of 5.00; 32 colleges are scored in total, but only 29 (the 28 longlist colleges minus the three that fail the screen on batch size) are actually eligible to be picked, since the brief's own four calibration campuses are scored but excluded by rule. The formula is mechanical, not judged by the scorers, and it is built to be checked: the four campuses where BeyondTech already works score at or above the longlist median, with DTU scoring the highest Fit of any college in the entire dataset, which is the calibration working exactly as intended rather than a result to be explained away.

### 14.1 Why 29, not 32 or 28

The dataset file holds 32 rows: 24 non-IIT longlist colleges, 4 contrast-set IITs, and 4 calibration campuses (DTU, NSUT, Thapar, BIT Mesra) [D all rows]. Of the 28 longlist-plus-contrast colleges, 3 fail screen filter F1 on batch size and are dropped from ranking before scoring even matters for the purpose of picking (IIIT Delhi, IIIT Allahabad, IIIT Hyderabad; section 13.4 above). That leaves 25 eligible longlist colleges plus the 4 contrast IITs, 29 colleges in total that are actually scored, ranked and eligible to appear on the picked-or-reserve ladder. The 4 calibration campuses are scored on the identical formula (32 rows total get a Fit score), but F3 of the screen rules them out of ranking by construction, since they are not "new ground" per the brief's own framing of Pillar 2 [B16]. The 29-college figure is therefore the number that matters for the ranked table in section 14.4 and the picks in section 15; the 32-row figure is the number that matters for the calibration check in section 14.5.

### 14.2 The seven criteria, what each measures and why it carries the weight it does

| # | Criterion | Weight | What it measures | Why this weight | Primary data source | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Recruiter overlap | 0.25 | Count of the 19 countable anchor recruiters (of a 20-firm named list, see section 14.3 below for why one name never counts) that visited the campus in the last two placement seasons | The heaviest weight in the formula, because BeyondTech's whole product is company-specific prep for the firms that actually visit a campus [B09, B12]; a college with no overlap gets no benefit from BeyondTech's existing question bank regardless of anything else | Official placement reports and brochures where available, otherwise aggregator "top recruiter" lists cross-checked against the fixed 20-firm list | Aggregator lists are a small, inconsistent subset of a campus's true recruiter roster, especially at very large campuses (VIT Vellore reports 632 to 868 total recruiting companies per season but only 2 of the 20 anchors could be confirmed by name, almost certainly an undercount, not a true absence) [D VIT Vellore.C1] |
| C2 | Non-tech offer depth | 0.15 | Share of offers in analytics, consulting, finance and business roles, or a fallback count of non-tech recruiters of any kind where a sector split is not published | Sizes how much of the demand inside a recruiter-overlap campus is actually non-tech, since overlap alone does not say whether those firms hire B.Tech graduates into non-tech roles in volume | Institute sector-split disclosures (rare; found cleanly only for BITS Pilani and Manipal) or a recruiter-count fallback | Most colleges in the dataset have no published sector split at all, forcing the cruder firm-count fallback for 27 of 32 rows, which cannot distinguish a campus with five small non-tech recruiters from one with five large-volume ones |
| C3 | Addressable base | 0.15 | B.Tech students graduating per year | Sizes the pool a founding cohort and a second season can actually be drawn from; too small a base caps how many seasons the campus can sustain before saturating | NIRF data submissions where retrievable, aggregator citations of the same filings otherwise, sanctioned-intake proxies as a last resort | At several colleges (VIT Vellore, Manipal, IIT Delhi, IIT BHU) the official NIRF placement table could not be parsed as clean text, forcing a seat-intake or offer-count proxy instead of a true graduating-cohort figure |
| C4 | Society surface | 0.15 | Count of active non-tech societies (consulting, finance, product, analytics, entrepreneurship, posting in the last 6 months) and their combined Instagram plus LinkedIn followers | Measures the existing door BeyondTech would borrow trust through, per the governing thought's own "borrow a society's trust first" mechanism | Society official pages, 180 Degrees Consulting's own branch directory, Instagram/LinkedIn handle checks | Follower counts are the single most common NOT FOUND cell in the whole dataset; dozens of named societies across the 32 rows have a confirmed name and no confirmed follower number, so C4 in practice often collapses to a raw society count rather than the two-part measure the criterion actually specifies |
| C5 | Prep vacuum | 0.10 | Count of three sub-criteria present: a society-published casebook or prep programme, a named external prep brand with ambassadors or alumni claims there, institute-run non-tech training | Tie-breaker: a campus with less incumbent structured prep is a campus where BeyondTech's own offer is more differentiated, not competing against a free casebook that already does the same job | Society own-pages, `competitors.csv`'s named-alumni-college lists, student-forum accounts of institute-run training | The band runs backwards from intuition (fewer markers found scores higher, "5+" meaning none of the three present), so a thin research pass on an obscure college can accidentally inflate its C5 score by finding nothing, which is why C5 only carries a 0.10 weight rather than a heavier one |
| C6 | Calendar fit | 0.10 | Months between BeyondTech's next batch start (15 Jan 2027, W05) and the campus's placement or internship season start | A campus whose hiring season sits just after BeyondTech's own cohort finishes gives the founding cohort the shortest possible wait before its own proof point lands | Official calendar pages and notices where found, cross-NIT or cross-IIT analogy where a campus-specific date could not be sourced | Only 2 of the 32 rows (VIT Vellore, the 4 IITs as a bloc) have an independently sourced season-start month; the other 27 rely on an "August, est." national-convention assumption that the research explicitly could not upgrade to confirmed for any individual NIT, state university or BITS campus |
| C7 | Access | 0.10 | Count of 0 to 4+ bridges: same city or under 300 km from an existing campus (+2), a BeyondTech mentor or testimonial alumnus there (+1), a society co-host history with a DTU or NSUT society (+1), named in BeyondTech's own ambassador call (+1) | Tie-breaker on the practical cost of the first conversation: a campus with an existing bridge needs less cold-start trust-building than one with none | `R-A-beyondtech.md`'s mentor/testimonial roster, `R-J-assets.md`'s coordinates, W13's ambassador-post text | Every one of the six named mentors and testimonials returns NOT FOUND for college of origin across the entire dataset (RA47/RA48), so the "+1 mentor/testimonial alumnus" bridge never actually fires for any college scored; the criterion's real variance comes almost entirely from distance and the ambassador post |

Formula, shown identically in the deck's tinted callout and here: `Fit score = 0.25*C1 + 0.15*C2 + 0.15*C3 + 0.15*C4 + 0.10*C5 + 0.10*C6 + 0.10*C7`, maximum 5.00, where each Cn is a 1 to 5 band score, not the raw measure [PLAN.md 7.4]. C1 alone is worth as much as C5, C6 and C7 combined, which is a deliberate design choice, not an accident: a college can be convenient to reach and short on incumbent competition and still be a weak pick if BeyondTech's own recruiter question bank simply does not apply there.

### 22.3 Instrumentation

Three mechanisms carry every number in the KPI tree from a channel activation to the shared lead sheet, and all three are cheap enough that none of them competes with the budget in section 12 (DTU budget) or section 20 (campus economics) for cash.

- **UTM codes per channel.** Every one of the 11 DTU channels and every new-campus channel gets its own UTM-tagged link: a society workshop's sign-up form, the gated resource library's download gate, each paid Meta or LinkedIn creative, and each micro-influencer's post. Without this, the model's own channel-level CAC figures in T3 and T4 would be unverifiable in practice; the UTM code is what turns a model assumption into a measured fact once the plan is live.
- **Referral codes per ambassador.** Once the ambassador flywheel starts (season 2 at each new campus, section 19), every ambassador gets a unique referral code, not a shared campus-wide link. This is the only way to compute an individual ambassador's enrolments-per-season figure against the A14 benchmark (bear 2, base 4, bull 7) rather than guessing at an average across the whole cohort, and it is also the control that makes ambassador-fraud detection possible (section 23's risk register).
- **A single shared lead sheet.** Every channel, every campus and every ambassador writes into one sheet, not a separate spreadsheet per activation. This is a deliberate choice against BeyondTech's current state, where the sales motion is a single founder's WhatsApp inbox and a fit-interview calendar [W06, W11]; a shared sheet is the minimum structure needed before the founder can delegate any part of first-contact screening, which is itself one of the two levers behind the hours-freed figure in T11.

### 22.4 The 30, 60 and 90 day signals, restated as what would prove us wrong

Section 21 presented T14's thresholds as decision gates on a calendar. Read the same numbers as falsification tests, in the spirit of PLAN.md's own falsification rules (section 4.6): each one is a number that, if missed, means the plan's own model of how a new campus adopts BeyondTech is wrong, not just slow.

- **Day 30, fewer than 6 founding-cohort applications.** If a campus has not produced at least 6 applications a month after its case league, the model's bear-case assumption that a co-branded event converts attendees into applicants at a workable rate is wrong for that campus specifically, and continuing to spend against it would be funding a thesis the first month of real data already rejected.
- **Day 60, a founding-cohort fill under 10.** If half the founding cohort's 20 base-case seats (A17) remain unfilled two months in, either the founding-cohort discount (A18, 40% base) is not enough to overcome price opacity at a brand-new campus, or the host society's trust transfer (section 17's ladder) did not actually transfer, and both are falsifications of the launch mechanism, not the launch calendar.
- **Day 90, fewer than 2 offers secured.** This is the sharpest test of all, because it is the first real-world instance of BeyondTech's own promise, a non-tech placement prep course that produces offers, actually landing somewhere other than DTU, NSUT, Thapar or BIT Mesra. If a founding cohort of 20 students produces fewer than 2 offers in a full placement cycle, the core transferability assumption behind the whole pan-India pillar (that BeyondTech's recruiter-specific question bank travels to any campus the same 20 anchor recruiters visit) is the thing that is wrong, not merely this one campus's execution.

Each of these three numbers scales above its stop threshold into an explicit scale signal (14 applications, 12 fill, 7 offers), so the same instrumentation that catches a failing campus early also catches a campus that is quietly outperforming the base case and should have its budget and ambassador count moved up to the bull-case figures in T10 before season 2 begins.

|---|---|---|
| 30 | Fewer than 6 founding-cohort applications [M T14] | The case league converts attendees into applicants at a workable rate |
| 60 | Founding-cohort fill under 10 of 20 base-case seats [M T14, A17] | The founding discount and the host society's trust transfer are enough to close enrolments |

### 27.4 The Customer Validation Plan

The plan below is designed to run before Season 1's founding cohorts lock their final numbers (section 21, month 3 to 4), and is scoped to three segments: DTU's own current students, DTU's non-enrolled aspirants (students who have heard of BeyondTech but not enrolled), and the 4 new-campus founding-cohort candidates once each campus's case league has run.

**Target response counts by segment**

| Segment | Target responses | Method | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| DTU current students (years 2 to 4, all branches) | 150 | Survey, distributed through the society partner shortlist (section 10) and the gated resource library's own download flow | Month 1 to 2 (before the DTU budget's paid channels are funded) |
| DTU non-enrolled aspirants (students who have heard of BeyondTech but not applied) | 80 | Survey, distributed through the structured referral channel and the placement-cell information session sign-up sheet | Month 2 to 3 |
| New-campus founding-cohort candidates (post-case-league, pre-enrolment, across all 4 picks) | 40 per campus (160 total) | Survey, distributed at the case-league event itself before founding-cohort applications open | Within the Season 1 and Season 2 launch windows (section 21) |
| A subset of each segment, for depth | 6 to 10 per segment (18 to 30 total) | One-on-one interview, drawn from survey respondents who opt in | Within 2 weeks of each segment's survey closing |

**The 14-question survey instrument**

1. What year of study are you in, and what branch?
2. Have you heard of BeyondTech before today? (yes, no, not sure)
3. If yes, how did you first hear about it? (referral from a friend or senior, a society event, LinkedIn, Instagram, a poster or flyer on campus, other)
4. Have you ever considered preparing for a non-tech role (consulting, product, analytics, business analyst) at a campus placement drive?
5. If you have not enrolled in BeyondTech or a similar programme, what is the main reason? (price uncertainty, timing, did not know such a programme existed, prefer to prepare alone or with seniors, other)
6. Would you be more likely to enrol if the exact fee were published on the website before you had to ask?
7. Which of the following would make you trust a non-tech placement programme more: a named senior's result, a society's endorsement, a free sample class, a money-back policy, other?
8. At what point in your degree would you ideally start structured non-tech placement preparation? (start of 2nd year, start of 3rd year, start of 4th year, only once placements begin)
9. Have you used any of BeyondTech's free resources (the roadmap PDFs or the Guesstimate sampler)? If yes, did you continue to a paid programme afterward?
10. Which companies would you most want help preparing for? (open text, used to cross-check the 20-firm anchor-recruiter list)
11. Would you be willing to recommend a programme like this to a junior if you had a good experience?
12. How much would you expect to pay for an 11-week non-tech placement preparation programme with mentor support? (open numeric, used to recalibrate A01)
13. If you are a 2nd-year student, would you enrol in a placement-prep programme now, or wait until your 3rd year? (used to recalibrate A28)
14. Would you be interested in becoming a campus ambassador for a programme like this, if the ambassador had already been through the programme themselves?

**The 6-question interview guide**

1. Walk me through the last time you looked for help preparing for a non-tech campus recruiter. What did you try, and what made you stop or continue?
2. When you heard about BeyondTech (or a similar programme), what made you trust it, or what made you hesitate?
3. If BeyondTech published its exact fee on the website tomorrow, would that change anything about how you think about it?
4. Tell me about the role your society (or a senior, or a WhatsApp group) played in how you prepared for placements. Would a formal partnership between that society and a programme like this change anything?
5. What is the one thing a non-tech placement programme could do that would make you recommend it to five juniors, unprompted?
6. If you were offered a founding-cohort discount to join a brand-new version of this programme at your own campus, what would make you say yes versus wait and see?

**What each answer would change**

| Survey question | If the answer shows | The decision it changes |
|---|---|---|
| Q12 (expected fee) | A median expectation below ₹8,000 | A01's bear-case fee assumption would need to fall below its current ₹8,000 floor, and T15's breakeven enrolment counts would need to be re-run higher |
| Q13 (2nd-year enrolment intent) | Fewer than 20% of surveyed 2nd-years say they would enrol before 3rd year | A28's 20% second-year-share target (base case) should be revised down, and the KPI tree's month-12 second-year-share target in T13 would need a lower ceiling |
| Q6 (fee transparency) | A majority say a published fee would make them more likely to enrol | Strengthens the case for the layer-1 DTU fix (publishing a price band) ahead of schedule, potentially moving it earlier than month 3 in section 21's calendar |
| Q3 (first-heard channel) | A channel other than referral or society events dominates (for example, Instagram) | A05's 80% base-case referral share would need to be revised down and a new channel added to T3 |
| Q7 (trust signal) | Society endorsement outranks a named senior's result or a money-back policy | Strengthens the trust-ladder sequencing in section 17 (borrow before seed) over an alternative sequencing that leads with individual testimonials |
| Q14 (ambassador willingness, conditional on having been through the programme) | Fewer than the number needed to hit A14's base-case ambassador count per campus | A20's season-2 multiple (3.0x base) would need to be revised down, since the ambassador flywheel depends on enough founding-cohort members being willing |

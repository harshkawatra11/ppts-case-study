# Inspiration deck study: six organiser decks, page by page

Source PDFs: `BT/a-few-inspos/*.pdf`. Rendered to PNG at 100 dpi in `BT/case/inspo/` (75 pages
total; render script and output are the only files in that folder besides this study).
Slugs: `ops` = Ops Cogitate (IIM Raipur, 11 pages), `p3` = p3.pdf (8 loose slides from
several unrelated cases, no single storyline), `sb` = Street Boyz / Opsimius (IIM
Bangalore, 11 pages), `ec` = TeamEliteConsultant (Shri Ram College of Commerce, Grant
Thornton CASEQuest, 10 pages), `iima` = IIM A Winning Deck (Costa Coffee, Armageddon Case
Competition 2023, 27 pages), `inno` = InnovatED (IIM Rohtak, Masters' Union EdTech case,
8 pages).

Every page of `iima` and `inno` was opened and read (these two were not studied before
this file). `ops`, `p3`, `sb`, `ec` were spot-checked at 4 to 5 pages each, chosen to cover
cover, body, appendix and any page the plan's section 14.3 diagram table already cites.

This file cross-checks, and in places corrects, the seed table in `BT/PLAN.md` section
14.3 (G1 to G20). Corrections and gaps are called out inline and summed up at the end
under "Diagram devices not yet in PLAN.md section 14.3."

**What this deck proves that the plan's seed table does not already capture:** the
appendix is not a dumping ground of leftover slides, it is a parallel, numbered,
rationale-columned ledger that lets every headline number on the core slides trace to a
build-up. `BT/PLAN.md` section 14 does not yet specify an appendix table format; this
deck's "value / rationale" two-column, numbered-exhibit convention is a ready-made recipe
(see the new-device list at the end of this file, ND1).

### S5. Specific weaknesses to avoid

- **Pasted dashboard/Excel screenshots standing in for a native chart** (`inno` p7): the
  one clear violation of the "native vector only, no imported chart images" rule this
  project's own `ROOT/CLAUDE.md` sets as non-negotiable. Confirms that rule is worth
  enforcing strictly rather than treating as aspirational, since even a strong finalist
  deck slipped on it.
- **No risk register, no consolidated KPI target table, and no dated closing ask in any
  of the six decks** (see S2 table): this is the organiser-deck blind spot `PLAN.md`
  section 14.2 already identifies as where this project's deck should go further. Two of
  the six (`inno`, `sb`) end on a bare "Thank You" slide, the exact anti-pattern
  `PLAN.md` P08 and the organiser's own playbook (`BT/case/03-organiser-playbook-digest.md`
  P08) warn against.
- **Mixed icon styles within a single deck**: `inno` combines flat line-icons (p2 to p4)
  with illustrated cartoon avatars (p5), a style break the brief's own playbook (P09, by
  extension) would likely mark down; this project's single consistent icon language (flat,
  ink-stroked, no stock art) avoids this.
- **Inconsistent map-leader geometry within the same deck**: `sb` uses curved arcs on its
  export map (p "Exports") but straight two-segment leaders on its site-plan map (p3), a
  small but visible inconsistency; this project should pick one leader geometry (per
  `PLAN.md` section 14.6) and hold it across every map slide.
- **Label-only or vague page titles are rare across the sample but not absent**: `p3`'s
  loose slides (not a cohesive deck, so less diagnostic) and `ec`'s repeated identical
  slide title across 2 consecutive pages (p2 and p3 both read "Bridging gaps: empowering
  trans lives in unprivileged areas") show how a title can fail to carry the argument
  forward slide to slide; `PLAN.md` P04's ghost-deck test at Gate G5 is the right defence.
- **Overcrowding in the single densest page found**: `inno` p3 at roughly 330 words plus
  9 distinct visual elements is at the edge of legibility even at this project's own
  22pt-floor, 4-to-5-type-level EY density doctrine; useful as an upper-bound caution, not
  a target.
- **Unsourced or loosely sourced stat call-outs**: several high-visibility numbers across
  the sample (`iima` p4's "1.4 Lakhs estimated campaign engagements", `inno` p5's "99%
  increase in EdTech usage since 2020") carry no inline citation on the slide itself, only
  a generic deck-level source line; `PLAN.md`'s per-number source-tag discipline (section
  2.4) is stricter than anything observed in this sample and should not be relaxed to
  match it.
- **A primary-research claim without a stated sample or method on the slide itself**:
  `inno` p6 labels its 3 pie charts "Primary Research" with a response count (299) given
  only in small print per chart, not the method (online form, self-selected); this
  project's own content-integrity rule (`PLAN.md` section 2.1, honest Customer Validation
  Plan instead of invented primary data) is the correct stricter standard.
- **A proper-noun inconsistency within one deck**: `iima`'s scrappage-policy slide
  (`p3` p1, a different case) names "Bronze, Silver, Gold & Silver" in its body text,
  repeating "Silver" and omitting a fourth distinct tier name, a small but real proofing
  miss; reinforces `PLAN.md` P12's proper-noun sweep as worth running literally, not just
  once informally.

  "hub" is a large central shape (a funnel or an entry-mode list) and multiple independent
  cards each get their own dashed leader into it from either side.
- **ND3, Hexagonal flywheel** (`inno` p4). A variant of G14 using hexagon cards around a
  circular hub instead of G14's four layered cards at compass points; worth noting as an
  alternate shape option under the same G14 entry rather than a wholly new number.
- **ND4, Continuous tapering chevron spine carrying numbered stop badges** (`ops` p6).
  Distinct from G1's discrete stacked trapezoids and from G5's road: one single ribbon
  shape (built as a long thin polygon tapering from top to bottom) with circular icon
  badges placed along its centreline at regular intervals, each paired with a label to the
  side. Useful for an ordered benefit or outcome ladder that is not a funnel (no narrowing
  population) and not a literal path (no geography).
- **ND5, Traffic-light status-dot grid, 3-state** (`sb` p "Exports", `sb` Exhibit 2).
  Distinct from G3's heat-grid (which PLAN specifies as a 1-to-5 numeric heat scale): this
  device uses exactly 3 colour states (red/yellow/green) with no number in the cell at
  all, just a filled circle, for a fast-read fitness-check table rather than a weighted
  score.
- **ND6, Boxed stakeholder flow-chart** (`sb` p "Financing and Government Interventions").
  A hub box (the reporting/project company) connected by labelled directional arrows to 5
  or 6 surrounding stakeholder boxes (sponsors, lenders, offtakers, EPC/O&M providers).
  Distinct from G14's flywheel (which implies a cycle) and from G12's issue tree (which
  implies hierarchy): this is a true process/relationship flow-chart with mixed arrow
  directions.
- **ND7, Price-tag polygon callout** (`inno` p6). A hexagon-with-punch-hole shape (vector
  path: rectangle with two angled end-caps, one side carrying a small circle "hole") used
  to present a single headline price, distinct from this project's own layered sticker
  card (`ROOT/CLAUDE.md` section 2) but close enough in spirit to adopt directly as a
  dedicated price/number callout shape, particularly for the BeyondTech fee-sensitivity
  exhibit (A01).
- **ND8, Toggle-switch metaphor as a feature-comparison diagram** (`p3` p5, Eat Confetti).
  Two cards rendered to resemble an on/off UI toggle (a pill shape with a circular thumb)
  used to represent two alternative service options, an unusual but legible device for
  contrasting exactly two binary choices without a table. Lower priority than ND1 to ND7
  for this project since the BeyondTech deck's comparisons are rarely binary.

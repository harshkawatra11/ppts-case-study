# The organiser's own playbook, digested: what it tells us about the judges

Source: `BT/Consulting_Pitch_Deck_Playbook.pdf`, "Consulting Pitch Deck Playbook", 20 pages, written
by (and for) DTU Consulting Group, the co-organiser and co-judge of this competition. Because DCG
wrote this document for its own members, it is the closest thing available to the judges' private
rubric. Every slide this team builds should satisfy the rules below, not just the brief's stated
weights.

The playbook itself labels every claim as Evidence (research-backed), Consensus (practitioner
consensus), Framework (a published framework) or My call (the playbook author's own judgment). That
same discipline is reused here: each row below cites its playbook section.

## P01 to P12: the rules that bind this deck

| ID | Playbook rule | Playbook source | How this plan obeys it |
|---|---|---|---|
| P01 | Teach the buyer one thing they did not know. Bringing new ideas was the #1 factor separating winners from runners-up in buyer interviews (RAIN Group, evidence-labelled) | Section 1, principle 2 | One outside-in exhibit per core slide. The strongest two: the recruiter-overlap index (no one has ranked colleges by BeyondTech's own recruiter list before) and the finding that the eight free roadmap PDFs are open, ungated Drive links |
| P02 | Play the buyer's problem back in their own words. Winners showed they understood buyer needs about 2.5x as often as runners-up (RAIN via Hinge, evidence-labelled) | Section 1, principle 3 | Slide 3 quotes the brief's own language on recall and channel dependency (B10, B11, B13) rather than restating it in our own words |
| P03 | Lead with the answer. A busy reader gets the recommendation in the first minute and reads everything else as supporting evidence (Minto's Pyramid Principle, framework-labelled) | Section 1, principle 4; section 3 | Slide 2 states the full governing thought and the three contrarian moves before any analysis slide |
| P04 | Titles alone, read top to bottom, should tell the whole story; if they don't, the deck has no argument (practitioner consensus) | Section 1, principle 5; section 3, "Build it in five steps" | Touchpoint B (ghost-deck test) at Gate G5 reads all 12 slide titles with no other content and checks they form a complete argument |
| P05 | Put three facts only this specific client gave you on the early slides, not generic facts that could apply to any company (practitioner consensus, ranked mistake #1 if absent) | Section 2 ("What we heard"); section 7, mistake 1 | Slides 2 to 4 use W05 (exact batch-start date), W06 (the actual gated admission flow) and W08/W13-W15 (the specific, checkable facts from BeyondTech's own pages), not generic placement-prep language |
| P06 | Source every number under it; make assumptions explicit (practitioner consensus, and Analyst Academy's McKinsey-chart teardown) | Section 4, "Sources" row; section 8, checklist item | Every content slide carries a 14pt source line; the appendix slide carries the full assumptions register with bear/base/bull and a basis for each |
| P07 | Promise decisions and artefacts the team controls, never revenue it does not control; overpromising raises perceived risk instead of lowering it (RAIN Group, evidence-labelled) | Section 7, mistake 9 | The deck promises enrolments, a scorecard, a launch calendar and kill/scale thresholds, never a revenue guarantee. Three scenarios (bear/base/bull) everywhere a number is uncertain |
| P08 | End on a dated, specific next step, not "Thank you" or "Questions?"; proposals that end in "let me know" tend to stall (Agiled, practitioner consensus) | Section 1, principle 10; section 7, mistake 8 | Slide 10 (the last core slide) closes with a dated first action. The separate thank-you slide (slide 12) exists only to repeat the team table, matching this project's standing cover/thank-you style |
| P09 | Use real artefacts: screenshots of the client's own product, not stock photography or generic AI illustration (practitioner consensus, "My call") | Section 4, "Images" row | Real screenshots of BeyondTech's own site (home hero, admission steps, resources page) and real logos. ## Additional structural notes from the playbook, used as design input (not binding rules)

- The playbook's own recommended skeleton is a 12-slide main deck plus an appendix, which matches
  this project's locked slide count of cover + 9 core + appendix + thank-you = 12 total, even though
  the two structures were decided independently (the brief's own 8-12 core-slide limit, plus this
  project's standing cover/appendix/thank-you convention).
- SCQA (Situation, Complication, Question, Answer) is the playbook's recommended opening structure
  (section 3); this maps directly to diagram G8 in `PLAN.md` section 14.3 and is used on slide 2.
- The playbook explicitly distinguishes a "presented deck" (sparse, three-second glance per slide)
  from a "leave-behind" (denser, reads without a presenter). Per the case brief this deliverable is a
  single PDF or PPTX with no stated live presentation, so this project treats the whole deck as a
  leave-behind: density stays high throughout, consistent with the EY-doctrine house style in
  `ROOT/CLAUDE.md` section 1, which this project's standing design system already commits to
  regardless of what the playbook would otherwise suggest for a presented deck.
- The playbook's own 45-point scoring rubric (its section 8) is reproduced as the organiser
  self-score in `PLAN.md` section 16.3 and is run by the critic at touchpoints C and D.

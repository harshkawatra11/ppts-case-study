# Judge Q&A bank: Eurogrip Case Study 4

Thirty questions a judging panel may ask about the mechanic advocacy programme, with answers of 40 to 90 words.
Each answer names the research section or appendix panel behind it. Model hash `b1125880f5`.

## 1. How did you count the mechanic and puncture-shop universe, and why trust 7.8 lakh

Four independent methods: an enterprise-survey estimate (5.0 lakh, range 2.7 to 7.5), a two-wheeler-parc
estimate (10.5 lakh, range 7.7 to 15.0), and two programme-enrolment floors, rival schemes at 1.5 lakh and
Eurogrip's own partner count at 0.3 lakh. The base case, 7.8 lakh, is the mean of the two estimates, never
below the larger floor. Research section 6, Appendix A1.

## 2. Why does the universe matter if members only reach 1.2 lakh of it

The universe sizes the addressable market, not the enrolment target. At steady state the programme reaches
about 15% of the base-case universe, a coverage cap set deliberately below Castrol's own enrolment share in
lubricants, so the budget stays inside 1.5% to 2% of relevant unit revenue. Research section 6, section 11.

## 3. Why do non-stocking mechanics matter if they never sell a tyre

The brief itself states a meaningful share of advocacy already comes from mechanics who do not stock Eurogrip.
Crediting the code at the point a rider buys, not at the point a shop stocks, is what lets the programme reach
that share at all. A stock-linked scheme such as cash per tyre cannot reach this segment by design. Research
section 7, section 10.

## 4. The brief's own insight on tools overtaking cash is struck through. Why does your design still lean on tools

That specific sentence is never presented as a finding, and no slide or document section cites it. The
tools-and-visibility direction instead comes from two live sentences: the judging criterion itself, which names
tools and brand visibility, and the unstruck insight on rising preference for branded merchandise. The pilot's
reward-currency arm test, tool credits against an equal cash value, is what actually tests the claim. Research
section 1, Appendix F.

## 5. Why not just run a cash-per-tyre scheme like the lubricant industry does

Modelled at the same budget, a cash-per-tyre scheme would cost about ₹41.7 crore a year, with about 91% of
that spend going to tyres that would have sold at that shop anyway. It also rewards only shops that stock
Eurogrip, missing the non-stocking majority of the advocacy network. Research section 9, Appendix A2.

## 6. How is 4.4 extra tyres a shop a year actually calculated

Annual programme spend divided by member shops divided by contribution margin per tyre. In the base case,
₹22.0 crore spent across 1.16 lakh members, against a contribution of about ₹419 per tyre, gives 4.4 extra
tyres a member shop needs to sell a year for the programme to break even. Research section 11, Appendix A2.

## 7. What if break-even comes in above your kill line of 12

The team set 12 extra tyres a year as the kill threshold before running the model. The base case clears it at
4.4, and even the bear case, 11.7, stays under it. If a future model run crossed 12, the response is to cut
Tier 3 hardware to lease-only first, and narrow the programme to the stocking and service segments only if
that is not enough. Research section 2, section 15.

## 8. Why should the panel believe the ₹1,628 cost per member a year

It is built bottom-up from five priced lines: tools and refills, visibility kits, training, field enrolment and
tech, each sourced from IndiaMART and Amazon.in tool prices and benchmarked adjacent-category programme costs,
not asserted as a round number. The full build-up, bear, base and bull, is in Appendix A2, Table T3.

## 9. How does the retailer have any incentive to log a mechanic's code

The retailer earns a small logging credit inside its own existing loyalty scheme for every code entered, so
logging is not an unpaid favour on top of a sale it would have made anyway. The pilot specifically tests
whether this incentive moves behaviour in the field before the design scales past two states. Research section
10, Appendix A6.

## 10. What stops a mechanic and a retailer colluding to fake codes

Three controls together: one credit per tyre serial number, so a code cannot be reused; a flag when the
retailer and the claimed mechanic are more than 15 kilometres apart; and random IVR call-backs to riders to
confirm the recommendation actually happened. Research section 10, Appendix A6.

## 11. Why Tamil Nadu and Uttar Pradesh as the two pilot states

Tamil Nadu is Eurogrip's home state, with the deepest existing distribution to pilot the loop quickly. Uttar
Pradesh has large two-wheeler registrations but a comparatively thin current Eurogrip presence, so it tests the
programme where the brand starts from a weaker position. Together they bracket the best and the harder case.
Research section 12, Appendix A1.

## 12. What exactly happens if a pilot gate fails

Three named gates. G1 at the end of quarter 2 checks 60% of members active plus a 15-point audit lift; failing
on activity alone triggers an onboarding fix, not a stop, but failing on the advocacy half stops expansion at
the two pilot states. G2 at quarter 4 checks the break-even rate itself. Research section 12, section 13,
Appendix A6.

## 13. How do mystery-rider audits actually measure a recommendation

An auditor poses as a rider asking a mechanic or puncture shop which tyre to buy, in both member and matched
control districts, and records whether Eurogrip is named unprompted. The design uses 140 shops an arm, 560
audits a year, at a cost of about ₹3.9 lakh, sized with a standard two-proportion sample formula. Research
section 13.

## 14. Is a mystery-shopping audit a validated method for this specific claim

General service-quality mystery-shopping literature is well established, but no published study was found
validating unprompted brand-recommendation audits for a two-wheeler tyre specifically. The document states the
design as methodologically analogous to published mystery-shopping studies, not identical to one, and treats
this as an open limitation. Research section 13, section 0.4.

## 15. Why does the scale phase wait until quarter 3 and 4 to judge results

Two full quarters of logged-code data are needed before a code-linked-tyres-per-member rate reflects steady
member behaviour rather than early-adopter enthusiasm. Judging the economics too early would conflate a
genuinely low rate with a rate that simply has not matured yet. Research section 12.

## 16. How does the programme scale nationally without losing cost discipline

Distributor-led enrolment keeps the fixed cost structure largely flat as members grow, since the same
distributor sales staff already visiting a territory add shops to the programme rather than a dedicated
national sales force being built from scratch. The budget model carries this through all eight rollout
quarters inside the 1.5% to 2% revenue band. Research section 12, Appendix A2.

## 17. What is the single biggest risk to this programme, and what is the control

Activity fading after the first six months, the highest-scored risk on the probability-times-impact register.
The control already built into the design is the six-month re-tiering review itself: a member who stops
logging codes loses tier status and tool-refill access, which is the loss-aversion mechanism meant to keep
activity from fading in the first place. Research section 14, Appendix A6.

## 18. Why tiers that renew every six months instead of a one-time sign-up reward

A one-time incentive is exactly the "high-cost, one-off" pattern the brief rules out. Renewal keeps the
programme self-sustaining: consumables run out every quarter, giving members a recurring reason to log codes,
and losing an already-held tier is a stronger motivator than a future reward never yet received. Research
section 10, section 2.

## 19. Does the field have any evidence this loss-aversion mechanism actually works on mechanics specifically, or only in general behavioural economics

No field evidence specific to Indian two-wheeler mechanics exists yet. The claim rests on general
behavioural-economics literature on loss aversion, and the reward-currency arm test inside the pilot is
designed partly to generate real evidence for this population before the design scales nationally. Research
section 10, section 13.

## 20. Where does the ₹22.0 crore in year two actually get spent

Five lines: tools and refills take the largest share at 55%, about ₹12.1 crore; visibility kits 16%; field
enrolment 14%; training 7%; and tech and audits 8%. The full year-by-year and line-by-line build is in
Appendix A2 and model table T4. Research section 11.

## 21. Why 1.75% of unit revenue as the base budget rather than the full 2% the brief allows

The base case spends at 1.75%, leaving the headroom to 2.0% as contingency rather than spending the ceiling
from day one. The brief's own 1.5% to 2% band was itself cross-checked against CEAT's and Apollo's disclosed
advertisement and sales-promotion spend, both near 2.1% of revenue in their most recent reported year. Research
section 4, Appendix A2.

## 22. How confident are you in the relevant unit revenue figure the budget is a percentage of

Less confident than in most other numbers in this document. No source discloses TVS Srichakra's replacement
revenue split directly, so the figure is derived from a CRISIL rationale's OEM, replacement and export brackets
applied to FY26 consolidated revenue, stated honestly as a derived assumption with bear, base and bull readings,
not a disclosed fact. Research section 3, section 1.10.

## 23. What if the retailer app, Bandhan, is no longer live the way the design assumes

The most recent primary description of Bandhan dates to its 2019 to 2020 launch window, not a current listing.
The design names the app but does not claim a current mechanic user count, and the model carries a one-time
build or extension cost as a named assumption in case the app needs rebuilding before the pilot can log a
single code. Research section 3, section 10, Appendix D.

## 24. How do you know mechanics actually decide which tyre a rider buys, and not just recommend one that gets overridden

No study was found attaching a percentage to how often a mechanic's recommendation decides the purchase, at any
source tier. The document uses the brief's own qualitative claim, that the mechanic is the most frequented
touchpoint and most often names the brand, without inventing a number the research could not support. Research
section 1.10, section 5.

## 25. What did you validate with real people before proposing this design

Nothing yet. No interviews, surveys or letters of intent were run for Case 4. Instead of presenting invented
primary research, the document proposes a named validation plan: 60 interviews across 4 cities, two per pilot
state, with mechanics, puncture shops and riders, run before the pilot's first code is logged. Research section
15.

## 26. Why tiers of repair-kit, gauge-and-apron, and inflator-and-listing specifically, not some other set of tools

Each tier's reward set is priced from the same tool-and-visibility cost benchmarks that back the budget model,
chosen to match what a shop at that activity level actually uses daily rather than a symbolic gift. Tier 3's
inflator and locator listing specifically target the stocking gap the brief's own insight names. Research
section 10, Appendix A2.

## 27. How does this design compare with what rival tyre makers already run

No major two-wheeler tyre rival, MRF, CEAT, Apollo, Bridgestone or Michelin, was found running a mechanic-facing
loyalty programme of any kind. Every rival loyalty structure found runs at the dealer or stockist layer.
A stock-independent, mechanic-facing design is a category no identified rival has entered. Research section 8,
Appendix A3.

## 28. What is the closest existing precedent for this kind of programme, even outside tyres

JK Tyre's own Advantage 2.0 dealer programme is the closest tracking-mechanism precedent, a dashboard built
around offtake and warranty-registration data, directly analogous to logging a code against a tyre serial. But
it is explicitly a dealer programme, not mechanic-facing, which is exactly the gap this design is built to fill.
Research section 8.

## 29. Why does the measurement system rely on four separate layers instead of one clean metric

A single metric cannot distinguish engagement fading from economics failing. Monthly active share, the
mystery-audit lift, code-linked tyre sales and the brand re-survey each isolate a different failure mode, so a
gate failure can be diagnosed by layer, logging stopped but advocacy strong points to onboarding, logging
strong but sales short points to the economics. Research section 13.

## 30. If you had to cut this to one sentence for a judge who reads nothing else, what is it

Pay for the advice, not the stock, and pay in tools and status rather than cash: a free tiered programme that
reaches 1.2 lakh mechanic and puncture shops for ₹22.0 crore a year, paying for itself at 4.4 extra tyres a
shop a year against a kill line of 12. Research section 2.

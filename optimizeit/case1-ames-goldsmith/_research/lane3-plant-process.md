## 2. Silver–Ethylene Oxide (EO) Catalyst Chemistry Background
*(General industry/technical knowledge — not specific to this plant unless noted)*

- **Why silver:** Silver is used industrially as the active catalytic metal for direct vapor-phase epoxidation of ethylene to ethylene oxide (C₂H₄ + ½O₂ → C₂H₄O). Silver is essentially the only metal catalyst that gives commercially useful selectivity toward the epoxide rather than full combustion to CO₂/H₂O; EO-grade silver catalysts (typically silver deposited on a low-surface-area α-alumina support, often with alkali-metal and rhenium promoters) can reach selectivity approaching ~90% in modern formulations. [general catalysis literature, corroborated in search summary]
- **Catalyst poisoning by sulfur:** Sulfur-containing compounds are well-documented poisons for silver EO catalysts. Even trace levels of H₂S, COS, mercaptans, or other sulfur species in the ethylene/oxygen feed will chemisorb onto the silver surface and convert surface silver to **silver sulfide (Ag₂S)**, which is catalytically inactive and essentially irreversible under process conditions — Ag₂S has an extremely low solubility product (Ksp ≈ 6.3 × 10⁻⁵⁰), so the reaction 2Ag + H₂S → Ag₂S + H₂ (or analogous oxidation-reduction with other sulfur species) proceeds essentially to completion at the catalyst surface once sulfur is present.
- **Industrial mitigation:** EO plants desulfurize the ethylene feed upstream of the reactor using a **sulfur guard bed** at ambient temperature specifically to protect the silver catalyst from this poisoning mechanism.
- **Implication for spent-catalyst composition:** Because sulfur poisoning is one of the principal deactivation/end-of-life mechanisms for EO silver catalysts (alongside normal sintering/loss of surface area and chloride-moderator effects), **spent EO silver catalyst returned for reclamation can be expected to carry some fraction of its silver as Ag₂S** (silver sulfide) rather than as pure metallic silver, in addition to residual alkali/rhenium promoters and the alumina support matrix. This is general catalyst-chemistry knowledge (not a plant-specific finding), but it is directly relevant background: **a refining plant that receives and processes spent EO catalyst as routine feedstock should be expected to have sulfide-bearing residues/sludges present in its process inventory and waste streams as a matter of normal operation**, independent of any specific accident.
- **Sources:** general catalysis/surface-chemistry literature on Ag₂S formation and Ksp (ScienceDirect "Silver Sulfide" topic overview; PMC review on near-ambient-pressure oxidation of silver with sulfur impurities); general process description of EO sulfur guard beds (widely documented EO-process design knowledge, corroborated via search).

## 3. Precious Metal / Silver Catalyst Refining — Process Literature
*(General industry/patent literature — not specific to this plant)*

### 3a. Spent EO catalyst → silver recovery, general routes

Multiple patents and technical papers describe the recovery of silver (and associated promoter metals such as rhenium) from spent Ag/α-Al₂O�3 ethylene-oxide catalyst. Recurring process elements across the literature:

1. **Physical prep:** grinding/comminution of the spent catalyst pellets to expose surface area.
2. **Optional roasting/oxidative pretreatment:** some processes roast the ground catalyst in a mixed gas atmosphere (e.g., chlorine/alkane/oxygen/nitrogen mixtures) to convert residual chlorides/organics and improve subsequent leaching.
3. **Acid leaching/dissolution — the core recovery step:** the near-universal industrial approach is to **strip and dissolve the silver from the spent catalyst by leaching with an acid in which silver is soluble, classically nitric acid** (sometimes with an oxidant such as hydrogen peroxide added to the nitric acid leach to boost dissolution of less-reactive silver species), followed by thorough washing/filtration of the alumina support residue (filter cake) away from the silver-bearing filtrate (pregnant leach solution).
   - Some modern/patented variants use alternative leach chemistries — e.g., cerium-oxidation leaching combined with ammonia complexation — reporting silver recovery as high as ~98%; another patented route dissolves the filtered residue in ammonia water with a reducing agent to directly precipitate "sponge silver."
4. **Metal separation/purification of the pregnant solution:** precipitation (e.g., with hydrochloric acid to precipitate silver chloride, or via reduction) or **electrodeposition/electrolytic refining** to recover metallic silver from solution, with reported total silver recovery rates commonly cited at 98–99.7%+ in the patent/technical literature. Rhenium (a common EO catalyst promoter) can be separately recovered via ion-exchange enrichment from the raffinate, in processes designed to co-recover both metals.
5. **Filter residue / spent alumina carrier:** after silver stripping, the depleted alumina support may itself be regenerated/reused as fresh catalyst carrier (a separate stream of patent literature covers "regeneration of the Al₂O₃ carrier material of spent Ag/Al₂O₃ supported catalysts").

**Key cited sources:**
- Google Patents/Patsnap, "Method for recovering silver and rhenium from waste ethylene oxide catalyst," CN113564367A — describes grinding → roasting (Cl/alkane/O₂/N₂ atmosphere) → nitric acid + H₂O₂ leach/filter → HCl precipitation of filtrate → ion-exchange rhenium recovery → ammonia-water dissolution + reduction of filter residue to sponge silver, with silver recovery ≥99.7% and rhenium recovery >87%.
- USPTO 5849319, "Uses for stripped spent silver catalysts" — confirms the general industrial description: silver values are recovered "by extracting or leaching the spent catalyst with an acid in which the silver is soluble, such as nitric acid, and then thoroughly washing the catalyst."
- USPTO 6156329, "Stripped spent silver catalysts and novel uses thereof" (related family).
- USPTO 4530910, "Regeneration of the Al₂O₃ carrier material of a spent Ag/Al₂O₃ supported catalyst."
- USPTO 5013533, "Process for recovering valuable metals from spent catalysts."

### 3b. Electrolytic silver refining (the Moebius process) — general metallurgical background

Once silver is in solution or has been cast into anode form, high-purity refining is classically done electrolytically:

- The **Moebius process** is the standard industrial electrolytic method for silver refining. Impure silver anodes are suspended in an electrolyte bath (**typically silver nitrate + nitric acid solution**); thin sheets of pure silver or stainless steel serve as cathodes. Under applied DC current, the impure anode dissolves (Ag → Ag⁺ + e⁻), silver ions migrate to the cathode and deposit as high-purity metallic silver, while base-metal/other impurities either stay in solution or fall to the tank bottom as **anode slime/"silver mud."**
- **Electrolyte management:** copper is a common contaminant that builds up in the silver nitrate/nitric acid electrolyte over repeated refining cycles; industry practice caps electrolyte copper at roughly ~60 g/L, above which copper co-deposits at the cathode and degrades product purity — meaning refiners periodically **bleed/regenerate or replace nitric-acid-based electrolyte**, generating a nitric-acid-bearing waste/recovery stream. A related patent (US8282903B2, "Method for recovering nitric acid and purifying silver nitrate electrolyte") addresses exactly this electrolyte-regeneration step.
- Electrolytic (Moebius-type) refining is explicitly the technology Ames Goldsmith's own facility listing states it uses for silver reclamation ("silver reclamation ... using electrolytic refining technology" — Marshall/b2bwv listing, §1 above), which ties the general Moebius-process description directly to this plant's stated capability (though the listing does not confirm Moebius by name specifically).

**Key cited sources:**
- [Comprehensive Guide to Electrolytic Silver Refining — MFG Shop / machinemfg.com](https://shop.machinemfg.com/an-overview-of-the-electrolytic-refining-of-silver/)
- [Innovations in Silver Refining: The Moebius Process — Noble Gold Investments](https://noblegoldinvestments.com/learn/blog/moebius-process-silver-refining/)

**Bottom line for PFD purposes:** the two-stage architecture consistently reported in the literature — **(1) acid (nitric acid) leach/dissolution of spent catalyst or scrap to get silver into solution, followed by (2) electrolytic (Moebius-type) deposition to recover LME/investment-grade pure silver, with acid/electrolyte recovery and regeneration as a supporting loop and a residue/anode-slime/filter-cake waste stream** — is well-supported as the generic industry-standard process architecture for exactly this category of plant (spent precious-metal-catalyst refiner). This is not confirmed as *this specific plant's* proprietary flowsheet, but it is the standard the industry literature converges on and is reasonable to use as a labeled "representative/generic" PFD.

### 3.2 EPA Risk Management Plan (RMP / CAA §112(r))

**No RMP filing for this facility was located.** Searches for "EPA Risk Management Plan
Catalyst Refiners Ames Goldsmith Institute" returned only generic RMP-program background
(EPA's RMP rule page, AFPM/industry commentary on RMP rule changes) and did not surface an
RMP registration, submission, or EPA enforcement record specific to this facility. This is
notable given the case brief's own framing: RMP applies only to facilities holding regulated
substances above threshold quantities, and **nitric acid itself is not on EPA's RMP §112(r)
regulated-substances list** (RMP's acid-related listed substances are chemicals like anhydrous
ammonia, chlorine, hydrofluoric acid, oleum, etc.; hydrogen sulfide *is* an RMP-listed toxic
substance at a 10,000-lb threshold, but as a reaction byproduct rather than a stored/handled
quantity, its formation here would not by itself have triggered an RMP filing obligation).
**No evidence either way was found confirming whether Catalyst Refiners had any other
RMP-triggering substance on-site (e.g., anhydrous ammonia in refrigeration, if present).**
This should be treated as an open/unresolved research question, not stated as a settled
"facility had no RMP" fact, since absence of search results is not proof of absence in EPA's
RMP database (which is not fully public-searchable by facility name via ordinary web search;
a targeted query of EPA's RMP*eSubmit or a FOIA-based lookup would be the reliable path,
neither of which was available in this research pass).

## 6. Casualty-count reconciliation note (cross-cutting flag)

Sources materially disagree on the number of people hospitalized/injured, which matters for a
compliance dossier's internal consistency:
- CSB press release (2026-04-23): "more than 30 others required medical care"
- CSB dedicated investigation page: "2 fatalities and **4** additional injuries" (likely a
  narrower/different category — possibly CSB's own direct-investigation injury count vs.
  total-treated-in-community count; not reconciled by any fetched source)
- Chemistry World: "21 hospitalised"
- Mountain State Spotlight: "19+ injuries (including 7 first responders)"
- PBS/NPR wire coverage: "19 to hospital" / other wire pickups say "30 more to hospitals"
- Case brief given for this task: "dozens hospitalized"

**Recommend using "2 fatalities; reported injury/hospitalization counts range from ~19 to
30+ depending on source and date of reporting (early counts were lower and rose as more
patients were treated); CSB's own case page cites 4 additional injuries, a narrower figure not
reconciled with press reporting"** rather than picking a single hospitalization number, to
avoid presenting false precision the sources themselves don't support (consistent with the
project's "statistics stay at the precision the source actually supports" rule).

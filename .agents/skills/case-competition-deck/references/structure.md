# Section Structure Blueprint (Case-Agnostic)

The 8-section structure that organizes any persuasive analytical deck. The structure is universal; the specific content within each section depends on the case type (see `case_types/*.md` for type-specific guidance).

Slide numbers in parentheses assume a 100-slide deck. Scale proportionally for shorter formats (e.g., 30-slide consulting case → divide ranges by ~3).

---

## How sections map by case type

| Section | M&A / Finance | Strategy | Marketing | Social Impact | Policy / Trade / IR | Operations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| II. Context | Industry analysis | Market analysis | Consumer + competitive landscape | Problem / population analysis | Geopolitical / economic context | Industry + value chain analysis |
| III. Actors | Acquirer + Target | Company + Competitors | Customer + Competitors + Brand | Beneficiaries + Implementers + Funders | Countries / Blocs / Domestic groups | Customers + Suppliers + Operations |
| IV. Analysis | Comparables + DCF + Synergies | Market sizing + NPV of options | Customer LTV + ROAS + brand metrics | Cost-effectiveness + SROI + outcomes | Welfare economics + impact assessment | Cost-benefit + throughput + change cost |
| V. Feasibility | Financing + governance + regulatory | Capability gap + competitive response | Brand fit + execution capacity | Implementation risk + sustainability | Political feasibility + diplomatic cost | Change management + capability |
| VI. Alternative | Alternative target | Alternative strategy | Alternative segment / positioning | Alternative intervention | Alternative policy instrument | Alternative redesign |

## Front matter (slides 1–5)

### Slide 1: Cover
- Deck title (often thematic — e.g., "The Road Ahead", "Engineering the Next GPU", "Closing the Last-Mile Gap")
- Team name (often punny — "The Cash Cows", "Kicking A$$ets", "Providentia Capital")
- Member names, school, location, date
- Single image or graphic (optional but common)
- No nav bar on this slide.

### Slide 2 (optional): Internal title or hook quote
A famous quote in large type, attribution beneath. Should thematically support the contrarian recommendation. Examples by case type:
- M&A: a quote about questioning conventional wisdom (e.g., "If I had asked people what they wanted, they would have said faster horses…" — Henry Ford)
- Strategy: a quote about reframing problems (Drucker, Christensen)
- Marketing: a quote about understanding customers differently
- Social impact: a quote about the limits of obvious solutions
- Policy: a quote about strategic patience or asymmetric response
- Operations: a quote about systems thinking

### Slide 3 (or 2): Table of contents
- All 8 sections + Appendix + Bibliography listed
- Page numbers next to each section
- Sub-sections shown indented under their parent
- Often previews the nav bar visual that will appear on every content slide

### Slide 4 (or 3): Table of abbreviations
- 30–50 acronyms used in the deck, alphabetized
- Two columns: Abbreviation, Explanation
- Compress into a single dense slide

Universal acronyms: EV, NPV, ROI, CAGR, YoY, USD, FY, bn, mm. Industry-specific acronyms vary — see case-type guides for sector-specific lists.

## I. Executive Summary (2 slides)

### Slide 5: Section divider
- Section title: "I. Executive Summary"
- Thematic title underneath (e.g., "The right choice for [actor]")
- Single image or graphic
- Nav bar with this section highlighted

### Slide 6: Executive summary content
The single most important slide in the deck. Layout:

**Top:** The case question as a sub-headline. Then the recommendation in a single sentence as the headline.

**Body — two columns:**

Left column: "Should NOT [obvious answer]"
- 3 bulleted reasons. Each reason is one short sentence stating a specific finding.
- Reason 1: The quantitative problem (overvaluation / poor ROI / wrong segment / insufficient impact / counterproductive policy)
- Reason 2: The strategic/fit problem (synergies / capability / brand fit / theory of change / strategic position)
- Reason 3: The feasibility problem (financing / capability / execution / political / etc.)

Right column: "Should [alternative answer]"
- 3 bulleted reasons. Same structure.
- Reason 1: The quantitative case (fair value / strong ROI / right segment / cost-effective / welfare-positive)
- Reason 2: The strategic fit (products / capability / segment fit / theory of change / strategic position)
- Reason 3: The implementation case (feasible / executable / supported / etc.)

This slide functions as the abstract for the entire deck. Every other slide either supports, qualifies, or implements something said here.

## II. Context / Environment Analysis (slides 7–14, ~6–8 slides)

The context section is a **funnel** from broadest framing to the specific gap that the alternative recommendation fills.

### Slide 7: Section divider
- Title: "II. [Context / Industry / Market / Problem] Analysis"
- Thematic title: a sentence framing the trajectory

### Slides 8–14: Content slides

Adapt to the case type, but the funnel logic is universal:

**Slide ~8: Macro framing.** The big-picture forces shaping this domain. Disruption / growth / decline / shift / crisis. Action title states the headline finding.

**Slide ~9: Quantify the trend.** Specific data showing the trend is real and accelerating. Two charts side by side (typically: trend forecast + revision history showing forecasts have been adjusted upward).

**Slide ~10: The underlying drivers.** Why the trend is structurally winning (economics, technology, demographics, regulation). Two charts.

**Slide ~11: Geographic / segment distribution.** Where the trend is playing out fastest.

**Slide ~12: The relevant sub-context.** Narrow to the geography/segment most relevant to the actors.

**Slide ~13: The preference / behavior shift.** Critical for foreshadowing — show how the relevant audience's preferences are evolving in a direction the obvious answer doesn't fully serve.

**Slide ~14: The seed slide.** The most important slide in the context section. End on a slide that surfaces a specific gap, opportunity, or shift that the *alternative* — but not the obvious answer — addresses.

**The seed slide is non-negotiable.** Without it, the alternative recommendation arrives as a surprise. With it, the reader infers the alternative themselves before it's named.

Examples by case type:
- M&A: "Limited availability of [Product] in the growing and highly profitable [Segment] represents a huge market opportunity" (without naming the alternative target)
- Strategy: "[Specific market segment] has X% CAGR but is underserved by incumbent players"
- Marketing: "[Specific consumer behavior change] is creating demand for [unmet need]"
- Social impact: "Direct aid in [region] has plateaued; the binding constraint has shifted to [institutional / structural / behavioral] factors"
- Policy: "[Country's] [policy lever X] has more leverage than [obvious lever Y] given the current diplomatic posture"
- Operations: "[Specific cost / waste / bottleneck] is the primary value leakage and is invisible in current ops metrics"

## III. Actors / Stakeholders (slides 15–30, ~14–18 slides)

Symmetric coverage of the players involved in the case.

### Slide 15: Section divider
Multi-part section, so the divider also shows the actors covered (with sub-section progress tracker).

### Sub-section structure

Each major actor gets ~5 slides in the same order — symmetry is essential because the reader pattern-matches:

1. **Overview** — what this actor is, scale, market position, business model
2. **Performance / state** — trajectory: financials / scale / momentum / capabilities
3. **Response to the relevant trend** — how this actor is positioned for the context section's trend
4. **Strengths / capabilities** — where this actor is uniquely well-positioned
5. **Challenges / gaps** — where this actor is weak (sets up the case-specific argument)

After the major actors, a final sub-section on **Strategic Fit / Alignment**:
- Strategic fit grid (dimensions of fit × verdict per dimension)
- Deep dive on the strongest fit dimension (sets up the analytical section)

### Case-type specifics for "Actor"

| Case type | Primary actors | What "performance" means | What "strategic fit" assesses |
|---|---|---|---|
| M&A | Acquirer + Target | Revenue, margin, market cap trajectories | Product / brand / tech / sales / geography fit |
| Strategy | Company + Competitors | Market share, growth, profitability | Capability gaps, competitive intensity |
| Marketing | Customer segment + Competitors + Brand | Customer behavior, brand equity, share of voice | Customer-need / brand-positioning fit |
| Social impact | Beneficiaries + Implementers + Funders | Outcomes achieved, scale, cost per outcome | Theory-of-change alignment |
| Policy | Countries + Blocs + Domestic groups | Capability, alignment, posture | Strategic interest alignment |
| Operations | Customer + Operations + Suppliers | Service level, cost, capacity, supplier dependence | End-to-end value chain alignment |

## IV. Quantitative Analysis (slides 31–50, ~17–22 slides)

The longest section. Almost always has three sub-sections, though what those sub-sections are varies by case type. The output is always the **moneyshot** — the central quantitative claim.

### Slide 31: Section divider
Shows three sub-sections appropriate to the case type.

### Three-sub-section structure (case-type specific)

| Case type | Sub-section A | Sub-section B | Sub-section C |
|---|---|---|---|
| M&A | Comparables | DCF | Synergies |
| Strategy | Market sizing (TAM/SAM/SOM) | Option NPV | Competitive response |
| Marketing | Customer LTV | Channel ROAS | Brand equity / awareness |
| Social impact | Cost-effectiveness | SROI / outcomes | Sustainability |
| Policy | Welfare economics | Strategic impact | Implementation cost |
| Operations | Cost-benefit | Throughput / capacity | Change cost / risk |

### Slide 32 (optional): Methodology roadmap

Before diving into the sub-sections, a single slide showing the entire analytical methodology. Boxes for each sub-section with arrows showing how they combine to produce the moneyshot.

Reduces cognitive load for the reader. Recommended.

### Sub-section structure (per sub-section)

Each sub-section is typically 5–7 slides:

1. **Sub-section progress tracker** (1 slide)
2. **Candidate universe** (1 slide) — the candidate set being analyzed (peer companies / market segments / customer cohorts / intervention types / policy options)
3. **MECE filter** (1 slide) — eliminate candidates using shared criteria, retain the relevant ones
4. **Application** (2–3 slides) — apply the methodology to the retained candidates / data
5. **Sensitivity / scenario / validation** (1–2 slides) — show the result is robust, with limitations acknowledged

### THE MONEYSHOT (slide ~50)

The visual climax of the entire deck. Single slide showing the central quantitative claim.

Format: a **waterfall chart** showing how the team got to the central number. Left stack: built-up value of the recommended path. Right stack: built-up cost / value-destruction of the rejected path. Gap between them = the moneyshot number.

Bottom of slide, in large bold type: the central claim.

Examples by case type:
- M&A: "If GM acquires Tesla, value destruction equals USD 26.2bn"
- Strategy: "Entering Vietnam vs China captures $4.2bn more NPV over 7 years"
- Marketing: "Repositioning to segment Y vs X delivers 31% higher CLV at 18% lower CAC"
- Social impact: "Institution-building reaches 3.2M people at $11/beneficiary vs. direct-aid at $84/beneficiary"
- Policy: "Targeted sanctions on sector Z reduce adversary GDP by 2.1% while imposing only 0.3% on our economy"
- Operations: "Distributed re-architecture saves $340M annually while improving service level from 92% to 98%"

The moneyshot number should be **the largest text on the slide**. Strong color (red, orange, gold). Visual focal point.

## V. Feasibility (slides 51–58, ~5–8 slides)

Show the obvious answer can't work even if the quantitative analysis were favorable. This section argues against the deal/strategy/intervention/policy from the *non-financial / non-quantitative* angle.

### Slide 51: Section divider

### Slides 52–57: Content slides

The feasibility critique varies by case type:

| Case type | Standard feasibility dimensions |
|---|---|
| M&A | Cash/equity/debt financing infeasibility, governance (ownership concentration, voting rights, founder control), regulatory/antitrust, cultural/HR integration |
| Strategy | Capability gap to execute, competitive response (game theory), regulatory, time-to-impact |
| Marketing | Brand fit / brand stretch limits, channel readiness, organizational capacity, measurement gaps |
| Social impact | Institutional capacity, political support, sustainability after pilot, unintended consequences |
| Policy | Political feasibility (legislative, coalition), diplomatic cost, retaliation risk, implementation infrastructure |
| Operations | Change management readiness, capability gaps, supplier transition cost, technology readiness |

Common slide types in this section:
- Multi-scenario infeasibility (each scenario shown as a column with why it fails)
- Risks grid (dimensions × threat level)
- Stakeholder reaction map (key actors × likely response × influence on outcome)

### Slide 58: Section closer — "Conclusively…"

Three-column synthesis:
- Column 1: The quantitative case ("Would destroy $X" / "Yields X% lower return")
- Column 2: The feasibility issues ("Several challenges fundamentally question the feasibility")
- Column 3: The verdict ("X should NOT do Y")

This is the deck's first synthesis moment. Lock in the negative verdict before pivoting to the alternative.

## VI. Recommended Alternative (slides 59–70, ~7–12 slides)

The alternative gets the same analytical rigor as the rejection.

### Slide 59: Section divider
Often shows three sub-sections:
1. Evaluating Alternatives
2. Deep Dive on Chosen Alternative
3. Implementation / Financial Feasibility

### Sub-section A: Evaluating alternatives (slides 60–63, ~4 slides)

**Slide 60: Strategic options.** Three-column comparison of broad option types. E.g., M&A: Develop in-house / Acquire / Partner. Strategy: Aggressive / Selective / Defer. Marketing: Repositioning / New segment / New product. Social: Direct aid / Institutional / Behavioral. Policy: Unilateral / Coalition / Negotiation. Operations: Optimize / Redesign / Outsource.

**Slide 61: Build/Buy/Partner (or analog).** Three-column path comparison with feasibility for each.

**Slides 62–63: Candidate option shortlist.** 4–8 candidates × 3–5 criteria + verdict column. All but one rejected. The chosen alternative wins on most criteria.

### Sub-section B: Deep Dive on Chosen Alternative (slides 64–66, ~3 slides)

**Slide 64: Why this option.** Three sub-sections showing fit with the acquirer/decision-maker. **Critical:** include 2–4 third-party expert quotes from named sources (analysts, journalists, industry publications, NGO researchers, academic experts, depending on case type). This positions the recommendation as something the field already sees.

**Slide 65: Asset / capability / approach base.** Visual map of what the chosen alternative brings.

**Slide 66: Value model / business model / theory of change.** How the alternative creates value or impact.

### Sub-section C: Implementation feasibility (slides 67–70, ~3–4 slides)

**Slide 67: Quantitative validation of the alternative.** The full analytical method applied to the alternative. Same template as the analysis of the rejected option, in mirror.

**Slide 68: The football field / value-curve / outcome distribution for the alternative.** Same template as the rejected option's; usually shows fair valuation or attractive returns.

**Slide 69 (optional): Pro forma / integrated state.** What does the post-decision state look like?

**Slide 70: Implementation plan.** How the recommended alternative gets executed. Phasing, key milestones, risks, mitigations.

---

## VII. Conclusion (slides 71–72)

### Slide 71: Section divider
- Title: "VII. Conclusion"
- Sub-title: A short thematic statement ("Sometimes it is simply not meant to be" / "Therefore…" / "The path forward")

### Slide 72: The synthesis slide
The deck's second and final synthesis moment. Common formats:

**Q&A format:** Headline = the final recommendation. Body = the case question + sub-questions in rows. Each row has bulleted answers from the section.

**Three-card format:** Headline + three cards horizontally covering the rationale (e.g., Quantitative / Strategic / Implementation).

**Section-recap format:** Headline + numbered list with one short bullet per major section.

No new content on the conclusion slide. Pure recap.

---

## Appendix (slides 73–90, ~15–18 slides)

The appendix is defensive depth. Every quantitative claim in the main deck should have an appendix backup.

### Slide 73: Appendix divider

### Slides 74–75: Appendix table of contents
Two slides showing what's in the appendix, organized into:
- Referenced deep dives (in main-deck order)
- Additional analyses (supplementary)

### Slides 76–90: Deep dive slides

Typical appendix content (varies by case type):
- Full models (DCF, market sizing, customer LTV, cost-effectiveness, welfare analysis, throughput model)
- Methodology choices (why specific peer groups, why specific scenario parameters, why specific benchmarks)
- Sub-segment / sub-group analyses
- Sensitivity tables
- Counter-argument analyses (why each alternative-to-our-alternative was rejected)
- Comparative analyses (relevant precedent cases, similar interventions, historical analogs)
- Stakeholder mapping
- Risk register

Each appendix slide is labeled in its corner with what main-deck slide it supports.

## Bibliography (slides 91–100, ~5–13 slides)

### Slide 91: Bibliography divider

### Slides 92–100: Source list slides

Harvard-style references. Format:
*Author, Initial. (Year) Title. Publication [online]. Available at: URL (Accessed: Date)*

Dense text slides — 15–25 references per slide. Alphabetical by author surname.

Include every source cited in any content slide. Citation discipline matters.

## Section length guidance

The total target is typically 100 slides (some case formats specify different limits). Suggested allocation:

| Section | Min slides | Max slides | Typical |
|---|---|---|---|
| Front matter | 3 | 5 | 4 |
| Executive Summary | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Context / Environment | 6 | 8 | 7 |
| Actors / Stakeholders | 14 | 18 | 16 |
| Quantitative Analysis | 17 | 22 | 20 |
| Feasibility | 5 | 8 | 7 |
| Recommended Alternative | 7 | 18 | 12 |
| Conclusion | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Appendix | 15 | 30 | 20 |
| Bibliography | 5 | 13 | 10 |
| **Total** | **75** | **126** | **100** |

### Compression guidance for shorter formats

If the case allows fewer slides (e.g., 30 slides for a consulting case), compress in this order:

1. **Cut the appendix first** — shrink from 20 slides down to 3–5 (or remove entirely; keep only critical backup slides)
2. **Compress the alternative section** — combine Evaluating Alternatives + Deep Dive into 3–4 slides
3. **Compress context section** — 3 slides instead of 7
4. **Compress actor analysis** — 2–3 slides per actor instead of 5
5. **Compress quantitative analysis** — 1 slide per sub-section type instead of 5–7

**Never cut:** executive summary, the moneyshot, the recommendation synthesis, the football field / value curve, section dividers, the bibliography.

### Expansion guidance for unconstrained formats

If the format allows more than 100 slides (or your case has multiple alternatives to analyze):
- Add multiple alternative deep dives if relevant
- Expand the appendix
- Add a hook quote slide
- Add a methodology roadmap

Don't pad the analytical section just to fill space. Every slide must earn its place.

# Case Type: Marketing

Marketing cases involve recommending a course of action around brand, campaign, positioning, customer acquisition, segmentation, or go-to-market — typically for a product, service, or brand.

**Calibration note:** Marketing patterns described here are extrapolated from the meta-playbook + standard marketing frameworks, not directly observed from a marketing corpus. Treat as plausible starting points; adjust to the specific case and audience.

---

## When this case type applies

**Typical prompts:**
- "Design a campaign for [product launch]"
- "How should [brand] reposition?"
- "Should [company] rebrand?"
- "What's the go-to-market strategy for [new product]?"
- "How should X reduce CAC / improve LTV?"
- "Which segment should X target?"
- "Should X enter [channel: D2C / retail / digital]?"
- "How should X respond to [competitor's marketing move]?"

**Audience:** CMO, brand team, marketing leadership. Expects customer-centric thinking, channel literacy, and ROI/ROAS discipline. Less banker-flex than M&A — visual sophistication and consumer insight matter more.

**Typical deliverable:** 30–60 slides. Visual quality matters more than in M&A — the deck should *look* like marketing.

## Section content adaptation

### II. Context = Consumer + Competitive Landscape

Funnel from broad market context to the specific customer insight:
- Category dynamics (growth, maturity, decline)
- Consumer behavior shifts (changing preferences, generational, post-pandemic)
- Channel evolution (D2C, social commerce, retail media, programmatic)
- Competitive set positioning
- The specific unmet need / under-served segment (foreshadow)

### III. Actors = Customer + Brand + Competitors

Standard sub-sections:
1. **The Brand** — Current positioning, equity, share, customer base
2. **Customer Segments** — Who buys today, who could buy, segment economics, jobs-to-be-done
3. **Competitors** — Direct and indirect, their positioning, share of voice, share of wallet
4. **Customer Journey** — Awareness → Consideration → Purchase → Loyalty, with conversion rates and pain points at each stage
5. **Brand-Customer Fit** — Where the brand wins / loses / could win across segments

### IV. Quantitative Analysis = Customer LTV + ROAS + Brand Metrics

**A. Customer LTV (Lifetime Value):**
- Segment-by-segment LTV calculation: ARPU × retention × gross margin × duration
- Cohort analysis over 12–36 months
- LTV / CAC ratio for each segment
- Best segments emerge

**B. Channel ROAS (Return on Ad Spend):**
- Spend allocation across channels (Paid social / SEM / Display / Retail media / Influencer / OOH / TV)
- ROAS per channel (historical or modeled)
- Marginal ROAS analysis (where the next dollar should go)
- Cost-per-acquisition / cost-per-click by channel

**C. Brand Metrics:**
- Awareness (unaided, aided), Consideration, Preference, Purchase intent
- Net Promoter Score (NPS), brand health tracking
- Share of voice vs share of market
- Brand equity components (relevance, differentiation, esteem, knowledge)

### V. Feasibility = Brand Fit + Execution Capacity + Measurement

Standard sub-topics:
1. **Brand-stretch limits** — Can the brand credibly extend to this positioning / segment / category?
2. **Channel capability** — Does the team have the operational depth in the chosen channels?
3. **Creative production capacity** — Can the team produce content at the required velocity / volume?
4. **Measurement and attribution** — Can the team measure success accurately? Multi-touch attribution, MMM, holdout testing?
5. **Organizational readiness** — Are sales / customer service / supply chain ready?

### VI. Recommended Alternative = Alternative Segment / Positioning / Campaign

Standard sub-sections:
1. **Evaluating alternatives** — 3-path framework (Repositioning / New segment / New product extension) + 4–8 specific option shortlist
2. **Deep dive on chosen approach** — Target segment, positioning statement, message hierarchy, creative direction, with 2–4 expert validations
3. **Implementation plan** — Campaign architecture, channel mix, phased launch, KPIs, test-and-learn structure

## Case-type-specific archetypes

### Customer Journey Map
Horizontal flow: Awareness → Consideration → Purchase → Loyalty → Advocacy. Each stage shows: Customer state, Touchpoints, Pain points, Opportunities. Sized boxes for conversion rates.

### Segmentation 2×2 (Positioning Map)
2×2 grid with axes that matter (e.g., Premium vs Mass × Convenience vs Quality). Competitors plotted as bubbles sized by share. White space = opportunity.

### Persona Cards
Side-by-side persona profiles: demographics, psychographics, jobs-to-be-done, pain points, channel preferences, message resonance. 3–5 personas with the target persona highlighted.

### LTV / CAC Curves
Two-line chart: cumulative revenue per customer (LTV) vs cumulative cost (CAC) over time. Payback period as the crossover. Compared across segments.

### Channel ROAS Comparison
Horizontal bar chart: each channel with current ROAS + marginal ROAS. The recommendation shifts spend from low-marginal-ROAS to high-marginal-ROAS channels.

### Marketing Mix Model Output
Waterfall showing each channel's contribution to total revenue, with paid vs organic decomposed.

### Brand Equity Pyramid (Keller, BrandAsset Valuator)
Pyramid layers: Salience → Performance → Imagery → Judgments → Feelings → Resonance. Show where brand is strong/weak.

### Message Hierarchy
Pyramid: Master brand promise (top) → Reasons to believe (middle layer) → Proof points (bottom). Ensures campaign consistency.

### Creative Mood Board / Concept Boards
For campaign or rebrand cases: visual reference for creative direction. Tone, color palette, typography, imagery style.

## The Marketing Moneyshot

Format varies — marketing moneyshots are often about efficiency improvement or growth uplift.

**Common formats:**
- **LTV/CAC waterfall** — Current LTV/CAC ratio + uplifts from recommendation = new LTV/CAC ratio
- **Channel reallocation impact** — Current channel mix revenue vs reallocated channel mix revenue
- **Segment-shift impact** — Current segment LTV × volume vs new segment LTV × volume

Bottom of slide, large bold type:
*"Repositioning to [segment Y] vs [segment X] delivers Y% higher LTV at Z% lower CAC"*
or
*"Reallocating spend to [channels] generates $X bn incremental revenue at the same budget"*
or
*"[New campaign approach] improves marketing efficiency from X to Y over 18 months"*

## Common frameworks used in marketing cases

- **4 P's** — Product / Price / Place / Promotion
- **7 P's** — adds People / Process / Physical evidence (services marketing)
- **STP** — Segmentation / Targeting / Positioning
- **AIDA** — Attention / Interest / Desire / Action (funnel)
- **AARRR (Pirate metrics)** — Acquisition / Activation / Retention / Referral / Revenue (growth marketing)
- **Customer Lifetime Value (CLV/LTV)** — economic value over the customer relationship
- **CAC / LTV ratio** — sustainability of acquisition
- **Jobs to be Done (JTBD)** — what customers are "hiring" the product to do
- **Brand Equity Pyramid (Keller / Aaker)** — building brand resonance
- **Customer Journey Mapping** — touchpoint analysis
- **Marketing Mix Modeling (MMM)** — econometric attribution across channels
- **Multi-Touch Attribution (MTA)** — digital attribution
- **NPS / CSAT** — customer satisfaction metrics

Beyond the universal ones:

LTV, CLV, CAC, ROAS, ROI, CPM, CPC, CPA, CPL, CPV, CTR, CR, CR2 (consideration to purchase), MAU/DAU, AOV (average order value), ARPU, NPS, CSAT, CES (customer effort score), MMM, MTA, MQL, SQL, SAL (sales accepted lead), BANT, ICP (ideal customer profile), ABM (account-based marketing), B2B, B2C, D2C, DTC, OOH, OTT, SEM, SEO, PPC, KPI, SOV (share of voice), SOM (share of market), GMV, OKR, JTBD.

## Example contrarian recommendation patterns

| Case framing | Obvious answer | Contrarian alternative |
|---|---|---|
| "How should X compete with [Y]?" | Match on features and price | Reposition around identity / different need |
| "Target millennials" | Yes, here's the millennial strategy | Target the overlooked Gen Z + Gen X segments instead |
| "Should X go D2C?" | Yes, here's the D2C plan | Build hybrid (D2C + selective retail) for capability and capital efficiency |
| "Rebrand to look more modern" | Yes, here's the rebrand | Don't rebrand visually — reposition the existing brand around an emerging value |
| "Maximize digital spend" | Yes, more programmatic | Cut paid programmatic, invest in earned media / community / partnerships |
| "Compete on price" | Yes, price match | Move upmarket, abandon competing on price |

**Pattern:** The contrarian recognizes that the obvious answer leaves customer LTV on the table, addresses a symptom not a cause, or fights on terms that disadvantage the brand.

## Common marketing failure modes

1. **Demographics-only segmentation** — segmenting by age/gender/income only, missing behavioral / needs-based segmentation
2. **Generic positioning** — "premium quality" / "trusted brand" — language that could apply to any brand
3. **Channel-list without ROAS** — listing channels to use without showing why those specifically
4. **No customer voice** — recommendation built from analyst data only, no quotes / research / qualitative insight
5. **No measurement plan** — recommending a campaign without specifying how success gets measured
6. **Brand stretch ignored** — proposing a positioning the brand can't credibly inhabit
7. **No competitive response** — assuming competitors stay still
8. **One-shot thinking** — single-campaign recommendation rather than program / always-on architecture

## Adapting deck length

- **15-slide pitch:** Front (1) + Exec (2) + Context (2) + Customer (2) + Quantitative (3) + Alternative (3) + Conclusion (1) + Appendix (1)
- **30-slide case:** Front (3) + Exec (2) + Context (4) + Actors (5) + Quantitative (6) + Feasibility (3) + Alternative (5) + Conclusion (1) + Appendix (1)
- **60-slide full deck:** Use the full 8-section template with marketing-specific archetypes

Always keep: positioning statement, customer insight, the moneyshot equivalent (efficiency or growth uplift), the recommendation synthesis.

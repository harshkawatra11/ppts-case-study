# Case Type: Operations

Operations cases involve recommending changes to supply chain, manufacturing, service delivery, process design, or operational restructuring — typically for cost reduction, throughput improvement, service level uplift, or capability transformation.

**Calibration note:** Operations patterns described here are extrapolated from the meta-playbook + standard operations frameworks (lean, Six Sigma, theory of constraints, supply chain management), not directly observed from an operations corpus. Treat as plausible starting points; adjust to the specific case and audience.

---

## When this case type applies

**Typical prompts:**
- "How should X restructure its supply chain?"
- "Redesign [process] to reduce cost / increase throughput / improve service level"
- "Should X consolidate / distribute / outsource [operations]?"
- "How should X respond to [supply chain disruption / capacity constraint]?"
- "Evaluate [operational change] for [company]"
- "Design the operating model for [new business / new geography]"
- "Improve [specific operational metric: OTIF, inventory turns, lead time, defect rate]"
- "Should X invest in [automation / digitization / new technology]?"

**Audience:** COO, VP Operations, plant managers, supply chain leadership, manufacturing executives. Expects systems thinking, quantified throughput / cost / quality analysis, change management awareness.

**Typical deliverable:** 30–60 slides with heavy emphasis on process diagrams, capacity analysis, and quantified improvement.

## Section content adaptation

### II. Context = Industry + Value Chain Analysis

Funnel from broad context to the specific operational opportunity:
- Industry dynamics affecting operations (demand volatility, supply disruption, regulation)
- Value chain mapping (where value is created, where leakage occurs)
- Competitive operations benchmarks (what best-in-class looks like)
- Technology trends (automation, AI, digital, sustainability)
- The specific operational gap the alternative addresses (foreshadow)

### III. Actors = Customers + Operations + Suppliers + Workforce

Standard sub-sections:
1. **Customers** — Demand patterns, service expectations, order profiles, segmentation
2. **Operations** — Current state, capacity, throughput, cost structure, performance metrics
3. **Suppliers** — Dependence, concentration, geographic distribution, performance
4. **Workforce** — Skills, productivity, capacity, change readiness
5. **Operational Fit / Capability Assessment** — Where the current operating model misaligns with strategy or customer needs

### IV. Quantitative Analysis = Cost-Benefit + Throughput + Change Cost

**A. Cost-Benefit Analysis:**
- Current state cost structure (variable, fixed, by function, by stage)
- Future state cost structure under recommended option
- Net savings with timing (year 1, 2, 3+)
- Investment required (CapEx, OpEx, change cost)
- ROI / IRR / payback period

**B. Throughput / Capacity Analysis:**
- Current bottlenecks (Theory of Constraints)
- Capacity utilization by stage
- Future state throughput potential
- Service level / OTIF / lead time projections
- Little's Law for inventory + lead time relationships

**C. Change Cost Analysis:**
- Implementation cost: technology, training, consulting, downtime, transition
- Risk-adjusted change cost (likelihood × cost of failure scenarios)
- Time-to-value: when do benefits start exceeding costs?

### V. Feasibility = Change Management + Capability + Supplier Transition

Standard sub-topics:
1. **Change management readiness** — Organization's track record with similar changes, leadership commitment, workforce buy-in
2. **Capability gaps** — Skills, technology, data, infrastructure needed to operate the new model
3. **Supplier transition costs and risk** — Switching costs, supplier concentration, geopolitical risk
4. **Technology readiness** — Is the required tech mature? Vendor risk?
5. **Customer transition impact** — Service disruption during change, customer-facing changes
6. **Regulatory / compliance** — Safety, environmental, labor, quality regulations
7. **Reversibility** — Can the change be reversed if it fails?

### VI. Recommended Alternative = Alternative Operational Design

Standard sub-sections:
1. **Evaluating alternatives** — 3-path framework (Optimize current / Redesign / Outsource) + 4–8 specific design shortlist (centralized vs distributed; automated vs manual; vertical vs platform; etc.)
2. **Deep dive on chosen design** — Operating model, capability model, technology stack, with 2–4 expert validations (industry analysts, technology assessors, named customer/supplier feedback)
3. **Implementation plan** — Phased transformation roadmap, milestone metrics, change management plan, risk mitigations

## Case-type-specific archetypes

### Value Stream Map (VSM)
End-to-end process flow showing each step with: cycle time, lead time, inventory, quality rate, value-add vs non-value-add classification. Current state and future state versions to show improvement.

### Process Flow Diagram
Sequential boxes representing process steps with arrows showing flow, branches for decision points, swimlanes for actors / departments.

### Throughput vs Capacity Curves
Line chart: cumulative throughput (Y axis) vs time/scale (X axis). Multiple lines for current / improved / target. Identifies bottlenecks and capacity headroom.

### Cost-to-Serve Waterfall
Waterfall showing total cost decomposed by stage / category / driver. Identifies the biggest cost components and where reductions are achievable.

### Network Topology Diagram
For supply chain cases: map of facilities, distribution centers, suppliers, customer flows. Current state and recommended future state.

### OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness) Decomposition
Manufacturing-specific: OEE = Availability × Performance × Quality. Each component's current and target state with improvement levers.

### Pareto Chart (80/20 Analysis)
Vertical bars showing contribution of each cause/SKU/customer to total problem/revenue/cost, with cumulative line. Identifies the vital few from the trivial many.

### Spaghetti / Heat Map Diagram
For facility / process layout cases: visualization of movement patterns / activity density showing where inefficiencies cluster.

### Capability Maturity Matrix
Grid: capability dimensions (rows) × maturity level (columns: 1=ad hoc / 2=defined / 3=managed / 4=optimized / 5=continuous). Current state and target state shown. Identifies investment priorities.

### Change Roadmap (Gantt-style)
Horizontal timeline showing parallel workstreams, milestones, dependencies, go-live dates. Includes business impact ramp-up.

### Theory of Constraints Diagram
Identifying the system's primary bottleneck. Showing how throughput is gated by one constraint and how the recommendation elevates the constraint.

## The Operations Moneyshot

**Common formats:**
- **Cost savings waterfall** — Current cost stack with each savings driver decomposed, ending at new cost
- **Throughput uplift comparison** — Current state throughput / capacity vs future state, with the gap = uplift
- **Service level improvement curve** — Current OTIF / fill rate vs target, with timeline to achievement
- **ROI / payback chart** — Cumulative savings vs cumulative investment over time, with break-even point

Bottom of slide, large bold type:
*"Distributed network redesign saves $340M annually while improving service level from 92% to 98%"*
or
*"Process automation reduces cost-to-serve by 27% with 14-month payback"*
or
*"Supply chain restructuring releases $X bn working capital and reduces lead time from 45 to 12 days"*

## Common frameworks used in operations cases

- **Lean / Toyota Production System** — Eliminate waste (muda), pull production, kaizen, jidoka
- **Six Sigma** — DMAIC (Define / Measure / Analyze / Improve / Control), DPMO, statistical process control
- **Theory of Constraints (Goldratt)** — Identify constraint, exploit it, subordinate, elevate, repeat
- **Throughput Accounting** — Throughput, Inventory, Operating Expense
- **Value Stream Mapping** — End-to-end process visualization
- **Little's Law** — L = λW (inventory = arrival rate × time in system)
- **Queueing Theory** — When and how queues form, capacity vs demand variability
- **SCOR Model** — Supply Chain Operations Reference (Plan / Source / Make / Deliver / Return)
- **APICS / CPIM** — Production and inventory management
- **OEE** — Overall Equipment Effectiveness
- **5S** — Sort / Set in order / Shine / Standardize / Sustain
- **Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)** — Full lifecycle cost beyond purchase price
- **Bullwhip Effect** — Demand amplification up the supply chain
- **Cost-to-Serve Analysis** — Customer-specific profitability
- **Activity-Based Costing (ABC)** — Cost allocation by activity

## Example contrarian recommendation patterns

| Case framing | Obvious answer | Contrarian alternative |
|---|---|---|
| "Optimize the supply chain" | Tune the current network | Redesign for distributed / digital topology |
| "Reduce manufacturing cost" | Lean / Six Sigma improvements | Platform-based modular redesign that eliminates the cost rather than reducing it |
| "Improve service level" | Add capacity / inventory | Redesign demand-side variability (postponement, lead-time pricing, customer collaboration) |
| "Should we outsource [function]?" | Yes, RFP and select vendor | Build internal capability + outsource the commodified part only |
| "Centralize operations to save cost" | Yes, consolidate | Distribute closer to customers; centralize what truly benefits from scale |
| "Invest in automation" | Yes, replace labor with robots | Augment labor + selectively automate the high-variance / dangerous / repetitive tasks |
| "Solve the bottleneck at [step]" | Add capacity at that step | Reduce demand on that step / redesign process to bypass it |
| "Reduce inventory" | Tighter forecasting | Reduce lead time, increase responsiveness — different theory of inventory |

**Pattern:** The contrarian recognizes that the obvious answer optimizes the current paradigm when the situation calls for a paradigm shift, or fixes a symptom (high cost / low throughput) by attacking the wrong root cause.

## Common operations failure modes

1. **No baseline measurement** — recommending improvements without quantifying current state rigorously
2. **Local optimization** — improving one stage without considering systemic effect
3. **Ignoring variability** — modeling means without standard deviations / distributions
4. **Capacity-only thinking** — adding capacity when the issue is variability, mix, or scheduling
5. **Technology-first thinking** — recommending automation / AI / digital without process redesign first
6. **Ignoring change management** — assuming the operational change happens automatically once specified
7. **No supplier / customer integration** — recommending changes that require partner cooperation without their involvement
8. **No risk modeling** — single-point estimates without disruption scenarios
9. **Sustainability / environmental ignored** — operational decisions with environmental footprint not assessed
10. **Service vs cost tradeoff hidden** — claiming improvements on all dimensions without acknowledging tradeoffs

- **15-slide pitch:** Front (1) + Exec (2) + Context (2) + Current state (2) + Quantitative (3) + Alternative (3) + Conclusion (1) + Appendix (1)
- **30-slide case:** Full 8-section structure compressed

A delivery partner is paid roughly the same to carry a ₹79 thali across
Pune as to carry a ₹700 family dinner across Bengaluru. That fact is the
whole case. Everything below is context.
In August 2025, Swiggy launched Toing, a standalone app selling
meals priced between ₹49 and ₹150 to students and young earners,
starting in Pune. The logic was simple enough. College students order
food often, just not at ₹700. Compact menus, low prices and volume
were supposed to make it work for restaurants and for the platform.
The trouble is arithmetic. Swiggy's core food delivery business runs at
an average order value of about ₹700 and, as of its most recent
shareholder letter, an adjusted EBITDA margin of roughly 3.1% of gross
order value. Last-mile delivery does not get cheaper because the
basket is small. Rider payouts and incentives, insurance and payment
processing are largely fixed per order. At a ₹700 basket, Swiggy's take
covers them with a thin margin to spare. At ₹99, it does not come
close, and every additional order widens the loss unless something
about the model changes.
Swiggy has been here before. In January 2025 it launched Snacc, a
separate app promising snacks and quick bites in 10 to 15 minutes,
starting in Bengaluru. Snacc was wound down within about a year. The
parallel is not exact, and you should not pretend it is: Snacc was a
speed bet, Toing is a price bet. But both were separate apps looking
for economics that the main app could not provide. Whether Toing is
the same mistake a second time or a different wager altogether is a
question you will need to take a position on.
Toing: Swiggy's ₹99 problem
The Situation

<!-- page 3 -->
Across the street, Eternal (formerly Zomato), the market leader, has not
launched a Toing rival. It has been in this neighbourhood before.
Zomato Everyday, its ₹89 home-style meals service, and Zomato Quick,
its 15-minute delivery service, were both shut in May 2025, with the
company saying it could not see either turning profitable without
compromising the customer experience. Since then, Eternal has
watched. Is that discipline, or is it handing a generation of first-time
food delivery customers to Swiggy?
The cost line, meanwhile, is not getting easier. The Code on Social
Security, in force since 21 November 2025, requires aggregators to
contribute 1 to 2% of annual turnover to gig-worker welfare, capped at
5% of what they pay those workers, and Karnataka has legislated a per-
transaction welfare fee of its own. Swiggy's public shareholders, who
bought into the company at its November 2024 listing on the promise
of a path to profitability, have their own arithmetic too.
You are the internal strategy team at Swiggy. The Board wants one
recommendation on Toing, with the numbers behind it:
1. Scale it Grow Toing as a business in its own right, with a credible
path to contribution breakeven.
2. Run it as a funnel. Accept that Toing loses money on every order,
and justify that as a customer acquisition channel for the core app
(and Instamart), measured against what Swiggy would otherwise
spend to acquire the same users.
3. Kill it. Shut Toing as Snacc was shut, and say what, if anything,
Swiggy should do instead for the under-₹150 customer.
Your mandate

<!-- page 4 -->
You may propose a hybrid or a fourth path. You must still commit to one
primary recommendation and be ready to explain why the others are
worse.
Whatever you recommend, the Board expects to see:
Per-order unit economics for a Toing order today. What the
customer pays, what the restaurant keeps, what the delivery costs,
what Swiggy is left with. State every assumption and where it came
from.
The levers that change that arithmetic, and by how much.
Batching several orders per trip, campus pickup points, fixed menus
and dedicated kitchens, a small delivery fee or subscription,
advertising, or anything else you can quantify. Be clear about which
levers Swiggy controls and which depend on restaurants or
customers accepting a worse experience.
If you argue for the funnel: what share of Toing users must migrate
to ₹700-type ordering, over what period, for the losses to pay back,
and how that compares with Swiggy's current cost of acquiring a
customer through marketing.
The competitive reading. What your recommendation does to
Swiggy's position against Eternal, and what Eternal's most likely
response is.
What would change your mind. The two or three assumptions
that, if wrong, flip your answer, and how Swiggy would know within
twelve months whether the call was right.

Rules
Public information only: annual reports, shareholder letters, investor
presentations, regulatory filings, credible press, and your own
primary research. Do not approach Swiggy, Eternal or their
employees for non-public information.
Primary research is encouraged. Most of you are Toing's target
customer. Surveys, order sampling and campus interviews are all
fair game, provided you disclose sample size and method.
Assumptions are not penalised. Unstated or unsourced assumptions
are.
Toing is a small business inside a large one. The Board does not
expect you to know its internal metrics. It expects you to estimate
them sensibly and show your work.
Format

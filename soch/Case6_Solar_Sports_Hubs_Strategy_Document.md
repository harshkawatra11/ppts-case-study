# KhelUrja — Solar-Powered Community Sports Hubs
### Case 6: Working Strategy Document + 5-Slide Deck Structure

*Prepared: August 2026 | Geography: India (Tier-2/3 towns and large villages) | All figures in INR unless stated*

> **The one-line thesis:** Public grounds in India don't fail for lack of land or capital — they fail because nobody funds their electricity, their upkeep, or their operator. KhelUrja takes a 20-year concession on grounds that already exist, builds a solar canopy that generates power *above* the play area (zero incremental land), and uses light + bookings + energy to pay for the ground's upkeep forever.

## PART A — PROBLEM ANALYSIS

### A1. Defining the problem precisely

The problem is **not** "there aren't enough sports facilities." India has built a great many. The problem is:

> **Publicly-owned sports grounds in rural and semi-urban India are capital-rich and operating-poor. They receive construction grants but no recurring funding for electricity, lighting, maintenance or staffing — so within 24–36 months of commissioning they become unusable, unlit, unsupervised, and eventually repurposed.**

The binding constraint is the **evening**. An unlit ground offers ~6–7 genuinely playable hours a day (nobody plays at 2 PM in a 42°C Indian summer). A lit ground offers 14–16. Lighting therefore **doubles the productive capacity of the asset without adding a single square foot of land** — and evening hours are simultaneously the hours with the highest willingness-to-pay and the hours with the least reliable rural power.

| **Lender (IREDA/REC/NABARD)** | Bankable, metered, contracted cash flows | A "sports NGO" is unbankable; a metered solar asset is not |

### A4. Root causes (not symptoms)

1. **Capex-only funding architecture.** Grants and CSR build. Nobody endows the ₹15–20 lakh/year it takes to *run* a facility. Decay is structural, not accidental.
2. **No accountable operator with a P&L.** Assets sit with a department, not an owner. CAG audits repeatedly find no handover, no operating scheme, no utilisation record.
3. **Darkness destroys the monetisable hours.** ~70% of Indian sports-facility willingness-to-pay sits in the 6–10 PM window. That window is dark *and* least reliably powered in rural India.
4. **Single-use land in a land-scarce country.** A ground earns from one use for ~7 hours; the vertical space above it, and 17 hours of the clock, are discarded entirely.
5. **A false trade-off between access and revenue.** Everyone assumes a ground must be either free-and-decaying or commercial-and-exclusionary.

> ### The central insight
> **Free community demand and paying commercial demand do not compete for the same hours. They compete only in the absence of light.**
> School PE and community play want 7–9 AM and 3–6 PM. Paying adults want 6–10 PM and weekends. Light is what separates them — and solar is what makes light free.

## PART B — PROPOSED SOLUTION

## B1. Core Value Proposition

**KhelUrja is a 20-year concession operator of community sports hubs, where an elevated solar canopy over the courts converts the ground into a distributed power plant — so that electricity savings, energy sales and evening bookings permanently fund free community access.**

**Positioning statement:**
> *For panchayats and school boards who own grounds they cannot afford to run, KhelUrja is a build-operate-transfer concession that turns the ground into a solar power plant and a community sports club at the same time. Unlike a CSR-built stadium that is handed over and then decays, KhelUrja's own energy savings and evening bookings pay for its upkeep for twenty years — and it hands back an appreciated asset, not a ruin.*

**We are not a turf business. We are not a solar EPC. We are creating a category: *community energy-sports infrastructure*.**

**Value created, by party:**

| For | Value delivered |
|---|---|
| Youth & community | ~5,800 additional asset-hours of **safe, lit, supervised** play per hub per year; ≥60% of scheduled hours free |
| Girls & women | Lit, fenced, staffed, toilet-equipped ground — the four conditions that actually determine parental permission; ≥40% female participation target, ring-fenced women-only slots |
| Panchayat / ULB | Free ₹95-lakh ground upgrade, 5% revenue share, cheaper power via group net metering, asset reverts at Year 20 |
| CSR funder | Two Schedule VII outcomes (cl. vii sports + cl. iv environment) from one rupee, with 20 years of instrumented, audited impact data |
| State sports body | A KIC-compatible, utilisation-guaranteed facility with **zero O&M liability** |
| DISCOM | Distributed generation at the feeder tail + battery discharge during evening peak |
| Lender / investor | A metered, contracted renewable asset with sports cash flows on top — 18–20% project IRR |

## B2. Key Features & Solution Components

### (a) The physical hub — "Standard Hub" (the profit engine)

Deployed on an **existing** public ground of 15,000–20,000 sq ft in a town/large village with ≥40,000 catchment population within 8 km.

| Component | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Solar canopy** | 75 kWp total. 50 kWp elevated canopy over ~5,000 sq ft of hard courts; 25 kWp over gallery, clubhouse roof, parking and perimeter walkway |
| **Play assets** | 1 box-cricket / futsal cage (8,000 sq ft, netted) + 2 pickleball courts + 1 multi-use court (badminton/basketball), all three under canopy |
| **Lighting** | ~5–6 kW glare-controlled LED, 6–8 fixtures on 4–6 poles at 6–8 m, delivering **200–300 lux** (recreational) with uniformity U₀ ≥ 0.6 per EN 12193 |
| **Storage** | **30 kWh LFP** + hybrid PCS + EMS — deliberately right-sized to cover *only* the revenue-generating evening load (5 kW lighting × 4 hrs + 10 kWh buffer) |
| **Grid interface** | On-grid with **Group / Virtual Net Metering** — surplus credited to the panchayat's other connections (street lights, water pumps, school, PHC, anganwadi) |
| **Ancillary** | 6 × 3.3 kW LEV/e-rickshaw charge points under parking canopy; prefab clubhouse (changing rooms, toilets, drinking water, store); SHG-run F&B kiosk; rainwater harvesting off the canopy gutter |
| **Tech** | QR/turnstile access, IoT energy meters + inverter API, CCTV, WhatsApp booking bot, listing on Hudle/Playo/KheloMore |

**Generation:** 75 kWp × ~1,450 kWh/kWp/yr = **~1.09 lakh kWh/year** (conservative 16.5% CUF, allowing for canopy soiling and shading).

### (b) The land & space engineering — "Generate above, play below, store at the edge"

*The case explicitly requires that panel placement maximise generation while minimising land use without compromising usability, safety or accessibility. Our design rules:*

**Rule 1 — Zero incremental land.** A 1 MW ground-mounted plant needs 4–5 acres and ₹8–40 lakh/acre of land. Our 75 kWp uses **zero incremental land** because it is stacked *above* courts, parking and walkways that already exist. Land-use efficiency: ~150 W per sq m of canopy; ~100 sq ft of court surface carries ~1 kW. **Land cost is the largest line item in Indian solar — and we have removed it entirely.** That, not the panel, is the source of our returns.

**Rule 2 — Panels only where a roof is functionally *desirable*, never over ball-flight sports.**
- ✅ **Canopy over:** pickleball, badminton, basketball, box cricket cage, futsal, gallery, parking, walkways, clubhouse roof, perimeter.
- ❌ **Never over:** full-size cricket or football pitches, athletics tracks, volleyball/kabaddi (high ball trajectory, or heritage of open play).
- On these court types the roof is an *upgrade*, not a compromise — it adds shade in 40°C+ heat and rain-proofs the surface, which is precisely why canopy-covered courts are our monsoon revenue hedge.

**Rule 3 — Clearance and structural safety.**
- Clear height **≥ 9 m** where badminton is played (BWF requirement), ≥ 7 m for basketball, ≥ 6 m for pickleball. Clear-span single-slope truss — **no columns inside the field of play or run-off margin**; all columns padded and set outside the run-off.
- Low tilt (10–15°) to cut wind loading; designed to **IS 875 Part 3** for local wind zone; third-party structural proof-check mandatory before commissioning.
- Full under-deck safety mesh beneath modules to arrest glass/hail debris; anti-glare (AR-coated) modules; laminated backsheets.
- Gutters double as **rainwater harvesting** into a storage tank for turf/court washing.

**Rule 4 — Storage at the edge, never in the play zone.** BESS + inverter room in a 10 × 12 ft ventilated, bunded container at the boundary behind the clubhouse, ≥6 m from the field of play, with fire detection and a dedicated access lane. **LFP chemistry chosen partly for thermal stability** (3,000–6,000 cycle life, low thermal runaway risk) — a non-negotiable when the neighbours are children.

**Rule 5 — Accessibility is designed in.** Ramped entry, one accessible toilet, a hard, level surface under canopy suitable for wheelchair basketball and para-badminton, and a reserved weekly para-sport slot in the Access Charter.

| Alternative | Why it fails | Our edge |

> **We don't buy land. We buy time — the ~77% of the clock that public grounds currently throw away.**

## B4. The Access Charter — how commerce subsidises community *without* crowding it out

This is contractually embedded in the concession and is a **termination-triggering covenant** held by the panchayat.

**The 60 : 30 : 10 rule (of scheduled hours, 6 AM – 10 PM):**
- **≥ 60% free** — schools, girls' programmes, para-athletes, community and panchayat use. Occupies mornings (6–9 AM) and weekday afternoons (2–6 PM).
- **≤ 30% commercially sold** — evenings 6–10 PM and weekend blocks.
- **10% subsidised** at ₹20–50 — youth leagues, community tournaments.

**Why this is not a sacrifice:** at planned occupancy we sell **5,476 of 23,360 available asset-hours = 23% of the clock.** We are contractually capped at 30% and don't need it.

> **Commerce uses 23% of the clock. The community gets the rest.**

**Additional binding safeguards:**
- **Ring-fenced Community Fund:** 15% of gross revenue + 100% of energy margin above a defined floor, spent only on coaching, equipment, girls' travel stipends and tournament entry.
- **Hub Council governance:** sarpanch/ward councillor + school head + 2 youth representatives (≥1 woman) + hub manager. Publishes quarterly accounts and utilisation data.
- **Anti-capture clause:** no single commercial buyer may book more than 4 consecutive hours/day; no conversion to a private club; no exclusive membership model. Breach permits the panchayat to terminate and take the asset.
- **Reversion:** all assets transfer to the panchayat at Year 20, free of encumbrance.

## B6. Pricing Strategy

**Principle: price against local disposable income and the local alternative, never against metro benchmarks.**

| Offering | Price | Derivation |
|---|---|---|
| Box cricket / futsal cage — peak | **₹600–800/hr** | Metro turfs charge ₹1,200–2,500/hr. Tier-2/3 WTP is ~40–50% of metro. At ₹700 for a 10-person group = **₹70/head — the price of a movie ticket** |
| Cage — off-peak | ₹300–400/hr | Fills structurally dead hours; a slot sold at 50% beats an empty slot |
| Courts — peak / off-peak | ₹300–400 / ₹150–200 | Indian pickleball benchmarks ₹500–1,500 (metro), ₹400–1,200 (urban clubs) |
| **Youth pass (unlimited off-peak)** | **₹99/month** | Deliberately set **below a monthly mobile recharge** so it is a habit purchase, not a discretionary one |
| Adult membership | ₹500–800/month | |
| Corporate annual pack | ₹60,000/yr | 4 hrs/month + 1 tournament |
| Coaching | ₹500–800/student/month | 50:50 revenue share with coach |
| **Energy to panchayat/ULB** | **₹5.00/unit, flat, 20 years** | Priced **between** the DISCOM feed-in tariff (₹2.20–3.80/unit) and their retail tariff (₹6.50–8.32/unit). Both sides gain; the 20-year flat price is an inflation hedge for the panchayat and the reason they sign |
| EV / LEV charging | ₹10/unit | Public CPOs charge ₹12–18/unit; our marginal cost is ~₹0 |

**Yield management:** dynamic three-tier pricing (peak / shoulder / off-peak); recurring weekly team slots at a 10% discount with advance payment (locked revenue, stickiest customer); deposits on prime-slot group bookings to kill no-shows; direct WhatsApp booking incentivised over aggregator booking to avoid 15–20% platform commission on repeat users.

### D1. Operational feasibility

- **Build time: 12–16 weeks.** Turf: 4–8 weeks (industry standard). Pickleball court: 5–8 weeks (concrete curing is the constraint, 21–28 days). Solar canopy: 8–10 weeks. These run in parallel. Reference: Tata Power Solar commissioned 820 kWp at CCI Mumbai in 100 days.

### Likely Q&A — prepared answers

**Q: What if the panchayat simply doesn't pay for the power?**
Structurally they can't default in cash terms — under Group/Virtual Net Metering the credit is applied directly to their DISCOM bill; we invoice only the residual, and we cap their offtake at 40% of generation. Even at zero panchayat revenue the hub still clears opex and debt.

**Q: Isn't this just a turf business with panels on top?**
No. A turf business pays ₹40,000–1,00,000/month in land rent and cannot serve free hours. We pay zero rent for twenty years — and the price of that rent is the Access Charter. The solar canopy is what makes a panchayat willing to sign it. Remove the solar and the deal doesn't exist.

**Q: Why not put the solar on a rooftop nearby and leave the ground alone?**
Because then you've generated power but you haven't lit the ground, and lighting — not generation — is what doubles the asset's usable hours. Generation alone doesn't create sports access; co-location does.

**Q: Aren't panels over a playing area a safety hazard?**
We never put panels over ball-flight sports. On covered courts the roof is an upgrade, not a compromise: ≥9 m clear span, no columns in the field of play, IS 875-III wind design, under-deck safety mesh, third-party structural proof-check, and ₹1 crore public liability cover.

**Q: Your occupancy looks optimistic for a small town.**
It's materially below the industry norm — urban turfs sell 7–9 hours a day; we model 4.5 on the cage and 3.5 on the courts, and we break even at 66% of that. Site selection carries the risk, which is why catchment population is a hard covenant, not an assumption.

**Q: What happens in Year 21?**
The assets revert to the panchayat, fully depreciated and with a documented O&M history — plus a community that has run the facility's governance council for two decades. That's the point: we're building an exit, not a dependency.

## Objective

Teams will select one business from the list provided by the organisers and analyse its decline
while operating within the original circumstances of its failure. The list of the failed
startups/companies is given at the end of this document.

The objective is not to replace the business with a new model, but to identify how the existing
business could have adapted, responded and survived while retaining its fundamental identity.

## Submission

Teams must submit a 3-slide case deck, excluding the cover slide, thank-you page and appendix. The
appendix is optional.

## Round 1 deliverables

### Slide 1: Executive Summary

Teams must establish the context of the case by presenting:

- **ES1** The Business: What the company did, its original value proposition and core business model.
- **ES2** The Market: Its target customers, market position and key competitors at the time of its
  decline.
- **ES3** The Failure Context: The primary challenge or turning point that contributed to its decline.
- **ES4** The Revival Thesis: A one-line statement of what the company could have done differently
  while retaining its fundamental business identity.

### Slide 2: Why Did It Fail?

Teams must diagnose the failure rather than merely describe it.

The analysis must identify:

- **WF1** Root Cause(s): What fundamentally caused the business to decline?
- **WF2** Critical Decisions: Which strategic, marketing, operational or other decisions accelerated
  the decline?
- **WF3** Failed Assumptions: Which assumptions about customers, markets, technology, competition or
  operations proved incorrect?
- **WF4** The Unavoidable Problem: What specific challenge must the company have confronted to
  survive?
- **WF5** Missed Opportunities: What realistic opportunities existed to respond to this challenge?
- **WF6** Point of Intervention: At what stage could a different decision realistically have changed
  the trajectory?

### Slide 3: What Could Have Been Done Differently?

Teams must present a revival thesis, not a replacement business.

The proposed solution must demonstrate:

- **RV1** The Strategic Intervention: What should the company have done differently?
- **RV2** Problem-Solution Fit: How does the proposed intervention directly address the identified
  root cause?
- **RV3** Continuity: How does the solution preserve the company's fundamental business model,
  identity and core value proposition?
- **RV4** Adaptation: How would the business respond to the same market, technological,
  operational, labour or competitive challenge that contributed to its failure?
- **RV5** Revival Outcome: What would the business realistically look like if the intervention had
  been implemented?
- **RV6** Initial Roadmap: What key steps are required to put the proposed solution into action?
- **RV7** Feasibility: Why could this solution realistically have worked given the business'
  resources and circumstances?

## Round 1 boundary

**BD1** Teams may evolve, reposition, rebrand, modify, optimise or restructure the existing
business. However, teams may not eliminate the central problem, replace the original business with
an unrelated model, or assume circumstances that remove the challenge altogether.

The test is simple: Same business. Same core identity. Same problem. Different responses.

## Appendix: list of failed startups (as given by the organisers)

1. **Doodhwala.** Founders Aakash Agarwal and Ebrahim Akbari. Founded 2015, Bengaluru. Subscription
   milk and daily-essentials delivery, mostly before 7 AM. Operated in Bengaluru, Pune and
   Hyderabad. Shut down 2019; FreshToHome took over its delivery operations.
2. **Stayzilla.** Founded 2005 as Inasra Technologies, rebranded Stayzilla in 2010. Founders
   Yogendra Vasupal, Sachit Singhi, Rupal Yogendra. Chennai. An online travel-tech homestay
   marketplace. Shut down 2017.
3. **Fashionara.** Launched 2012. Founders Arun Sirdeshmukh and Darpan Munjal. Bengaluru. An
   "eMall" for premium and luxury fashion. Shut down May 2016.
4. **Local Banya.** Founded May 2012. Founders Karan Mehrotra, Rashi Choudhary, Amit Naik. Mumbai.
   Mumbai's first major online grocery platform. Operations suspended Oct 2015.
5. **BharatAgri.** Founded April 2017. Founders Siddharth Dialani and Sai Gole. Pune, later also
   Bengaluru. Tech-enabled personalised farm advice. Shut down November 2025.
6. **Koo.** Founded 2019, launched March 2020. Founders Aprameya Radhakrishna and Mayank
   Bidawatka. Bengaluru. A multilingual microblogging platform, an alternative to Twitter/X. Shut
   down July 2024.
7. **Dunzo.** Founders Kabeer Biswas, Ankur Agarwal, Dalvir Suri, Mukund Jha. Bengaluru. Founded
   2014/2015, began as a WhatsApp-based service. A hyperlocal on-demand delivery service for
   groceries, medicines, food, packages and errands within a city. Shut down January 2025.

## Gaps

- The McKinsey "Thinking Inside the Subscription Box" (February 2018) primary page could not be opened directly in this session: one attempt returned a 403 and two further attempts timed out after 60 seconds. All three McKinsey rows (RC09, RC10, RC11) rest on search-engine summaries that agree closely with one another on sample size, survey window and the 38%/62% cancellation figures, but none is a direct document read. A follow-up session with a different fetch method (for example, a cached or mirrored copy) is needed before these figures can be marked VERIFIED.
- The 1010data study itself (its own report or methodology note, as opposed to press coverage of it) was not locatable as a standalone primary document; both press accounts opened in this session (Fast Company, 403; The FERN, paywalled) could not be read directly. RC01 to RC03 rest on search-engine summaries and should be treated with the same caution the case brief already applies to the contrast with HarvestBox's own 34% six-month figure.
- No study or source with company-level, Indian-market retention-by-month data for a meal-kit, meal-prep or similar subscription box was found; the one named Indian academic paper located (RC07) covers adoption drivers, not retention or churn.
- C3 (pause/skip effects on churn and order frequency) returned only subscription-commerce vendor blogs with no named underlying study, sample size or methodology for any of the specific percentages found (RC13 to RC15). No company-disclosed or academic figure on pause/skip effects specifically in the meal-kit or food-subscription category was found.
- C4 and C6 returned only agency or vendor blog pages with India-specific figures; no RBI, NASSCOM, IAMAI, Redseer, Bain or other named-institution report with an Indian D2C CAC, LTV:CAC or conversion benchmark was found or opened in this session. These rows are recorded as directional, not as verified benchmarks, consistent with their [blog] flag.
- C5's academic primary source (Schmitt, Skiera and Van den Bulte 2011) is now solidly verified (RC20 to RC26), but it is a single European retail-banking study; no second industry (especially no DTC subscription or meal-kit) referral study was found to corroborate or extend its figures to this case's category.

- **Blue Apron six-month retention: Bloomberg Second Measure (28%, May 2016 snapshot) against 1010data (about 10%, October 2016).** V-B's file (`research/raw/R-B.md`, row RB33) records Bloomberg Second Measure's direct-read figure that 28% of Blue Apron customers remained subscribed six months after first purchase in a snapshot dated May 2016, against 16% each for Plated and HelloFresh in the same snapshot. Both figures are recorded here, as neither this lane nor Lane B could independently open 1010data's own underlying report; only Bloomberg Second Measure's page was read directly (by V-B), while 1010data's figures rest on press coverage (Fast Company, The FERN) that could not itself be opened directly in this session (see Gaps, above). The two studies differ in at least three ways that plausibly explain much of the gap, though none can be confirmed as the full explanation without the 1010data methodology note: (1) different data providers with different underlying transaction panels (Bloomberg Second Measure's card-transaction panel against 1010data's own consumer-spending panel); (2) a five-month difference in snapshot date (May 2016 against October 2016), during which retention could plausibly have fallen further as Blue Apron's subscriber base matured and competition intensified; (3) Second Measure's own page carries a disclaimer, noted by V-B, that its dataset methodology changed in July 2022, which does not explain a gap recorded from a 2016 snapshot but signals that retention figures from this provider should not be chained across years without checking for methodology breaks.

## Gaps

1. No single named report gives both meal kits and ready-to-eat growth for India on a comparable basis to
   the case's own 8% (meal kits) and 15% (ready-to-eat) figures; every third-party figure found uses a
   different product definition and base year (see A1/A2 and Conflicts).
2. A2: no credible, independently reproducible figure exists for the size of the India meal-kit-specifically
   (as opposed to broader "prepared meals") market in US dollars. RA09's ecommerceDB figures could not be
   reproduced on the live page in this session.
3. A6: no source found gives a cooking-only (as distinct from combined domestic-work) time figure for urban
   Indian households, still less split by dual-income status, which is what A6 actually asks for.
4. A7: no source found gives a private-label share figure specific to meal kits or chilled ready-to-eat in
   Indian modern trade; all figures found are for grocery/supermarket categories generally.
5. Quick commerce AOV, GOV and dark-store figures (A3) are drawn from trade press reporting on investor
   calls and shareholder letters rather than the primary filings themselves; the primary investor-relations
   pages were not fetched directly in this session.
6. No figure was found, verified or not, for Snacc (named in the question text) in any source opened this
   session.

## Conflicts

1. **India RTE/ready-meals market size and growth, by publisher (A1).** IMARC (RA02): $6.2 bn in 2025,
   forecast $12.3 bn by 2034, CAGR 7.61%. Ken Research (RA03): $1,280 million in 2025, forecast $3,184
   million by 2031, CAGR 16.40%. Technavio (RA04): about $2.17 bn in 2025, CAGR 28.8% (2025 to 2030).
   GlobalData (RA05): INR 19,931.60 million (roughly $240 million at typical FY25 rates) in 2025, CAGR
   14.29% (2020 to 2025). These four cannot describe the same market on the same definition: IMARC's figure
   is roughly 25 to 5 times larger than the other three for a comparable year, and the reported CAGRs range
   from 7.6% to 28.8%. Each report uses its own product scope (ambient/chilled/frozen "ready meals" versus a
   broader RTE-plus-RTC basket versus frozen food alone); none states a methodology detailed enough in the
   fetched excerpt to reconcile the gap.
2. **India meal-kit market size (A2).** GlobalData (RA07): INR 266.55 million in 2025 (about $3.2 million).
   ecommerceDB (RA09, unverifiable in this session): $238.0 million by 2024. These differ by roughly two
   orders of magnitude for overlapping years; both cannot be right for the same market scope, and neither
   could be reconciled with the other within this session's access.
3. **Time spent on domestic/cooking work by Indian women (A6).** GKToday's reading of the 2019 NSO survey
   (RA27/RA28): "over four hours a day," men "25 minutes a day." Manorama Yearbook's reading of the same
   survey (RA29): women 299 minutes a day (about 5 hours), men 97 minutes a day. The two summaries of one
   survey round to different totals for men (25 minutes versus 97 minutes) even though both describe women
   at roughly 4 to 5 hours; this looks like one source reporting a narrower "cooking and food preparation"
   slice and the other the full "unpaid domestic services" aggregate, but the fetched excerpts do not state
   this explicitly.
4. **India private-label share (A7).** FoodNavigator/Nielsen reading (RA30): about 4.5% across all
   categories. The unverifiable secondary academic figure (RA33): 6% at a similar period. Both are in the
   same rough range (4.5 to 6%) but are not the same figure and come from different named sources (Nielsen
   versus an unnamed 2015 academic paper), so both are kept rather than reconciled.

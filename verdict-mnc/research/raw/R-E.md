## Gaps

- FSSAI's own faqs pages (fssai.gov.in/faqs/?p=2430, ?p=924, ?p=2425, ?p=2376, ?p=2431) returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch in this session and could not be read as primary text, so every Schedule 4 fact in section E2 rests on a secondary site paraphrasing FSSAI rather than FSSAI's own wording. The source hierarchy in PLAN.md section 6 asks for fssai.gov.in text directly; this could not be obtained here and should be retried with a different access method before the research document cites E2 facts as settled.
- No primary FSSAI document (regulation text, circular PDF or gazette notification) was opened directly for E1 (licence thresholds), E3 (beyond the two government-broadcaster items RE16 to RE19), or E7's recall regulation (RE54 to RE58). All of these rest on secondary compliance-consultancy or law-firm summaries.
- E4 (shelf life and MAP/HPP) rests entirely on vendor pages (JBT Marel, Quintus Technologies sell HPP equipment) and one Australian trade article citing a UK guideline. No Indian-specific or FSSAI-specific shelf-life extension figure for chilled ready-to-eat meals was found.
- E5 cost benchmarks (central kitchen, cold-chain logistics) are thin and inconsistent: no named, dated, India-specific cost-per-kilometre or cost-per-pallet-day figure for reefer transport or cold storage was found in this pass. The figures in RE29 to RE37 are either single-company anecdotes or market-size forecasts, not unit-cost benchmarks.
- E6 (food waste) rests on three different company-commissioned or lightly-sourced studies (RE38 to RE41), none independently replicated, and two (RE38, RE39) are commissioned by the companies whose products they favour (HelloFresh, Blue Apron).
- No Indian-specific food-waste comparison between meal kits and grocery retail was found; all E6 rows are from the US/UK/global meal-kit market and should be flagged as context only, not applied to the Indian market, if used in the research document.
- The 2016 draft e-commerce food regulation (RE22, RE23) was a consultation draft; this pass did not establish whether it was ever finalised as a standalone regulation, superseded by the FSS (Licensing and Registration of Food Businesses) Regulations amendments, or folded into the November 2024 advisory (RE16 to RE19). Treat RE22 and RE23 as historical context only.

## Conflicts

- **India refrigerated-trucks market size.** A general search-engine summary (RE33) gave "INR 6.4 Billion in 2023." A direct read of the IMARC Group report page itself (RE32) gives "INR 7.4 Billion in 2024." The two do not reconcile on either the year or the figure; RE32, read directly from the primary page, is the more reliable of the two.
- **India cold-chain/cold-storage market size.** Two different aggregator sources give incompatible figures for what appears to be an overlapping but not identically scoped market: RE34 gives USD 4,701 million in 2024 rising to USD 12,192.35 million by 2030 (a cold chain storage and logistics market, CAGR 17.04%), while RE35 gives USD 9.60 billion in 2025 rising to USD 13.69 billion by 2031 (a cold storage market, CAGR 6.05%). Both are kept; neither was traced to the full underlying named report, and the scope difference (storage and logistics versus storage only) may explain part of the gap.
- **Cloud-kitchen setup cost in India.** RE29 gives Rs 1 to 3 lakh (home-based) or Rs 5 to 20 lakh (rented/owned space). RE30 gives Rs 8 to 20 lakh with monthly rent of Rs 25,000 to Rs 80,000. The ranges overlap at the top end (around Rs 20 lakh) but disagree sharply at the low end; both are kept because neither cites a named, dated primary source.
- **Food waste reduction from meal kits versus grocery shopping.** Three different figures answer the same underlying question with no common methodology disclosed: RE38 (HelloFresh-commissioned, 21% less food waste, 2020), RE39 (Blue Apron-cited, 62% less food waste), and RE40 (an unnamed study reported by ESM, 38% less food waste). All three are kept because each is attributed to a different study or company and none could be shown to be the same measurement restated.
- **FSSAI licence turnover thresholds, old versus new.** RE01 (basic registration to Rs 12 lakh, state licence to Rs 20 crore, central licence above Rs 20 crore) and RE02 (basic registration to Rs 1.5 crore, state licence to Rs 50 crore, central licence above Rs 50 crore, effective 1 April 2026) are not a genuine disagreement; RE02 supersedes RE01 by regulatory amendment and is recorded as CORRECTED rather than as a conflict, but both figures are kept here because a reader checking an older secondary source (as in RE01) will encounter the pre-amendment numbers.

For an 87,000 word document this is a genuinely high hit rate, and it is the single most important thing a judge can rely on.

**Second, the honest reading is real, not decorative.** Section 2.7 and section 19.6 both state, without softening, that the base case is a break even plan and not a profit plan, that every one of the eight sensitivity inputs turns the run rate negative on its downside alone, and that the single largest bridge step rests on the two assumptions with the thinnest outside evidence. Section 20 repeats this with the actual break even thresholds worked out (section 20.5), so a reader does not just hear "there is risk," they see exactly how close the referral assumption and the conversion assumption sit to flipping the plan negative. This is the load bearing honesty the rubric's "pre-empt your own weaknesses" pattern asks for, and it survives contact with the forty judge questions in section 27 rather than being contradicted by them.

## Issues

**1. IMPORTANT: T9's "24 month revenue vs no decision" column mixes two different time bases across its own four rows, and the research document inherits this without flagging it (research document section 16.2; `model/model_tables.md` table T9).**

Current state: section 16.2 reproduces T9 verbatim, showing Proposal A as written at plus 0.0 revenue against no decision. But section 13.2, two tables earlier in the same document, states plainly that A as written's year 2 revenue is 46.6 against 42.0 with no decision, "a year 2 lift of plus 4.6 Cr." Working the cumulative two year figures from T4 confirms this: A as written's two year revenue (42.0 plus 46.6 equals 88.6) against no decision's two year revenue (42.0 plus 42.0 equals 84.0) is a gap of 4.6, not 0.0. The same check on B and C as written (T9 shows plus 7.6 and plus 5.4) reproduces exactly if the comparison uses year 1 alone (49.6 minus 42.0 equals 7.6; 47.4 minus 42.0 equals 5.4), while the "A rebuilt" row in the same column (plus 5.1) only reproduces on the full two year cumulative basis (89.1 minus 84.0 equals 5.1). So three rows of one column use a year 1 only basis and the fourth row uses a two year cumulative basis, under one header that reads "24 month revenue vs no decision" for all four.

Fix: add a footnote to section 16.2's table, where T9 is first reproduced, stating which basis each row's revenue figure actually uses, and note the same point where section 13.2's plus 4.6 Cr figure is stated, so the two sections do not read as contradicting each other to a judge who reads both. This does not require touching the table's own numbers, the growth score, or the scorecard's weighting; it only requires disclosing a methodology difference the document otherwise discloses everywhere else (see section 4's handling of the three unreconciled case figures, which is the model this fix should follow).

Why it matters: a judge who reads section 13 before section 16 has a ready made cross question, "why does your own table say Proposal A gained nothing in revenue when your diagnosis section said plus 4.6 Cr." The document's own standard, stated in section 0.6 and applied rigorously in section 4, is to disclose a gap rather than let a reader find it; this is the one place that standard was not applied to the document's own internal arithmetic.

**2. MODERATE: citation tag formatting is inconsistent across the document (for example research document lines 494, 498 and 499; compare the backtick convention used in section 0.2 and almost everywhere else).**

Current state: the document's own tag legend in section 0.2 and the great majority of citations use a backtick wrapped tag, for example `[C K5]`. A count across the document finds 321 case tag occurrences in total and only 212 of them backtick wrapped, meaning roughly a third, concentrated in sections 5 through 7 and scattered elsewhere, use a plain bracket tag instead, for example "55% [C R2]" with no backticks at lines 494, 498 and 499.

Fix: a single pass to wrap every plain bracket tag in backticks, matching the convention stated in section 0.2. This is a mechanical find and replace and changes no number, no finding and no title.

Why it matters: the rebuild workflow in section 25.5 tells a reader to search the script for an assumption's label and expects the document's own tags to be mechanically greppable for the same reason; an inconsistent tag format undermines that promise in a document whose central credibility claim is that every number traces to a tag.

**3. MODERATE: the document has no standalone table of contents with section numbers, despite running to roughly 130 pages.**

Current state: section 1's coverage matrix maps case IDs to sections, which is valuable but is not a navigational table of contents; a judge or team member trying to jump to, say, section 20's sensitivity table during a three minute cross questioning window has no single page to scan for it.

Fix: add a one page table of contents immediately after section 0, listing all twenty nine main sections and the six appendices by number and title. This adds a page, not a finding, and touches no locked content.

Why it matters: the rubric's structural pass or fail list names a table of contents as a load bearing element for exactly this reason, fast navigation under time pressure, and a document this large is the case where its absence costs the most.

**4. MINOR: four process sections (25, 26, 27, 28) carry topic label headings rather than action title findings, which is a defensible but unflagged exception to the rule stated in section 3.4 that every H2 is locked as a finding.**

Current state: "Assumptions register and model summary," "Slide blueprint for the deck," "Forty judge questions, answered" and "Seven minute talk track for three speakers" describe what the section contains rather than asserting a finding with a number, unlike every argument section from 2 to 24.

Fix: no title change is proposed here. The fix is a one line note, where section 3.4's rule is stated (or in this document's own section 0), that sections 25 to 28 are reference and rehearsal material rather than argument sections, and are exempt from the action title rule for that reason.

Why it matters: without that one line, a careful reader could read section 3.4's "every H2 is a finding and is locked" as a claim the document does not quite keep, when the real explanation is that four sections are a different kind of content, appendix and rehearsal, not argument. Stating the exception once removes the only place in the document where a rule is stated more strongly than it is applied.

## One highest leverage suggestion

Fix issue 1 first. It is the only finding in this review that is a genuine, checkable internal contradiction rather than a formatting or navigation gap, it sits in the scorecard table that carries the growth score for all four options, and it is exactly the kind of thing the ten hardest judge questions in section 27.2 were built to anticipate, yet it slipped past that same discipline. A one sentence footnote closes it without touching a single locked number, the recommendation, or any H2 title.

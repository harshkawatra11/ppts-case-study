2. Board answer (executive summary)

Recommendation. Do not buy Liminal outright. Take a 24.9% stake for ₹400 Cr of balance-sheet cash, with a milestone second tranche of up to ₹600 Cr paid only if Liminal's FY29 ARR reaches ₹500 to ₹650 Cr. Add an MPC technology licence, a right of first refusal with a 30-day matching right against the PhonePe-Mastercard consortium, and an option to take control in months 30 to 42 at fair value, exercisable only if five gates are met.

The proposed ₹4,000 Cr acquisition destroys ₹7,638 Cr of shareholder value in our base case (₹117.5 per share, 6.4% of market cap) M. It stays negative even if every constraint in the case disappears: with zero churn and zero capital drag it still loses ₹2,203 Cr in the base case and ₹1,073 Cr in the bull case M. The price pays for the bull case twice, and the ₹750 Cr annual capital drag C §3.2 is larger than Liminal's entire ARR (3.4x) and larger than Paytm's entire PAT (156%) M.

The minority structure pays fair value for 24.9% of Liminal's economics (base NPV +₹211 Cr, probability-weighted +₹128 Cr) M. It keeps Liminal neutral, which is the thing its bank and exchange clients are paying for. It blocks the consortium from walking away with Liminal unopposed. And it lets Paytm decide on control only after the two unknowns that sink the full deal (client churn and the RBI capital regime) have resolved.

3. Situation overview: Paytm needs growth outside payments, and cannot afford an EPS hit to get it

Answer. On case figures, Paytm trades at 247x earnings and 172x EBITDA M. The market has priced in a turnaround that has barely started showing in earnings. Core UPI growth is capped by a two-player duopoly, and Paytm's regulatory record makes RBI trust its scarcest asset. Any acquisition that dilutes EPS or invites fresh RBI scrutiny attacks both of Paytm's valuation supports at once.

3.3 Regulatory history: RBI trust is Paytm's scarcest asset

  • RBI stopped Paytm Payments Bank (PPBL) from onboarding new customers in March 2022 S08.
  • On 31 January 2024, citing "persistent non-compliances and continued material supervisory concerns", RBI barred deposits and top-ups after 29 February 2024 S08. On 16 February it extended the cut-off to 15 March 2024 S09. The case references these 2024 restrictions C §1.
  • In April 2026 RBI cancelled PPBL's banking licence outright S10, and the Delhi High Court ordered winding up in July 2026 S112. Paytm said the financial impact was nearly nil because the investment was already impaired S07.
  • Paytm's payment aggregator arm, PPSL, received final online PA authorisation on 26 November 2025 S12 and authorisation for offline and cross-border (inward and outward) PA on 17 December 2025 S11. The PA licence is recent and hard-won.
  • The Enforcement Directorate issued a ₹611 Cr FEMA show-cause notice in March 2025 S100; RBI compounded part of it in February 2026 for ₹18.76 lakh S101.

The implication is simple. Paytm has just rebuilt its standing with RBI. The case's capital trap C §3.2 is RBI applying bank-like capital to a PA group that owns crypto custody, at a time when RBI has told Parliament it favours containing crypto, "leaning towards prohibition" S57. A deal that puts custodied digital assets inside the Paytm group spends regulatory capital Paytm does not have to spare.

3.4 The UPI MDR: real money, but not a reason to do this deal

The MDR mechanics C §3.4, S01, S02:

  • 0.4% on person-to-merchant UPI payments above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300 per transaction, from 15 October 2026. Payments up to ₹2,000 and all P2P transfers stay free.
  • Merchants pay, not consumers.
  • Research adds that essential sectors (railways, telecom, agri inputs) pay a flat ₹5, that merchants with monthly UPI QR receipts under ₹1 lakh are exempt, and that about 96% of P2M transactions are unaffected S01.

For Paytm, the case sets incremental net revenue at ₹115 Cr/yr C §3.4. After tax that is ₹86.05 Cr, or +₹1.345 of EPS, lifting standalone EPS from ₹7.50 to ₹8.845 (+17.9%) M.

Three analytical points follow:

  1. The MDR is deal-independent. Paytm earns it whether it buys Liminal, takes a minority, forms a JV or walks away. It belongs in every path's baseline, and we show EPS against both ₹7.50 (the case baseline) and ₹8.845 (the MDR-adjusted baseline), so no path gets credit for revenue it did not create.
  2. It barely dents the drag. ₹115 Cr covers 15.3% of the ₹750 Cr drag pre-tax, and 11.5% on a like-for-like cash basis, because the drag is a cash outflow and the MDR is taxable income M.
  3. It carries political risk. A zero-MDR regime existed until October 2026 for policy reasons. If half the MDR were rolled back, standalone EPS would be ₹8.17 rather than ₹8.85 M. Our recommendation does not depend on the MDR in any way.

4. Strategic value of custody and MPC: real, gated and years away, so buy an option

Answer to Q5.1.2 and Q5.1.3. Custody and MPC infrastructure could matter to Paytm in all four arenas the case names. In every one of them the opening is controlled by a regulator and is 2 to 7 years away. Uncertain, gated, long-dated value is the textbook case for a real option: a small irreversible commitment now, with the right (not the obligation) to scale once the uncertainty resolves. The opportunity justifies a bounded ₹400 Cr step (0.34% of market cap). It does not justify an unbounded commitment of ₹4,000 Cr plus ₹750 Cr every year.

Buying the company now pays the full price of an asset whose value depends on events Paytm cannot control. This is the foreshadowing for the recommendation: the structure must be sized for uncertainty.

5.5 Due-diligence flags a board must hear

  1. The WazirX incident. About $234.9M was drained from a WazirX multisig wallet run under a custody arrangement with Liminal on 18 July 2024, in an attack attributed to North Korea's Lazarus Group S33. WazirX blamed Liminal, and Liminal denied a breach S33. A Grant Thornton audit in September 2024 found the breach originated outside Liminal's systems S34. WazirX ended the custody relationship S35. Delhi Police's chargesheet alleged Liminal had not provided logs for the hack date S36. WazirX's restructuring was sanctioned by the Singapore High Court in October 2025 S37. No direct suit against Liminal was found. Diligence must include an independent forensic review and a litigation search.
  2. The ADGM withdrawal. The reason Liminal's Abu Dhabi licence was withdrawn within a year is not public S39. Diligence must obtain it.
  3. Revenue verification. The case's ₹220 Cr ARR C §2 governs the model, but no public source confirms Liminal's current revenue S43. A quality-of-earnings review is a condition precedent.

6.3 The game tree

  1. Paytm offers the minority structure (24.9%, ROFR, matching right, Tranche 2 to a ₹4,016 Cr headline). Liminal's founders get independence (which protects their ARR), ₹150 Cr of secondary liquidity for early holders, ₹250 Cr of growth capital, and a fair-value exit via the option.
  2. If Liminal accepts, which is our base case, the consortium is blocked by the ROFR on any future sale.
  3. If Liminal shops the deal and the consortium bids for control, Paytm uses its matching right only at or below the maximum justified control price. That is ₹1,797 Cr in the base case with zero churn and zero drag, and ₹1,201 Cr at case churn M. A consortium bid for control will exceed that, so Paytm lets it win.
  4. The consortium then inherits a 30 to 45% revolt risk C §3.1 (PhonePe is a consumer-wallet payments rival to HDFC and Axis too), and PhonePe, as an RBI-regulated PA and PPI issuer, faces the same kind of RBI capital scrutiny the case describes for Paytm (our inference from the case logic, not a case fact).
  5. Paytm's Plan B: license an alternative MPC stack (Fireblocks-class providers), and position a Paytm-aligned neutral offering to capture defecting Liminal clients.

8.3 Can a licence do the job instead? (Q5.1.4)

A commercial MPC licence alone captures most of the operating benefit: we assume 70% of the revenue synergy A22 plus the avoided ₹40 Cr/yr build A20. It fails in three ways:

The licence plus 24.9% equity plus a ROFR fixes all three. The equity is what makes the licence durable.

12. Regulatory path and compliance architecture

Answer to Q5.4.1 to Q5.4.4. The minority needs no RBI approval and, on our reading, no mandatory CCI filing; it needs Board approval, a stock-exchange disclosure and an RBI comfort letter that we make a condition precedent. Investing through One97 into Liminal's Indian entity avoids the overseas investment regime, which Paytm would likely fail, and keeps the PA licence ring-fenced.

12.1 Why the Indian entity, and why One97

  • The ODI trap. Under the FEMA Overseas Investment Rules 2022, an Indian company that is not in financial services may invest in a foreign financial-services entity only if it posted net profits in each of the preceding three years S97, S98. One97 lost about ₹1,417 Cr in FY24 and ₹658.7 Cr in FY25 before earning ₹552 Cr in FY26 S16, S06. A stake in Liminal's Singapore parent would therefore need the RBI approval route (or a contestable argument that crypto custody is not "financial services"). Any open ED matter would also require an agency NOC S99, S100.
  • The domestic route. First Answer India Technologies is an Indian company. One97 has been an Indian-owned and -controlled company since early 2026 (domestic investors 50.3%) S13, S14, so its investment is not indirect foreign investment. One open point for diligence: Ant Group retains an economic interest in about 5.22% of Paytm through optionally convertible debentures held by Resilient Asset Management S127, and no regulator has ruled on whether that matters under Press Note 3. Paying in cash, not shares, keeps this off the critical path. A primary subscription is resident-to-resident and outside FEMA pricing; Companies Act valuation rules apply S109.
  • PA ring-fence. RBI's 2025 PA directions require prior approval for a change of control of the PA itself (PPSL) and fit-and-proper promoters, with RBI's decision final S110, S111. Investing at the One97 level, not PPSL, keeps Liminal out of the PA's balance sheet and licence.

12.4 Does owning custody add regulatory or capital requirements? (Q5.4.2)

Yes. The case assumes RBI would require bank-like capital reserves once Paytm owns the custody infrastructure, a ₹750 Cr/yr FCF drag C §3.2. RBI's 2026 stance on crypto (containment "leaning towards prohibition" S57, S133) makes that assumption plausible, not conservative. Our structure is built so Paytm does not own the infrastructure. The RBI comfort letter converts the assumption that a 24.9% non-controlling stake is not "ownership" into a confirmed fact before any cash moves. If RBI declines, the deal lapses at a cost of diligence fees only.

Stress test: if RBI applied a proportional drag to the minority (24.9% × ₹750 Cr = ₹187 Cr/yr), the minority's NPV would fall to −₹996 Cr M. That is exactly why the comfort letter is a condition precedent rather than a hope.

18. Anticipated judge questions

  1. "Your DCF gives Liminal ₹1,391 Cr. BitGo IPO'd at $2B and BVNK sold for $1.8B. Isn't that too conservative?" BitGo now trades at a ~$0.94B market cap and a ~$1.23B enterprise value, about 6.5x its ~$188M fee revenue S45, S46, S82; Copper is drawing offers at 8% of its peak valuation S47. The football field gives precedents a 20% weight and still lands at 7.5x ARR. Even the top of the precedent range (15x on BVNK) is below 18.2x.
  2. "Why 24.9% and not 26% or 30%?" Below 25% there is no power to block a special resolution, the stake stays under CCI's material-influence line for minority exemptions, and it signals non-control to clients and RBI. At 20% or more Paytm still gets significant influence and equity accounting S114.
  3. "Why would the founders accept a minority when a consortium wants control?" Independence protects their ARR; they get ₹250 Cr of growth capital, ₹150 Cr of liquidity for early holders, and a route to a ₹4,016 Cr headline valuation for delivered ARR. Control by a consumer-wallet owner puts 30% to 45% of their ARR at risk C §3.1.
  4. "What if RBI applies the drag to your minority?" Then the deal does not happen. RBI comfort is a condition precedent. Stress-tested, a proportional drag would take NPV to −₹996 Cr, which is exactly why we will not proceed without the letter.
  5. "Isn't the MDR a reason to do the deal?" No. Paytm earns it in every path. It offsets 15% of the drag.
  6. "Why not just license?" A licence dies on a change of control. Equity plus a ROFR is what makes the licence durable.
  7. "What does the option cost you?" Nothing beyond the fair price of the stake. The strike is fair value at exercise, so the option's value is the right to decide later, not a bargain price.
  8. "Your churn assumptions for the minority are unvalidated." Correct, and we say so. The Customer Validation Plan and client comfort letters are conditions precedent. The recommendation survives up to 57.6% churn.
  9. "What if the consortium also walks?" Liminal stays independent with Paytm as its largest strategic shareholder, which is the base case.
  10. "Walk-away has an NPV of zero and no risk. Why not that?" It forgoes ₹406 Cr of synergy and build value and leaves Liminal open to the consortium. The minority is NPV-positive and keeps both.
  11. "Why is the JV worse than the minority?" Joint control attracts half the drag (₹375 Cr/yr) and offers no block on the consortium; even with zero drag it is slightly negative.
  12. "The case says AA-. Your research says there is no CRISIL rating." The case governs. Either way, our structure leaves every credit metric unchanged.
  13. "How do you handle the WazirX history?" An independent forensic review is a condition precedent. The Grant Thornton audit placed the breach outside Liminal S34, but the Delhi Police allegation on logs S36 needs closing.
  14. "Why invest in the Indian entity?" Because One97 would likely fail the ODI three-year profit test for a foreign stake S97, and Liminal's case clients are Indian.
  15. "What is the single number that decides this?" ₹750 Cr a year. It is larger than Liminal's ARR and Paytm's PAT. Only non-control removes it.
  16. "Why not an all-equity full deal? It halves the dilution." Financing changes EPS, not value. Every mix has the same −₹7,638 Cr NPV.
  17. "What if Liminal's ARR is lower than ₹220 Cr?" Quality of earnings is a CP; closing ARR below ₹200 Cr is a walk-away trigger.
  18. "Can the Board still get control if the market opens up?" Yes, via the option in months 30 to 42, at fair value, once the five gates hold.
  19. "Is the ₹400 Cr stake itself value-destroying at bear case?" Slightly: −₹113 Cr in bear against +₹211 Cr in base, probability-weighted +₹128 Cr. The downside is bounded at the stake price, protected by a liquidation preference.
  20. "How do you know the drag would not apply to the consortium?" We do not claim to know. We infer from the case logic that an RBI-regulated PA and wallet issuer would face comparable scrutiny. That is why we treat it as a risk to the consortium, not a certainty.

# R-D-TIEBREAK: Tie-break universe profiles (Nifty Pharma and Nifty Healthcare, excluding NIFTY 50 names)
Accessed: 13 Oct 2026. Researcher: lane agent R-D-TIEBREAK.

This lane is a breadth lane, not a depth lane (PLAN.md §4.2). It covers every company in the Nifty Pharma index
and the Nifty Healthcare index that is not already a NIFTY 50 Track D name (Sun Pharmaceutical, Cipla, Dr. Reddy's
Laboratories, Apollo Hospitals, Max Healthcare Institute are excluded here; each has its own full dossier in
`R-D-SUNPHARMA.md`, `R-D-CIPLA.md`, `R-D-DRREDDY.md`, `R-D-APOLLOHOSP.md`, `R-D-MAXHEALTH.md`). The 85% VERIFIED
gate (PLAN.md §4.2 Gate G-R1) does not apply to this lane; it still never fabricates a figure, quote, or URL, and
every number traces to a real source.

**Universe determination.** The live NSE index constituent files were read directly on 13 Oct 2026 (the primary
`niftyindices.com` host timed out, as PLAN.md §1.6 anticipated; the fallback `archives.nseindia.com` host answered):

  Cipla, Divi's Laboratories, Dr. Reddy's Laboratories, Gland Pharma, Glenmark Pharmaceuticals, Ipca Laboratories,
  Laurus Labs, Lupin, Mankind Pharma, Piramal Pharma, Sai Life Sciences, Sun Pharmaceutical Industries, Torrent
  Pharmaceuticals, Wockhardt, Zydus Lifesciences.

  Pharmaceuticals, Alkem Laboratories, Aurobindo Pharma, Biocon, Divi's Laboratories, Gland Pharma, Glenmark
  Pharmaceuticals, Ipca Laboratories, Laurus Labs, Lupin, Mankind Pharma, Piramal Pharma, Sai Life Sciences, Torrent

  This list does not include some names the plan's own illustrative list guessed at (Syngene International, Dr Lal
  PathLabs, Narayana Hrudayalaya, Global Health/Medanta); those are not current constituents of either index as read
  directly on 13 Oct 2026, and are not profiled here on that basis (logged as a Gap below for a possible later

- 15 of the 19 sit in both indices (sub-industry: pharmaceuticals, mostly US-generics/India-branded/CDMO mixed
  models). Abbott India, Ajanta Pharmaceuticals, Piramal Pharma, Sai Life Sciences and Wockhardt are Pharma-only.
  Fortis Healthcare and KIMS are Healthcare-only, and are hospital operators, not pharmaceutical manufacturers.

**Financial snapshot methodology.** All three-year figures (FY2024, FY2025, FY2026, year ended 31 Mar) are read
directly from each company's consolidated income-statement page on stockanalysis.com, a financial-data aggregator,
not from the company's own annual report. None of the 19 companies' primary annual reports were opened in this
pass, consistent with this lane's breadth-over-depth mandate; every figure below should be treated as provisional
until a depth pass (if one is ever commissioned for a tie-break name during the finals) opens the primary filing.
stockanalysis.com does not report an EBITDA margin for any of these companies; the schema's "EBITDA margin" column
is therefore filled with the page's "operating margin" (EBIT-based, not EBITDA-based) and labelled as such in every
row. This is a real, named limitation, not a silent substitution.

## Investigation angles (3 to 4 companies only, per PLAN.md's instruction not to write practice hypotheses for every company)

- **Alkem Laboratories.** The USFDA classified the Daman (Amaliya) formulation facility Official Action Indicated in
  the same year the company's CEO, Vikas Gupta, disclosed his resignation (24 Apr 2026, effective 30 Jun 2026),
  while Q1 FY27 consolidated profit fell about 21% YoY on 11% revenue growth. A plausible test for the engine: does
  the leadership change precede, coincide with, or follow the regulatory escalation in time, and does management's
  own narrative (margin pressure attributed to "severe margin pressures" in press coverage) survive a check against
  which specific cost line actually moved, rather than being accepted as the explanation on its face.
- **Biocon.** Biocon completed buying out Mylan/Viatris's remaining Biocon Biologics stake (21 Jan 2026, raising its
  holding to about 98%, partly funded by a Rs 4,150 crore QIP on 14 Jan 2026) in the same year a USFDA pre-license
  inspection of the same Biocon Biologics unit's Bengaluru site produced a Form 483 with five observations (Apr
  2026). A plausible test: whether the capital raised and deployed to consolidate ownership was sized against a
  base case that already priced in the regulatory inspection risk at the exact site being consolidated, or whether
  the two events are being treated by the market as unrelated when the balance sheet now concentrates both the
  upside and the inspection risk in one shareholder.
- **Torrent Pharmaceuticals.** The JB Chemicals acquisition (announced mid-2025, Rs 19,500 crore, open offer closed
  Dec 2025 with minimal subscription) is structured as a share-swap merger whose completion status could not be
  confirmed as of 13 Oct 2026 in this pass. A plausible test: compare Torrent's standalone margin trajectory
  (operating margin 23.98% to 25.41% to 24.61% across FY24 to FY26, essentially flat to slightly down in FY26) against
  the synergy case management is likely to have made at announcement, once the merger's actual completion date and
  first combined results are available.
- **Wockhardt.** Zaynich's USFDA approval (late May or early June 2026) drove a 16 to 19% intraday share-price rally
  and is described by the company as the first fully Indian-discovered new chemical entity to win US approval, yet
  FY2026 revenue (Rs 3,462.0 crore) and PAT (Rs 213.0 crore, the company's first profitable year after two loss-making
  years) cannot yet reflect any US commercial sales of a drug approved near the fiscal year's close. This is a
  direct instance of the narrative-versus-number divergence this lane's parent dossiers are built to test: the
  reported financial turnaround and the regulatory headline are two separate stories until a quarter with actual US
  Zaynich sales data exists, and a summarising agent that does not separate the two risks crediting FY2026's
  profitability to a drug that had not yet generated revenue in that year.

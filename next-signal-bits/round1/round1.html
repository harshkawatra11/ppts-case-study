The approach in one line

Next Signal computes before it reads, looks outside the company before it trusts the company, and spends most of its effort trying to kill its own hypotheses. A discovery is whatever survives that effort.

1. Why this is not a summariser

A system that only reads a company's own documents mostly restates what management already chose to emphasise. The brief's own worked example finds its story in a computed gap between narrative (volume-led premium growth) and arithmetic (price versus volume split, receivable days rising from 28 to 46). A summarising agent that does not compute this from raw disclosed numbers cannot reach that kind of finding, because the finding only exists after the calculation. Track D compounds this: the most informative evidence often sits outside the company entirely (USFDA inspection records, a manufacturing partner's regulatory status, peer segment numbers for the same quarter), which a single-corpus retrieval agent has no mechanism to go find or to notice contradicts the company's own framing.

Our build inverts the usual order. A deterministic metric library (44 formulas) computes peer z-scores and statistical change-points across every disclosed period before any language model is asked to form an opinion. The model's only job is to explain an anomaly the arithmetic has already found, never to originate a number shown to the user.

3. Workflow: ticker to tested discovery

  1. Discover. Resolve the company (NSE index CSVs, live if needed), score candidate angles by 0.35·data_availability + 0.40·anomaly_signal + 0.25·novelty_prior, keep the top 4.
  2. Connect. Fetch documents in parallel; BM25 ranks candidate pages per metric; an LLM extracts facts with a verbatim quote, verified character-for-character against the source page before it is trusted.
  3. Calculate. 44 formulas (growth, mix, profitability, working capital, regulatory, hospital metrics), peer-relative z-scores, PELT change-point detection, and an event study against NIFTY 50.
  4. Reason. An LLM proposes up to 6 hypotheses from the ranked anomalies, each with explicit predictions and disconfirmers; deterministic rival hypotheses (sector, FX, one-off, accounting) are generated automatically.
  5. Challenge. Nine falsification tests (peer, FX, one-off, reclassification, seasonality, persistence, novelty, a disconfirming search, a point-in-time replay) plus an Analysis of Competing Hypotheses matrix. The strongest surviving rival becomes the stated Counterargument.
  6. Prove. A deterministic strength score (0 to 10: independence, reliability, corroboration, survival, persistence, minus penalties) labels the discovery Strong, Moderate or Weak with its reasoning shown, builds the Falsification Condition from the hypothesis's own prediction, and writes the brief's eight required elements.

4. Track D, and generalisation without tuning

The universe is read live from NSE Indices CSVs at run time, never hard-coded: NIFTY 50 filtered to Healthcare (5 names today) plus the tie-break universe (Nifty Pharma and Nifty Healthcare minus those 5, roughly 19 further companies as of October 2026). A company routes to one of two sub-industry playbooks, pharma (10 angles: business- model shift, regulatory-before-mainstream, geographic mix, exclusivity cliffs, launch-masks-erosion, working capital, R&D intensity, profit quality, partner dependence, cross-channel disclosure) or hospital (6 angles: announced-versus-commissioned beds, ARPOB decomposition, occupancy dilution, segment drag, regulatory price exposure, organic-versus-acquired growth), plus a generic fallback (6 angles) for any company outside those two. A company-name test asserts no symbol or company name appears anywhere in the engine's own logic, only in the universe loader's live data and the evaluation harness.

5. Triangulation and contradiction detection

Evidence is counted by independent origin, not by document: a press item that merely restates a company filing within three days collapses into that filing's origin rather than counting as a second source. Five contradiction types are checked automatically: a numeric mismatch between two sources for the same metric and period (primary-source precedence resolves it); a narrative-versus-number divergence (management's framing points one way, the computed trend points the other); a guidance-versus-actual miss; a cross-exchange mismatch (NSE versus SEC); and an aggregator-versus-primary disagreement. This last type is not theoretical: while building this system, two secondary sources gave materially different net-profit figures for the same company and quarter, and the primary filing (opened directly) confirmed one was simply wrong. The engine's rule, applied here by hand before it was automated, is to always trust the primary filing.

6. Self-falsification

Every hypothesis faces the same nine-test battery before it can become a discovery. A hypothesis that only explains a sector-wide move, or that disappears once a disclosed one-off is excluded, or that a rival explanation scores more consistently in the Analysis of Competing Hypotheses matrix, is downgraded or killed, and the kill reason is shown, not hidden. The Falsification Condition itself is built from the hypothesis's own first prediction: a named metric, a comparator, a threshold derived from the pre-change period or the peer median, and a dated next check against a named future filing. If nothing survives, the system says so plainly rather than forcing a weak claim.

7. Output: dashboard and report, both

A Next.js dashboard shows the discovery, a strength bar with the Moderate/Strong thresholds marked, a swimlane timeline with the change-point drawn on the plot, an Analysis of Competing Hypotheses heat grid, and an evidence drawer that opens the exact quote and source for any number clicked. The same run exports to Markdown, a styled PDF, an Excel workbook (one sheet per table), and a CSV bundle, so either required format (report or dashboard) is simply a button click from the other.

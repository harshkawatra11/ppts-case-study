## The post (final copy, v3: hard cap of 200 to 220 words, hashtags excluded)

Getting shortlisted into the top 7 teams at IIT Delhi's M&A Masterminds was not on my cards for 2026. More than 1,500 teams registered. Ours made the final seven.

The brief: should Paytm acquire Liminal Custody Solutions, a crypto custody firm, for ₹4,000 Cr? It read like a clean growth story.

The numbers told a different one. In our base case, full ownership destroyed ₹7,638 Cr of value. Even with zero churn and zero capital drag it still lost ₹2,203 Cr, because the price sat at 2.4x fair value. Liminal's moat was its neutrality. Under a payments rival, 30 to 45% of its revenue was expected to walk.

So the question changed. It stopped being whether to buy and became how to capture the upside without inheriting the risk.

Our answer was structure over control: a 24.9% stake for ₹400 Cr, a technology licence, a right of first refusal, and a gated option to take control in 2029. Base-case NPV: +₹211 Cr, with no new debt or shares.

None of it happens without my team.

- Aakriti Aggarwal (B.Com, first year) and Ranvir Kawatra (B.Tech, first year) were on their first case study ever. They worked like seasoned analysts.

My takeaway: the best recommendation is often not the deal, but the discipline to restructure it.

## Deck 1: The Cash Cows (2019)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Competition | Global Case Competition at Harvard 2019 |
| Case | Should GM acquire Tesla? |
| Outcome | Winner |
| Team | Mathias Bohn, Rodrigo Garcia, Marlène Luce, Roman Zanoli |
| School | HEC Paris |
| Date | March 1, 2019 |
| Pages | 106 (no cap that year) |
| Tool | Microsoft PowerPoint |

### Recommendation
*GM should not acquire Tesla, but instead acquire Rivian, an EV truck manufacturer whose products and brand will have strategic value for GM in the future EV landscape.*

### Key numbers
- Tesla overvalued by 18% (DCF) / 17% (multiples)
- Value destroyed: **$26.2bn**
- Premium decomposition: 37.5% comparable + 10% Elon Musk premium

### Section structure
1. Cover (slide 1) + Internal title (slide 2)
2. Hook quote (slide 3) — Henry Ford: "If I had asked people what they wanted, they would have said faster horses…"
3. TOC + Abbreviations (slides 4–5)
4. Executive Summary (slides 6–7)
5. Industry Analysis / Market Analysis (slides 8–14)
6. Strategic Fit — GM (slides 15–21), Tesla (slides 22–27), Fit (slides 28–30)
7. Financial Analysis — Comparables (31–39), DCF (40–44), Synergies (45–49)
8. Feasibility (slides 50–54)
9. Alternative Solution — Rivian (slides 55–61)
10. Conclusion (slides 62–63)
11. Appendix (slides 64–93)
12. Bibliography (slides 94–106)

### Foreshadowing
Slide 14: "Major car manufacturers are readying their EV pickup portfolios — limited availability of EV models in the growing and highly profitable pickup truck segment represents a huge market opportunity." (Rivian named on slide 58, 44 slides later)

### Standout archetypes used
- Hook quote (slide 3) — only deck to include this
- Methodology roadmap (slide 33) — only deck to include this
- Strategic fit grid (slide 29) — 6 dimensions × 2 verdicts
- Football field (slide 44)
- Moneyshot waterfall (slide 49) — $26.2bn destroyed
- "Conclusively…" section closer (slide 54)
- Q&A conclusion grid (slide 63)
- Build/Buy/Partner template (slide 57)
- Deep dive on Rivian with 4 expert quotes (slide 59)

## Deck 2: Providentia Capital (2020)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Competition | Global Case Competition at Harvard 2020 |
| Case | Should Amazon acquire Netflix? |
| Outcome | Winner |
| Team | Aanand Negi, Kunal Vats, Ojas Jhamb, Raghav Nath, Sparsh Sehgal |
| School | Shaheed Sukhdev College of Business Studies, University of Delhi |
| Date | March 20, 2020 |
| Pages | 100 (hit the new cap exactly) |
| Tool | Microsoft PowerPoint |

### Recommendation
*Amazon should not acquire Netflix, but instead acquire ViacomCBS.*

### Key numbers
- Netflix overvalued by 43% (DCF)
- Value destroyed: **$147.7bn**
- Premium decomposition: 55% control premium + 10% Poison Pill premium

### Section structure
1. Cover (slide 1)
2. TOC + Abbreviations (slides 2–3)
3. Executive Summary (slides 4–5)
4. Industry Analysis (slides 6–13)
5. Company Overview — Amazon (slides 14–20), Netflix (slides 21–27)
6. Strategic Fit (slides 28–38)
7. Financial Analysis — Comparables (39–45), DCF (46–52)
8. Acquisition Feasibility (slides 53–59)
9. Alternative Solution — three sub-sections: Evaluating Alternatives (60–67), Post-Acquisition Strategy (68–72), Financial Feasibility (73–77)
10. Conclusion (slides 78–79)
11. Appendix (slides 80–95)
12. Bibliography (slides 96–100)

### Foreshadowing
Slide 19: "Amazon has its own Methodology of Acquisitions" — categorizes Amazon's M&A history and concludes "Amazon has generally not participated in horizontal acquisitions." (ViacomCBS named on slide 64, 45 slides later)

### Standout archetypes used
- Full DCF model on one main-deck slide (slide 48) — first deck to do this
- Sensitivity matrix (slide 49) — first deck to include this
- Football field (slide 51)
- Moneyshot waterfall (slide 52) — $147.7bn destroyed (largest in corpus)
- Pro forma combined-entity P&L (slide 75) — only deck to include this
- EPS accretion/dilution analysis (slide 76) — only deck to include this
- Debt tranche structure (slide 77) — only deck to include this
- Three-sub-section alternative solution

### Notable polish issue
Slide 5 contains the line "whose products and brand will have strategic value for GM in the future EV landscape" — literally copy-pasted from Cash Cows 2019 and forgotten in editing. They won anyway.

## Deck 3: Kicking A$$ets (2021)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Competition | Global Case Competition at Harvard 2021 |
| Case | Should Nvidia merge with AMD? |
| Outcome | Winner |
| Team | Rhea Kumar, Hannah Liu |
| School | Temple University, Fox School of Business |
| Date | March 6, 2021 |
| Pages | 100 (hit the cap exactly) |
| Tool | Microsoft PowerPoint |

### Recommendation
*Nvidia should not acquire AMD and should instead acquire MediaTek.*

(Note: They recast the merger question as an acquisition question to fit the template.)

### Key numbers
- AMD overvalued by 33.4%
- Value destroyed: **$57.18bn**
- Premium: 46% semiconductor industry M&A average (no company-specific decomposition — missed opportunity)

### Section structure
1. Cover (slide 1)
2. TOC + Abbreviations (slides 2–3)
3. Executive Summary (slides 4–5) — slide 4 is a divider showing "Nvidia's Course of Action"
4. Industry Overview (slides 6–12)
5. Company Analysis — Nvidia (13–19), AMD (20–25), Strategic Fit (26–30)
6. Financial Analysis — Comparables (31–38), DCF (39–45), Synergies (46–50)
7. Acquisition Feasibility (slides 51–58)
8. Alternative Solution — MediaTek (slides 59–70)
9. Conclusion (slides 71–72)
10. Appendix (slides 73–90)
11. Bibliography (slides 91–100)

### Foreshadowing
Slide 12: "GPUs are used for almost all training. They will soon lose market share to ASICs, until the compute market is evenly divided by 2025." (MediaTek, which makes SoCs with ASIC-like architectures, named on slide 63, 51 slides later)

### Standout archetypes used
- 8-target acquisition shortlist (slides 62–63) — widest shortlist in the corpus
- Build/Buy/Partner template (slide 61) — "Acquisition / Partnership / Development"
- Football field (slide 44 for AMD, slide 69 for MediaTek)
- Sensitivity matrix with both Exit Multiple and Perpetual Growth Rate methods (slide 43)
- Moneyshot waterfall (slide 50) — $57.18bn destroyed
- Double synthesis: "Conclusively…" (slide 58) + "Therefore…" (slide 72)
- Strategic Fit slides framed as "Foreseen Problems" — using the strategic fit section to build the case against the merger

### Notable choices
- Two-author team (smaller than the other winners) — still delivered 100 slides
- Recast the merger question as an acquisition (Nvidia acquires AMD) without acknowledging the reframe
- Skipped the hook quote slide
- Skipped the company-specific premium decomposition (used industry average only)

## Patterns observed across all three winners

### Always present
- Cover + TOC + Abbreviations front matter
- 2-column for/against executive summary
- Contrarian "don't buy X, buy Y" recommendation
- Foreshadowing of alternative 30+ slides before reveal
- Section dividers + sub-section progress trackers
- Persistent navigation bar on content slides
- MECE peer group filter table
- Three-scenario FCF projection
- Football field valuation
- Moneyshot "$X bn destroyed" waterfall slide
- Acquisition target comparison table
- Deep dive on chosen alternative with expert quotes
- Section-closer recap before pivoting from rejection to alternative
- Conclusion synthesis slide with no new content
- Appendix with deep dives
- Harvard-style bibliography (5–13 slides)
- Action titles (full-sentence findings)
- Source citations on content slides

### Present in 2 of 3
- Sensitivity analysis matrix
- Premium decomposition with company-specific add-on
- Build/Buy/Partner template
- "Conclusively…" section closer + "Therefore…" final synthesis (the double synthesis)

### Present in only 1 of 3 (style choices)
- Hook quote slide (Cash Cows only)
- Methodology roadmap (Cash Cows only)
- Full DCF model on main-deck slide (Providentia; partial in Kicking A$$ets)
- Pro forma combined-entity P&L (Providentia only)
- EPS accretion/dilution analysis (Providentia only)
- Debt tranche structure (Providentia only)
- Three-sub-section alternative solution (Providentia only)
- Strategic fit framed as "Foreseen Problems" (Kicking A$$ets only)

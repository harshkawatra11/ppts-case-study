# Case Type: Strategy

Strategy cases involve recommending a course of action for a company facing a strategic decision — typically market entry, competitive response, growth, diversification, or divestiture.

**Important note on calibration:** Unlike M&A cases (where we have corpus data), strategy patterns described here are extrapolated from the meta-playbook + standard consulting frameworks. They reflect plausible application of the corpus patterns to strategy contexts, not directly observed winning decks. Treat the section content as a reasoned starting point and adjust based on the specific competition.

---

## When this case type applies

**Typical prompts:**
- "Should X enter [market/segment/geography]?"
- "How should X respond to [competitor's move]?"
- "What is X's growth strategy for the next 5 years?"
- "Should X diversify into [adjacent business]?"
- "How should X compete with [competitor]?"
- "Should X divest [business unit]?"
- Consulting case interview problems

**Audience:** CEO, board, strategy team, McKinsey/BCG-style consulting context. Expects MECE thinking, structured analysis, and clear recommendations.

**Typical deliverable:** 15–60 slide PowerPoint (consulting cases are often shorter than M&A decks).

## Section content adaptation

### II. Context = Market / Industry Analysis

Funnel from broad market context to the specific opportunity:
- Industry size and growth (TAM)
- Industry structure (Porter's 5 forces, often)
- Customer trends and unmet needs
- Competitive dynamics and consolidation
- Technology / regulatory disruption
- The specific opportunity gap (foreshadow the recommendation)

### III. Actors = Company + Competitors + Customers

Standard sub-sections:
1. **The Company** — Current position, capabilities, financial trajectory, strategic constraints
2. **Direct Competitors** — Top 3–5 competitors, their positioning, recent moves, threat level
3. **Adjacent / Indirect competitors** — emerging threats from outside the industry
4. **Customer segments** — What customers want, how they're served today, segment economics
5. **Strategic Fit / Capability Assessment** — gaps between current company and what the opportunity requires

### IV. Quantitative Analysis = Market Sizing + NPV of Options

**A. Market Sizing (TAM / SAM / SOM):**
- TAM (Total Addressable Market) — full market value
- SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market) — the portion the company could realistically address given its capabilities
- SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market) — what the company can actually capture
- Five-year forecast under different scenarios

**B. Strategic Option NPV:**
- Identify 3–5 strategic options
- For each: Revenue projection, Cost structure (CapEx, OpEx), Risks, NPV calculation
- Bear / Base / Bull scenarios per option
- Comparative NPV summary

**C. Competitive Response Modeling:**
- Game theory: what's the likely competitor response to each option?
- Adjusted NPV accounting for response
- Time-to-impact analysis

### V. Feasibility = Capability Gap + Competitive Response + Time-to-Impact

Standard sub-topics:
1. **Capability gap** — Does the company have the talent, technology, IP, distribution, brand to execute? Where are the gaps?
2. **Competitive response** — How will competitors react? Will they imitate, undercut, retaliate?
3. **Time-to-impact** — Will the strategic move pay off in time? Or will the market move before execution completes?
4. **Regulatory / external risks** — Antitrust, regulation, geopolitical
5. **Organizational change capacity** — Can the company actually execute the change?

### VI. Recommended Alternative = Alternative Strategy

Standard sub-sections:
1. **Evaluating alternatives** — 3-path framework (Direct attack / Adjacent positioning / Defer or wait) + 4–8 specific option shortlist
2. **Deep dive on chosen strategy** — Strategic logic, capability fit, customer alignment, with 2–4 expert quotes (industry analysts, consultants, academic experts)
3. **Implementation plan** — Phased roadmap, milestones, metrics, governance, change management plan

## Case-type-specific archetypes

### Porter's Five Forces analysis
Layout: 5 boxes arranged around a central "Industry Profitability" box. Each box assesses one force (Buyer power / Supplier power / Threat of substitutes / Threat of new entrants / Industry rivalry) with rating (Low/Medium/High). Output: industry attractiveness assessment.

### BCG Matrix / Growth-Share Matrix
2×2 grid: Market growth (Y axis) × Relative market share (X axis). Place businesses or products in Stars / Cash Cows / Question Marks / Dogs.

### McKinsey 9-Box / GE Matrix
3×3 grid: Industry attractiveness × Business strength. Place units in Invest / Selectively invest / Harvest / Divest cells.

### Ansoff Matrix
2×2 grid: Products (Existing / New) × Markets (Existing / New). Four growth strategies: Market penetration / Product development / Market development / Diversification.

### TAM / SAM / SOM funnel
Three concentric circles or descending bars showing total market → addressable market → obtainable market with reasoning for each narrowing.

### Strategic Options NPV Comparison
Bar chart or waterfall showing NPV of each strategic option side by side, with NPV bands (low/realistic/high) per option.

### Competitive Response Game Tree
Decision tree showing the company's move + likely competitor responses + counter-responses, with payoff at each leaf.

### Capability Heatmap
Grid: Capability dimensions (rows) × Strategic options (columns). Each cell colored to show capability fit (Strong / Moderate / Gap).

## The Strategy Moneyshot

Format varies but the principle is identical: a waterfall or comparison showing why the recommended strategy delivers more value than the alternatives.

**Common formats:**
- **NPV waterfall** — Each strategic option as a stacked bar showing Revenue – Cost = NPV. The recommended option's NPV is highest.
- **Capture-vs-cost** — A scatter chart showing each option positioned on (Investment required, Expected NPV captured). The recommended option is in the high-NPV / reasonable-investment quadrant.
- **Time-to-impact comparison** — A timeline showing when each option pays off vs the recommended option.

Bottom of slide, large bold type:
*"Pursuing [recommended strategy] vs [obvious strategy] captures $X bn more NPV over Y years"*
or
*"Entering [recommended market] delivers Y% higher returns than [obvious market] over Z years"*

## Common frameworks used in strategy cases

- **Porter's Five Forces** — Industry attractiveness
- **Porter's Generic Strategies** — Cost leadership / Differentiation / Focus
- **Value Chain Analysis** — Where value is created/captured
- **BCG / GE / Ansoff Matrices** — Portfolio decisions
- **3 C's** — Company / Customer / Competition
- **4 P's** — Product / Price / Place / Promotion (also a marketing framework)
- **7 S's** — Strategy / Structure / Systems / Shared values / Skills / Style / Staff
- **TAM/SAM/SOM** — Market sizing
- **NPV / IRR / Payback period** — Financial assessment of options
- **MECE thinking** — Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive option structuring
- **Issue trees** — Decomposing the strategic question into MECE sub-questions

## Example contrarian recommendation patterns

The contrarian play in strategy cases often looks like:

| Case framing | Obvious answer | Contrarian alternative |
|---|---|---|
| "Should X enter China?" | Yes, here's the entry plan | No, enter Vietnam / Indonesia / India where capability fits better |
| "How should X compete with Y on price?" | Match Y's price | Move upmarket / change value proposition / abandon competing on price |
| "Should X diversify into [adjacent business]?" | Yes, with execution plan | No — double down on core, defend competitive position |
| "How should X grow?" | Geographic expansion of current product | Product extension to current customers (deeper before wider) |
| "Should X respond to disruptor Z?" | Direct competitive response | Acquire / partner with Z / build complementary moat |
| "What's the 5-year strategy?" | Linear extrapolation of current business | Repositioning around a disruption the team identifies |

**Pattern:** The contrarian recognizes a constraint, a capability gap, or a hidden second-order effect the obvious answer misses.

## Common strategy failure modes

1. **Generic recommendation** — could apply to any company in the industry
2. **No market sizing rigor** — TAM/SAM/SOM not done carefully
3. **Ignoring competitive response** — assuming the world stays still while X executes
4. **Capability gap glossed over** — claiming X can execute without showing it has the capabilities
5. **Implementation as an afterthought** — recommendation given without detailed execution plan
6. **No alternatives evaluated** — single option presented as obvious without stress-testing
7. **Framework-first thinking** — applying Porter's / SWOT / 7S without actually answering the question
8. **No time-to-impact analysis** — ignoring when the recommendation pays off
9. **Linear forecasting** — extrapolating current trends rather than thinking about discontinuities

## Adapting deck length

Strategy cases often have shorter formats than the 100-slide M&A template. Compression guidance:

- **30-slide consulting case:** Front matter (3) + Exec summary (2) + Context (3) + Actors (4) + Quantitative (6) + Feasibility (3) + Alternative (5) + Conclusion (1) + Appendix (3)
- **15-slide top-of-funnel:** Front matter (1) + Exec summary (2) + Context (2) + Quantitative (4) + Alternative (3) + Conclusion (1) + Appendix (2). Drop the actors section, fold into context.
- **60-slide expanded:** Add depth to context, actors, alternative sections; expand appendix.

Always keep: executive summary, the moneyshot equivalent, the recommendation synthesis, sources.

# Making small-parcel exports work

**CaseQuest USME DTU | Team IdeaForge | Harsh Kawatra and Dayita Arora**

**Research date:** 28 October 2026. **Audience:** DPIIT and the 2026 Inter-Ministerial Committee on E-Commerce Exports. **Status:** Policy proposal and evidence review. No primary interviews, commercial agreements or pilot results have been claimed.

## 1. Executive recommendation

DPIIT should convene a Cluster Export Compact around existing export rules and services. The compact would give small producers local help choosing among postal, courier and exporter-on-record routes; obtain transparent, comparable commercial service terms; and measure whether this help improves seller economics without weakening customs and tax controls. Local assistance and commercial accountability are the proposed increments. India's government has already removed the courier value cap, broadened eligible export benefits, introduced risk-based courier returns, and enabled registered exporters-on-record for inventory-based e-commerce exports [P04, P05, P06, P10]. Re-announcing those reforms would not solve the case.

The committee should authorise a controlled, six-cluster pilot only after local operating partners, legal process owners and budget authority are verified. A group-level early-access comparison can estimate changes in administrative time, controllable export cost and repeat exporting. Customs and tax integrity require a separate audit sample; a small pilot cannot establish a national fraud rate.

The first decision is an assisted route-choice and procurement pilot. The second decision, after observation, is whether to scale, revise or close it. The proposal is conditional on measurable seller benefit after service fees and no material deterioration in integrity indicators.

## 3. The problem that remains after the 2026 reforms

The brief describes a first-time exporter facing registrations, invoices, declarations, route-specific processes, high parcel rates and expensive returns [C]. Its core trade-off is legitimate: indiscriminate relief could weaken customs checks or revenue protection. Yet the case's policy setting has moved. Since the brief was drafted, several relevant provisions have taken effect. The remaining question is whether a low-volume seller can understand and use them on fair commercial terms.

The measurable unit is a shipment, not an abstract exporter. A seller chooses a product, destination, price, route and service provider. Each choice changes eligibility, fees, delivery risk and possible return recovery. A successful pilot should make this choice easier and cheaper where evidence supports it, and refuse a route where the product is restricted or economics are negative.

## 4. Current policy baseline

| Date | Existing measure | What it does | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 Sep 2024 | Eligible courier exports gain Duty Drawback, RoDTEP and RoSCTL | Allows specified benefits through the prescribed customs workflow | Universal benefit for every shipment [P02, P03] |
| 15 Jan 2026 | Eligible electronic postal exports gain the same categories of benefits | Expands potential route choice | That manual postal filings qualify [P04] |
| 1 Apr 2026 | ₹10 lakh courier export consignment cap removed | Removes a value ceiling | Product exportability or simple documentation [P05, P06] |
| 1 Apr 2026 | Risk-based courier re-import treatment and ECCS Return Module | Addresses returned or rejected exported goods | Unconditional duty-free entry [P06, P07] |
| 5 Aug 2026 | Inventory-based cross-border export framework | Permits a registered exporter-on-record, or EOR, to procure from sellers-on-record against confirmed overseas orders and handle export and destination compliance | Universal availability or fair terms in every cluster [P10] |
| 1 Oct 2026 | New FEMA export/import regulations commence | Replaces the prior export regulation prospectively | A verified specific settlement timeline in this report [P11] |

The August EOR framework already provides for digital inventory segregation, seller payment, visibility into order status, benefit pass-through, returns and annual certification. The compact therefore cannot claim to create the EOR model. Its testable contribution is comparison among routes, practical uptake, non-exclusive seller choice and enforcement of commercial transparency [P10].

ECEHs, Districts as Export Hubs, Dak Ghar Niryat Kendras and Trade Connect also predate this proposal. Their announced existence does not show operating capacity in each proposed pilot cluster [P09, P12, R4-S01].

## 5. Seller segments and product fit

The pilot should recruit by product and route suitability, then record whether participants are women-led, first-time exporters or home-based producers. This avoids assuming all artisans face the same cost structure. Six archetypes provide a useful comparison: light textiles, non-fragile crafts, bulky home décor, fragile décor, imitation jewellery and permitted leather accessories. Precious jewellery, wildlife-derived material, regulated products and restricted goods need separate legal checks.

| Product archetype | Primary economic pressure | Primary compliance check | Pilot learning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light textile | Platform fee and destination tax | Labelling and product classification | Whether low weight supports postal export |
| Metal craft | Weight and protective packing | Material and classification | Whether pooled collection offsets weight |
| Silk accessory | Value and seller documentation | Origin and product claims | Whether assisted filing helps weavers |
| Bulky décor | Volumetric freight | Dimensions and breakage handling | Where pooling stops helping |
| Imitation jewellery | Returns and product description | Classification and destination rules | Whether benefits outweigh complexity |
| Leather accessory | Product and material restrictions | Permitted material and destination standards | Whether route eligibility narrows choice |

These are analytical segments. No representative return rate, order volume or willingness-to-pay observation has been established for them.

## 6. Postal, courier and EOR journeys

India Post describes electronic PBE-3 filing for e-commerce exports through its Dak Ghar Niryat Kendra portal, with applicable IEC, GSTIN and AD-code documentation. PBE-4 serves a different non-e-commerce category. Courier declarations use authorised couriers and ECCS processes; specified benefit claims use their required customs processing route. The registered EOR exports in its own name and takes the responsibilities in its governing framework [P03, P08, P10].

| Step | Direct postal/courier seller | EOR route | Proposed desk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product and destination check | Seller remains responsible | EOR assumes prescribed export and destination duties | Explain route eligibility; record uncertainty |
| Order and price | Seller contracts with buyer/platform | EOR buys against confirmed overseas order | Show comparable net receipts and charges |
| Export filing | Route-specific authorised filing | EOR exports in its own name | Help assemble records; no legal waiver |
| Shipment | Postal or courier operator | EOR fulfilment chain | Compare available quotes and service levels |
| Return | Seller and agent document lawful treatment | EOR follows framework and contract | Link original shipment to outcome |
| Settlement | Seller and AD bank reconcile proceeds | EOR pays SOR on prescribed terms | Track status, query gaps and escalate |

One universal form or government portal is not assumed. Any reuse of seller information requires lawful consent and system-level feasibility review.

## 8. Platform access and bargaining

Platform access has three distinct hurdles: an eligible seller account, a listing that can reach a buyer, and an order whose net proceeds remain attractive after fees. Amazon Global Selling's US pricing shows $0.99 per item for an Individual plan or $39.99 per month for a Professional plan, with category-dependent referral and fulfilment charges. Its UAE pricing currently advertises a limited-time zero-subscription offer. These are examples of fee variation, not a national average or a commitment to this pilot [R2-01, R2-02].

The compact should publish provider charges, compare route quotations and prohibit exclusive access to enrolled sellers. Sellers should retain their own transaction records and have an account suspension and payment dispute path. Shared procurement concerns collection, packing, logistics and support services; sellers must set their own product prices. No provider has signed a rate or partnership for this proposal.

eBay International Shipping is not an Indian-seller precedent: its cited programme is restricted to US sellers and US-located items [R2-04]. Avoid importing foreign programme terms into the Indian business case.

## 9. Shipping economics

An official India Post calculator query for a 250 g Delhi-to-US shipment returned ₹638 for an eligible International Tracked Packet and ₹1,127 for an International Parcel when checked on 28 October 2026 [R2-03]. The first service is relevant only if the merchandise, destination, dimensions and booking conditions qualify. The calculator's lower printed-papers rate cannot price ordinary commercial merchandise. These quotes need a fresh check at deck freeze.

Compare like for like: origin, destination, product, weight, volumetric weight, insurance, tracking, taxes, delivery service and permitted contents. The compact's pooling benefit equals the unpooled service bill less pooled price, collection overhead, handling, inventory holding and delay cost. Without a provider quote, a pooled discount is an assumption. The model should show the order volume at which the net saving turns positive.

## 10. Returns: five decisions, not one blanket exemption

An Indian export returned by an overseas buyer can invoke the risk-based courier re-import pathway subject to evidence and neutralisation of export benefits. An uncleared import in an Indian courier terminal follows a separate return-to-origin process after more than fifteen days and other conditions. EOR-rejected inventory follows the EOR framework's prescribed re-export, seller return or disposal options [P06, P10].

The economic decision compares physical return, lawful overseas resale, refund without return, and loss or damage. Each outcome has a separate probability, reverse freight, refund, recoverable value and tax adjustment. A parcel should appear in only one terminal outcome. The seller and EOR contracts must identify who pays when a customer changes their mind and when a parcel is damaged.

## 11. Destination-market constraints

| Destination | Verified relevant rule | Decision implication |
|---|---|---|
| United States | Low-value goods generally lost the former $800 duty-free de minimis treatment from 29 Aug 2025 [R3-01] | Price current duties and entry fees by product and route |
| European Union | Temporary €3 duty applies from 1 Jul 2026 to relevant low-value consignments per distinct tariff category [R3-02] | Count categories; do not add an unfinalised handling fee |
| Great Britain | For qualifying goods at £135 or less, VAT responsibility differs between direct and online-marketplace sales [R3-04] | Distinguish seller and marketplace collection |
| UAE | VAT applies to imports, with payment method affected by recipient registration [R3-05] | Identify importer and payer before assigning cost |

Do not infer a numeric destination duty for a product without its classification, country detail and current tariff. IOSS is a VAT collection mechanism; it does not automatically remove the EU's distinct small-parcel duty [R3-03]. The comparison therefore includes a field for who bears each charge and who remits it.

## 12. Policy options

Five alternatives should be compared on seller economics (25%), integrity (25%), feasibility (20%), inclusion and bargaining (15%), and fiscal sustainability (15%). These weights are team judgement. Hard legal and authority checks precede scoring.

| Option | Likely strength | Binding concern to test |
|---|---|---|
| Existing schemes plus outreach | Low incremental cost | Does advice change shipment economics? |
| New government marketplace | Direct public control | Duplication, procurement and adoption burden |
| Broad freight subsidy | Immediate price relief | Fiscal cost, targeting and durability |
| Single-platform EOR partnership | Integrated service | Seller dependence and excluded product/platform routes |
| Cluster Export Compact | Choice, local help, measurement | Coordination cost and uncertain pooled savings |

Score only after comparable evidence and model outputs exist. An alternative that performs better under equal weights or reasonable weight shifts should replace the initial recommendation.

## 13. Cluster Export Compact operating model

A local desk begins with a product and destination eligibility check. It then presents the seller with comparable net proceeds for direct postal, courier and eligible EOR options. It supports authorised documentation, tracks shipment and payment status, and helps close returns or discrepancies. The desk must disclose providers' charges, not steer every seller toward one platform.

At launch the compact should require: written agency responsibilities, a standard seller consent record, a provider fee schedule, common service-level definitions, a complaints route, and records that link order, invoice, shipment, benefit, payment and return. System integration is conditional on technical and legal approval. The local desk cannot grant customs release.

## 14. Customs and revenue integrity

Use three proposed operational categories: routine eligible, document clarification and specialist review. These are workflow labels, not current customs risk categories. A traceable record should tie seller identity to order, declared product, value, quantity, weight, route, customs reference, benefit claim, payment and return. Customs officers keep examination and release authority.

Potential flags include duplicate invoice or benefit references, implausible value or weight, product restrictions, anomalous repeat returns and unsettled payments. A flag should lead to review, not an automatic sanction. Combine targeted checks with a separately sampled assurance stream. New exporters should not be treated as inherently suspect.

The integrity measures are confirmed non-compliance in an independently sampled shipment set, unclosed benefit reversals and unresolved payment records. A small pilot may be too weak to estimate rare misuse; report sample size and confidence interval.

## 15. Data, privacy and audit

The desk should request only information needed to advise and support an authorised route. Seller consent should name the recipient and purpose for each proposed data transfer. Access controls separate platform commercial information from public authority records. Preserve an audit trail of changes to declared value, product, beneficiary and return status.

A proposed score should never become a hidden decision right. The seller needs a correction and appeal channel, and government officers need a way to see the underlying evidence. Exact data retention periods require a legal review before system procurement.

## 18. Pilot operations and inclusion

The proposed design enrolls 1,200 sellers, 200 in each of six clusters. Each cluster has ten operational groups of twenty. Five groups receive early access and five receive later access, yielding 600 early and 600 comparison sellers. Both retain access to existing public services. Baseline data include prior exporting, product, route, gender, business form and seller experience.

Recruitment through district bodies and producer organisations should use local-language information, accessible times and an independent complaint route. Record women-led and first-generation participation rather than asserting a quota has been achieved. The comparison group receives the same eventual service if the pilot proceeds.

Months 1 and 2 cover legal checks, partners, procurement and baseline. Months 3 to 8 operate the service. Month 9 is independent evaluation. Months 10 to 12 allow conditional expansion, redesign or closure. The timetable is a proposal, not an announced government schedule.

## 19. Economics, budget and scenarios

The model's unit is seller cohort by product, destination and route. Delivered contribution is retained receipts less product, packaging, freight, platform/payment/FX charges, seller-borne destination costs and compliance time. Expected contribution sums mutually exclusive delivered, returned, overseas-resold, refund-without-return and unrecoverable outcomes. Fixed charges are deducted once monthly. These formulas are design choices; modelled values must be generated, labelled and checked before citation.

The reproducible model's representative lane is an **illustrative eligible 250 g textile accessory from Delhi to the United States by post**. The ₹638 postal quote is the only observed unit-cost input. Its product eligibility, dimensions, date and origin must be rechecked before anyone uses the estimate for a real exporter [R2-03, M T01]. All sale prices, product costs, fee percentages, return probabilities, pooled discounts, compliance minutes and pilot costs are team assumptions. The model tests whether a feasible combination would improve economics; it does not measure the current market.

| Scenario | Baseline expected contribution per dispatch | Proposed expected contribution | Difference | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Downside | −₹507.55 | −₹562.55 | −₹55.00 | Service fees deepen an already unviable lane |
| Base | ₹460.50 | ₹570.35 | +₹109.85 | Improvement is conditional on 15% freight saving, shorter admin time and one point lower returns |
| Upside | ₹1,074.37 | ₹1,275.00 | +₹200.63 | Stronger assumptions raise the result |

These are model outputs, rounded for display [M T02, T03, T10]. The downside is decision-relevant: the compact must decline to pool on a lane where the service fee and collection expense exceed benefits.

The base freight bill falls from ₹638 to ₹542.30 under an **assumed** 15% discount. The proposed ₹30 service charge and ₹22 collection expense yield ₹43.70 of net pooling saving per order. With no freight discount the same fees turn pooling into a ₹52 cost [M T04, T07]. The base compliance-time assumption falls from forty to twenty minutes at an assumed ₹120 per hour, cutting booked administrative cost from ₹80 to ₹40. The assumed return rate falls from 10% to 9%. None of those changes is an observed programme outcome [M T01].

Budget lines are local desk staff, translation/training, provider procurement, data handling, audit, evaluation and contingency. Government spending, seller economic gain, provider resources and tax transfers should be separate accounts. The case provides no actual pilot budget, return rate, pooled discount or shipment volume. Downside, base and upside scenarios must state those assumptions explicitly. A negative result narrows the cohort; it must not be hidden.

The illustrative public budget is ₹96 lakh: ₹36 lakh for local desks, ₹12 lakh for training, ₹24 lakh for technology support, ₹12 lakh for evaluation and ₹12 lakh contingency [M T08]. It is an assumed design budget, not a sanctioned government allocation. The base adoption path assumes 360 active early-access sellers by month nine and 234 meeting the model's retention input, producing a calculated public cost of about ₹41,026 per retained early-access seller. The input retention definition still needs operational measurement and the denominator excludes later-access sellers. Thus the figure is a planning stress test, not an impact estimate [M T09].

## 20. Evaluation and scale gates

The three proposed operating targets are 20% lower controllable service cost, 50% less seller administrative time and 30% faster return resolution. They are targets, not forecasts. Controllable cost excludes product cost, taxes and unrelated expenses. Use median and 90th-percentile customs time, first shipment, ninety-day repeat exporting, net contribution per dispatched order, grievances, payment exceptions and integrity measures as supporting indicators.

Estimate changes against the later-access comparison groups where allocation and sample quality permit. A statistical-power calculation requires observed baseline variance and group correlation. If the integrity sample cannot rule out a material deterioration, defer scaling and continue measurement. Scale only when economic improvement persists after fees and public subsidy is identified separately.

## 21. Risks and reversibility

| Risk | Trigger | Response | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Provider capture | One operator receives most enrolled orders without a price advantage | Open comparable procurement and publish route shares | DPIIT/local operator |
| Weak adoption | Few sellers complete a first shipment | Diagnose by product, route and documentation step | District desk |
| No freight saving | Collection overhead exceeds discount | Stop pooling on that lane | Logistics lead |
| Return losses | Recovery costs exceed expected value | Compare lawful recovery options at order level | Seller/EOR |
| Customs anomaly | Higher confirmed misuse or missing records | Pause affected route and audit | CBIC |
| Data leakage | Unauthorised seller or buyer information access | Restrict access, investigate and notify as required | Operator |
| Fiscal drift | Cost per retained exporter exceeds limit | Freeze expansion and renegotiate services | DPIIT |

The pilot remains reversible: discontinue a desk contract, retain statutory processes and preserve seller access to existing public routes.

## 22. Implementation and decisions

The committee's first authorisation should cover a time-limited pilot mandate, named agency contacts, a legal review, baseline study and open service procurement. The launch decision follows verified provider coverage, appropriate customs interfaces, current RBI procedure and local seller recruitment. The month-nine decision uses independent results and disclosed uncertainty. There is no reason to procure a national portal before this test.

## 23. Questions judges should ask

**What is new when EOR and returns reforms already exist?** The proposal measures practical uptake and creates seller-facing route choice, comparative commercial terms and public outcome accountability. It does not recreate those legal tools.

**Who is exporter of record?** For direct export, the seller or applicable direct-export entity remains responsible under its route. In the August 2026 registered EOR framework, the EOR procures against confirmed overseas orders and exports in its own name [P10].

**Does pooled shipping guarantee cheaper parcels?** No. Collection and coordination costs may exceed any discount. The model tests a lane-specific break-even.

**Do low-risk shipments bypass customs?** No. Customs retains release and examination authority; the compact's categories support preparation and escalation.

**Can a 1,200-seller trial prove tax integrity?** It can measure records and sample selected shipments. Rare fraud may remain statistically unmeasurable without larger samples or longer observation.

**Are the named clusters ready?** They are candidates with verified product relevance, not confirmed agreements.

## Appendix E. Operating decisions and analytical protocols

### E1. What the local desk must decide before a first order

An export desk creates value only if it changes a real decision. It cannot simply hand out a list of schemes. For each prospective order, the operator should prepare a short decision record with the product description, proposed classification, material, seller identity, buyer destination, selling price, packed weight, dimensions, expected service level and proposed return policy. The operator then compares the legally available routes. The route with the lowest posted freight rate may still be inferior if it lacks tracking, requires a costly destination clearance service or creates an unrecoverable return.

The desk should issue a route recommendation in four parts. First, it states which routes appear legally eligible and which need specialist verification. Second, it calculates the seller's expected net receipts under each available route. Third, it describes who becomes the exporter, who owns the shipment records and who is responsible for a refund. Fourth, it records the seller's informed choice. This record protects seller choice and makes later evaluation possible. It must not be presented as legal clearance or a customs decision.

| Decision field | Postal/direct | Courier/direct | Registered EOR | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exporter identity | Verify seller's applicable status | Verify seller's applicable status | Registered EOR exports in its own name | Determines legal and record duties |
| Product eligibility | Check service and export rules | Check service and export rules | Check EOR scope and contract | A cheaper quote cannot cure ineligibility |
| Filing route | Electronic postal bill where applicable | Authorised courier declaration | EOR's authorised workflow | Benefit and traceability requirements differ |
| Price to seller | Buyer receipt less direct fees | Buyer receipt less direct fees | EOR purchase price and pass-through | Compare net amounts, not headline sale price |
| Return owner | Seller and selected operator | Seller and selected operator | EOR contract and governing framework | Defines recovery and benefit neutralisation |
| Destination obligation | Identify seller, buyer and service roles | Identify seller, buyer and service roles | EOR holds prescribed responsibility | Prevents an unpriced duty or tax surprise |
| Evidence | Order, invoice, postage, customs and payment record | Order, invoice, courier, customs and payment record | EOR purchase, export, payment and benefit record | Needed for audit and dispute resolution |

If a product is outside a service's eligibility or is restricted, the desk should record that conclusion and stop the cost comparison for that route. It should not suggest that a seller split a consignment, alter a declared value or change a product description to fit a cheaper service. A referral to the competent regulator or an authorised specialist is the appropriate result when classification or material rules are uncertain [P06, P08, P10].

### E2. Worked route comparison protocol

The model's Delhi to United States textile accessory is a deliberately narrow illustration. The ₹638 postal quote is a public calculator result for a specified weight and route, not a live offer for every textile order [R2-03]. A real comparison should begin with the same origin and destination address, collection requirement, packed dimensions, actual weight, declared value and promised delivery window. If one quote excludes collection, insurance, remote-area charges or destination clearance, add those items before ranking it. If a route requires a platform fee or fulfilment charge, show the fee separately from the carrier charge.

Three price views should appear on the desk's worksheet. The **buyer price** states what the overseas customer pays, including disclosed delivery and tax charges. The **seller receipt** shows the amount reaching the seller after platform, payment, FX, EOR or service fees. The **economic contribution** subtracts product, packing, transport, administration and expected return losses. These views answer different questions. A buyer can see a competitive posted price while the seller earns little; a seller can receive cash promptly while the programme pays an excessive subsidy; a logistics discount can disappear after collection costs.

The comparison should present a range rather than a single apparently precise outcome where destination duty, actual weight or returns are uncertain. A narrow quote may be used in a test order, but it should not justify a national benefit claim. The case's broad category labels also conceal material variation: a small scarf and bulky rug can both be textiles while having entirely different freight economics. The pilot's product archetypes are a sampling plan, not a claim of uniform costs.

### E3. Return closure procedure

The return pathway should be selected before sale and revisited after the actual reason for return is known. At order creation, the seller or EOR needs a written rule for damaged goods, wrong goods, buyer remorse, failed delivery, customs refusal and unclaimed stock. When a return arises, the operator should link the customer claim to the original order, export declaration, benefit reference, payment and tracking event. The next decision is whether physical recovery is lawful and economically worthwhile.

| Outcome | Trigger to evaluate | Primary cost terms | Required closure evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Re-import to India | Recoverable goods and viable reverse route | Reverse freight, customs handling, repair, benefit adjustment | Original export, return reason, re-import record, adjustment record |
| Lawful overseas resale | Stock and market permit local disposition | Storage, local taxes, resale commission, discount | Inventory transfer, lawful sale, proceeds and tax treatment |
| Refund without return | Recovery costs exceed value | Refund, fees not refunded, lost product | Buyer refund, disposition decision, accounting closure |
| Unrecoverable loss | Damage, loss or restricted disposal | Product write-off and disposal | Carrier evidence, insurance claim or disposal record |

The returned-export process in the March 2026 CBIC circular is tied to conditions and revenue neutralisation [P06]. It must not be merged with return to origin for an uncleared import or with an EOR's reverse logistics obligations [P07, P10]. For evaluation, measure the median days from first return notification to final financial closure, not merely the day a parcel moves back across a border. A slow benefit reversal or unsettled refund is still an open return.

### E4. Public procurement and competition boundaries

The compact's commercial lever is buying services collectively on behalf of eligible sellers. The tender should specify comparable units: pickup per parcel, packing per product class, international lane, tracking, delivery attempt, exception handling, reverse freight and support response. Quote validity, surcharge rules and volume tiers should be disclosed. The operator should publish the seller-facing fee schedule and permit a seller to use another compliant route. This creates a testable commercial offer without appointing one platform as the owner of a cluster's demand.

Commercial independence matters on both sides. Sellers should not coordinate the final prices they charge buyers. Providers should not receive competitively sensitive seller or rival-provider data beyond what is needed to perform their contract. If a logistics provider operates the local desk, the desk's route comparison must be auditable by an independent manager. A provider's large market share in pilot orders is not itself proof of misconduct; the evaluation should determine whether that share followed price, service and seller choice. Legal advice should review the procurement and data arrangements before launch. The report does not assert a competition-law exemption.

Where a negotiated discount is unavailable, a good tender still yields information. It can show that low volume, remote collection or return exposure makes pooling unattractive. The programme can then concentrate on documentation assistance or product readiness. The model's no-discount scenario quantifies this boundary for its illustrative lane: ₹52 of service and collection expense per order makes pooling costly before other gains [M T07].

### E5. Controls by failure mode

The controls should be paired with evidence and an owner. A generic instruction to use risk-based checks is too broad for implementation. In particular, a local desk can improve the quality of declared information, but it cannot decide whether a parcel is released. An EOR can obtain commercial documents and fulfil statutory obligations under its framework, but it cannot validate its own export benefit claim without the applicable authority's process.

| Failure mode | Preventive record or check | Detection route | Decision owner | Seller correction path |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wrong product or material | Product description, composition and relevant supporting documents | Classification and restriction review | Competent customs or export authority | Supply corrected evidence or change route |
| Undervalued shipment | Order price, buyer receipt, commercial invoice and comparable transaction record | Value anomaly and sampled review | Customs | Explain discount or correct declaration |
| Duplicate benefit | Unique original shipment and claim reference | Duplicate-reference query | Benefit-processing authority | Reconcile or reverse claim |
| Unmatched payment | Order, invoice, settlement and AD-bank references | Ageing and exception report | Applicable bank and regulator | Provide settlement evidence |
| Unclosed return | Return reference linked to original export | Open-case ageing | Operator and relevant authority | Select lawful disposition and adjustment |
| Provider overcharge | Published quote and final invoice | Charge variance report | Procurement manager | Rebill and appeal |
| Data misuse | Consent, role-limited access and activity log | Access review and incident report | Data controller/operator | Correction and complaint channel |

The table is an operating proposal, not a summary of controls already deployed nationwide. Before connecting systems, each authority must approve the precise data elements, legal basis and decision rights. Where an interface does not exist, a manual reference record with access controls may be more credible than claiming seamless digital integration.

### E6. Measurement dictionary

The programme should preregister denominators before the first seller is enrolled. Otherwise a favourable figure can be produced by changing who counts as active. Three denominators must remain distinct: enrolled sellers, sellers who dispatch at least one eligible order, and sellers who export again after ninety days. Counting a seller who attended training as an exporter would overstate conversion. Counting only successful shipments in the cost measure would hide failed-dispatch expense.

| Measure | Numerator | Denominator or time window | Collection source | Main interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Activation | Sellers dispatching first eligible order | All enrolled sellers within 90 days | Desk and shipment record | Whether access becomes action |
| Retention | Sellers dispatching another eligible order | First shippers with 90-day observation | Shipment record | Repeat use, not training attendance |
| Cost per dispatch | Controllable fees and seller admin value | All dispatched eligible orders | Invoices and time log | Includes failed deliveries |
| Contribution | Receipts less stated costs and expected loss | All dispatched eligible orders | Seller ledger and outcome record | Seller economics after fees |
| Administrative time | Seller minutes on required tasks | Sampled shipments by route | Time diary and observation | Time saved for seller, not agency alone |
| Customs time | Release timestamp less submission timestamp | Eligible declarations by route | Official event log | Report median and 90th percentile |
| Return closure | Final financial closure less return notice | Returns notified in window | Case record | Includes refund and benefit closure |
| Reconciliation gap | Open settlement or benefit exception | Orders old enough to be due | Bank and authority records | Must use applicable legal timetable |
| Confirmed non-compliance | Cases substantiated after review | Independently sampled shipments | Audit file | Disclose confidence interval |
| Complaint resolution | Resolved within published standard | Complaints received | Grievance log | Check independent appeal |
| Public cost per retained seller | Attributable budget | Sellers meeting retention definition | Budget and shipment record | Not a social benefit measure |

All measures should be broken down by cluster, product archetype, route, first-time-exporter status and gender where collection is lawful and reliable. The evaluator should document missing data. A missing cost record cannot be treated as zero, and a return still open at observation cutoff should be censored or reported separately rather than called resolved.

### E7. Evaluation design and inference limits

The proposed early-access design has ten operating groups in each cluster. Allocating five groups to early access and five to later access allows operational delivery while retaining a comparison [Section 18]. Its inference depends on how groups form and on whether the allocation is respected. Groups may differ in product, prior export experience and access to a postal centre. If organisers choose high-performing groups for early access, the observed difference will be biased. The evaluator should create groups before allocation, record baseline characteristics and, where feasible, randomise within cluster and product strata.

The primary analysis should compare the change in a preregistered economic outcome between early and later groups over the same time window. A group-level analysis or standard errors adjusted for group clustering is needed because members of one group share training and local conditions. The total of 1,200 sellers does not mean 1,200 independent observations. Attrition matters: a seller who stops exporting may disappear from transaction data precisely because the service failed. Retention and zero-order months must remain in the outcome file.

The three target percentages are operating thresholds, not assumed effect sizes. The evaluator needs baseline variance and intragroup correlation to calculate a minimum detectable effect before making a causal performance claim. Customs integrity is a different statistical problem. Confirmed misuse may be rare, and a 1,200-seller cohort may be too small to rule out a meaningful deterioration. The programme should therefore use independently selected shipment samples, report the number examined, and state the interval compatible with the observed rate. A finding of no detected violation is not proof that violations are absent.

Spillovers should also be recorded. A later-access seller may learn from an early-access neighbour, receive a carrier's new price anyway, or move onto an EOR route that becomes available to everyone. These changes reduce a simple treatment contrast. They do not necessarily reduce public value, but they change what the comparison estimates. The evaluation should report both the assigned-group effect and actual service uptake, and explain the identification assumptions for each.

### E8. Scaling decision as a conditional contract

At month nine the committee should avoid a single pass or fail score. It should inspect four ledgers together. The seller ledger shows contribution after all seller-borne fees and expected returns. The public ledger shows programme expenditure and cost per retained exporter. The integrity ledger shows customs, benefit and payment exceptions, audit coverage and correction time. The delivery ledger shows staffing, service levels, complaints and whether participating providers honour their quotes. Each ledger answers a different question.

A scale decision requires positive seller economics in the product-route cohorts being expanded, a public cost that is affordable under the approved budget, no material integrity deterioration under the available evidence, and demonstrated provider capacity. If one cohort improves while bulky or fragile goods remain negative, scale only the viable cohort. If freight discounts do not materialise but administrative savings do, retain the assistance component and abandon pooling on that lane. If integrity evidence is inconclusive, extend observation or narrow the route. If provider terms are opaque, do not reward that provider with a larger exclusive mandate.

The model is a planning instrument for these decisions. It does not establish a causal effect, prove willingness to pay or measure a national export opportunity. The base result of +₹109.85 per dispatched order follows a particular bundle of assumptions, and the downside result of −₹55 shows the direction can reverse [M T10]. The central recommendation is consequently to test and condition expansion, not to claim that the model's base result has already occurred.

### E9. Evidence still required before an implementation order

The research has enough primary policy material to distinguish several enacted reforms from the proposed compact, but it does not establish that all six candidate clusters are launch-ready. A prelaunch dossier should include: the current full EOR notification and registration rules; current RBI primary operational instructions; a product-specific export and destination compliance check; written postal and courier quotes for each proposed lane; verified local desk premises and staffing; proposed data-sharing agreements; the tender and grievance terms; and an evaluation protocol signed by the responsible agencies. If an official source has changed since the 28 October 2026 research date, the new instrument takes precedence in the implementation dossier.

Seller interviews and transaction records are particularly important for calibrating the model. Obtain actual packing dimensions and freight bills, platform remittance statements, refund histories and time diaries. These records can replace assumed costs and probabilities. Until then, the report's calculated monetary improvements must be read as scenario outputs. The programme should publish the difference between the proposal approved, the service actually delivered and the outcomes observed. This separation is essential to honest public evaluation.

## Gaps

- The brief's A-R4 (Intellegens white paper) could not be located as a named, citable document on the company's own domain; only third-party vendor-profile paraphrases were found (I06).
- A-R5 and A-R7 and A-R11 and A-R12 and A-R13 could not be matched with confidence to a specific confirmed title, author list and DOI; search results either return a different-but-adjacent paper, a dead end, or a title mismatch against the brief's stated title (I07, I09, I13, I14, I15).
- No self-driving lab paper specific to pigmented architectural/decorative paint formulation (as opposed to thin films, electrochromics, or wettability coatings) was found; this is a real gap in the literature as searched, not only a search failure, and should be stated plainly in the research document rather than papered over (I26).
- Exact percentage accuracy gains from physics-informed neural networks over non-physics-informed baselines, on a matched test set, were not found as a single clean comparable number; the two closest results found (I36, I37) report absolute metrics (F1, R-squared, epoch counts) rather than a baseline-versus-PINN percentage delta, and I36/I37's exact source titles could not be confirmed, so they are marked UNVERIFIABLE.
- The co-author LinkedIn claim of "2x-3x more top candidates" for the Krokidas et al. Bayesian-optimisation paper (I33) was not cross-checked against the paper's own results section or supplementary tables in this pass; treat it as a secondary-source figure until the primary paper's results are read directly.

## Conflicts

- A-R3 and A-R8 in the brief appear, on the evidence of their identical ACS Polymers Au volume/issue/page string (3/3/239) and matching DOI suffix (acspolymersau.2c00053), to be the same Martin and Audus (2023) paper cited twice under two separate reference IDs, rather than two distinct sources (I05, I10).
- NIST's own bibliographic listing for the Martin and Audus paper gives the title as "Advances and Trends in Machine Learning and Artificial Intelligence: A Polymer Perspective," while the published ACS Polymers Au and PMC record gives "Emerging Trends in Machine Learning: A Polymer Perspective." Both titles point to the same DOI (10.1021/acspolymersau.2c00053); the research document should use the published-record title ("Emerging Trends...") and may note the NIST listing's variant title in a footnote (I05).
- Two different, independently published Bayesian-optimisation-of-nanoporous-materials results exist in the literature with different numbers: the 2025 Krokidas et al. Digital Discovery paper (ranking-metric framing, no clean percentage found in the primary text) and a 2021 ChemRxiv preprint reporting "30% of the top 100 ranked COFs after ~120 evaluations." These are different studies and should not be conflated as one result (I32, I34).

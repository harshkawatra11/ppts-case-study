## Conflicts

1. IARC Monograph Volume 93 (2010) classifies titanium dioxide as Group 2B, possibly carcinogenic to humans (R-E38, R-E39), while ACGIH classifies it as A4, not classifiable as a human carcinogen (R-E40). Both classifications are current and both should be stated side by side in the research document; neither supersedes the other.
2. NIOSH's 2011 recommended exposure limits split TiO2 into fine (2.4 mg/m3) and ultrafine (0.3 mg/m3) TWA values (R-E36), which sit well below OSHA's enforceable PEL of 15 mg/m3 total dust (R-E34) and below ACGIH's 2021 finescale/nanoscale respirable values of 2.5 and 0.2 mg/m3 (R-E35). All three are legitimate, differently-scoped numbers (recommended vs enforceable, total dust vs respirable fraction); the research document should state which one it is citing and why.
3. Acrylic/styrene-acrylic binder footprint: the model's A26 assumption is 2.5 kg CO2e per kg of binder solids. The only binder footprint figure found (R-E50, ICM via Climatiq) is 1.76 kg CO2e per kg of a 34-percent-solids liquid product, which converts arithmetically to about 5.2 kg CO2e per kg of solids (R-E51). The two numbers differ by roughly a factor of two, but the dataset's system boundary (cradle-to-shelf vs cradle-to-gate) and GWP methodology vintage (AR4 vs whatever the model assumption used) are not confirmed to match, so this is recorded as a conflict for the main agent to resolve under the update protocol (PLAN.md section 5.3), not acted on here.
4. A PCC label is applied inconsistently in the marketplace: some sellers call ground or natural calcite "PCC" (R-E15), while true precipitated calcium carbonate is a chemically distinct, carbonation-route product (R-E07 to R-E09). Any price or particle-size figure sourced from a marketplace listing should be read with this label confusion in mind.
